# Head Injury — First Aid

## WHAT THIS IS
A child or person has hit their head — from a fall, a blow, a road accident, or other impact. Most childhood head bumps are minor. However, some head injuries are serious and can cause bleeding inside the skull that develops slowly — meaning the person may seem fine at first, then deteriorate hours later. Knowing the warning signs is essential.

---

## IMMEDIATE ACTION

1. **Keep the person still and calm** — do not move them unnecessarily, especially if the fall or impact was significant and there is any chance of a neck injury.

2. **If the person is unconscious but breathing** — do not move them unless they are in immediate danger (fire, traffic). Place them in the recovery position: roll onto the side, stabilise the head and neck together as a unit, support with knees bent. Keep the airway open.

3. **If the person is not breathing** — begin CPR if you know how and call for help immediately.

4. **Control bleeding from the head** — apply gentle, firm pressure with a clean cloth. Head wounds bleed heavily even when minor, so do not be alarmed by the amount of blood from a small cut. But do apply pressure.

5. **Do not remove any object embedded in the skull** — if something is stuck in the head, stabilise it in place with cloth around it and go directly to hospital. Never pull it out.

6. **Apply a cold compress to a bump (swelling)** — a clean cloth soaked in cool water reduces swelling and pain. This is only for conscious, alert people with minor bumps.

7. **Observe closely for the next 24 hours** — even if the person seems fine, watch for the warning signs listed below.

8. **Keep the person awake and talking for the first 2 hours** — brief checking is appropriate. You do not need to prevent sleep entirely, but check the person every 1–2 hours during sleep for the first night.

---

## WHAT NOT TO DO

- **Do NOT give aspirin or ibuprofen for head injury pain** — these thin the blood and can worsen internal bleeding. Use paracetamol only.
- **Do NOT leave the person alone** — for the first 24 hours, someone must be observing them.
- **Do NOT let the person "sleep it off" without monitoring** — a person who loses consciousness from a head injury and is left to sleep without regular checks may deteriorate and die without anyone noticing.
- **Do NOT shake or slap a person who appears unconscious** — if they are not responding, do not shake them. Check for breathing and go to hospital.
- **Do NOT give food or drink** if the person is drowsy, confused, or vomiting — they may need surgery and must have an empty stomach.
- **Do NOT assume a helmet means no serious injury** — helmets reduce but do not eliminate head injury risk.

---

## WHEN TO GO TO HOSPITAL

Go to hospital **immediately** — do not wait — if after a head injury the person:

🔴 Lost consciousness at any point — even briefly  
🔴 Is confused, does not know where they are, or cannot answer simple questions  
🔴 Has a seizure (convulsion) after the injury  
🔴 Has vomited more than once  
🔴 Has a severe headache that is getting worse, not better  
🔴 Has one pupil larger than the other  
🔴 Has weakness, numbness, or cannot move an arm or leg normally  
🔴 Has blood or clear fluid coming from the ear or nose  
🔴 Is a baby or young child who will not stop crying after a head injury  
🔴 Was in a road accident or high-impact fall (from height, significant force)  
🔴 Is taking any blood-thinning medication  

**Go the same day** if:
- You are unsure how hard the impact was
- The child is under 2 years — young children's skulls are more vulnerable and symptoms can be harder to assess

**A person who was fine immediately after a head injury but develops any of the above signs hours later** — go to hospital immediately. This pattern (appearing fine then deteriorating) is the hallmark of serious intracranial bleeding.

---

## SAFETY NET

Most childhood head bumps are minor and the child recovers quickly. But you cannot know from the outside whether there is bleeding inside the skull.

The rule is simple: **if in doubt, go out** — to hospital.

Any child who loses consciousness after a head injury, even for a few seconds, must be assessed by a health worker. This is not an overreaction. It is the correct response.
