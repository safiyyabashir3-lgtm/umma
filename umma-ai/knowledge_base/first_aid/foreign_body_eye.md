# Foreign Body in the Eye — First Aid

## WHAT THIS IS
Something has entered the eye — dust, sand, an insect, a small piece of metal, wood, or other material. The eye is red, painful, watering heavily, and the person cannot stop blinking or rubbing it. This is very common in children.

---

## IMMEDIATE ACTION

1. **Tell the person — do not rub the eye** — rubbing pushes the object deeper and can scratch the eye surface.

2. **Wash your hands** before touching the eye area.

3. **Look at the eye in good light** — gently pull down the lower eyelid and look for the object. Ask the person to look up, down, and sideways so you can see all areas.

4. **If you can clearly see the object sitting on the white of the eye or inside the lower eyelid — flush the eye:**
   - Tilt the head so the affected eye is facing down
   - Pour clean water gently across the eye from the inner corner (near nose) outward
   - Use a clean cup, a syringe without the needle, or a water bottle with a small stream
   - Flush continuously for 10–15 minutes
   - Let the water carry the object out — do not try to wipe or pick it

5. **After flushing, check if the object is gone** — if the person still feels discomfort, flush again.

6. **If the eye is still uncomfortable after flushing** — cover loosely with a clean cloth (do not press) and go to a clinic or eye unit.

---

## WHAT NOT TO DO

- **Do NOT rub the eye** — at any point. This is the most important rule.
- **Do NOT try to remove an object that is stuck to the eyeball** — this requires a health worker with proper equipment.
- **Do NOT use dirty water** — infection in the eye is serious. Use the cleanest water available.
- **Do NOT use cotton wool, tissue, or dry cloth to wipe the eye** — fibres can stick and cause more damage. Use flowing water only.
- **Do NOT press on the eye** — if there is a large object penetrating the eye (a stick, metal fragment that appears lodged), do not press or pull it. Cover the eye loosely and go directly to hospital.
- **Do NOT put traditional substances in the eye** — no breast milk, no herbs, no eye drops that are not prescribed. These introduce infection.

---

## WHEN TO GO TO HOSPITAL

Go to a clinic or eye unit **the same day** if:
- Flushing did not remove the object and the eye still feels something is there
- The object appears stuck on the eyeball — do not try to remove it
- The person cannot open the eye due to pain or light sensitivity
- There is visible blood in the eye
- Vision seems blurry or changed after the object entered
- The eye becomes increasingly red and swollen after flushing

Go **immediately as an emergency** if:
- There is a large object visibly stuck in or through the eye (pen, stick, metal rod) — do not remove it, stabilise it and get to hospital now
- The eye was exposed to a chemical or acid — flush immediately with large amounts of water for 20 minutes without stopping, then go to hospital

---

## SAFETY NET

If there is any doubt about whether the object has been fully removed, go to a clinic for a proper examination. The eye surface (cornea) can be scratched even after the object is gone, and a scratched cornea needs treatment to prevent infection.

An eye that continues to feel scratchy, watery, or painful 30 minutes after flushing has not resolved on its own — seek care.
