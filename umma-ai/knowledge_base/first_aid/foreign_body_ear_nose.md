# Foreign Body in Ear or Nose — First Aid

## WHAT THIS IS
A child has put an object into their ear or nose — or an insect has entered the ear. Common objects include beads, seeds, small stones, pieces of food, foam, button batteries, or small toy parts. This is extremely common in young children aged 1–5 years. The child may or may not tell you it happened.

**Button batteries are a medical emergency** — a small round battery lodged in the nose or ear destroys tissue within hours. If you suspect a button battery, go to hospital immediately — do not attempt any home removal.

---

## IMMEDIATE ACTION

**For an object in the EAR:**

1. **Keep the child calm** — do not frighten them or they will push the object deeper by moving suddenly.

2. **Look in the ear in good light** — if you can clearly see the object near the entrance of the ear canal, and it is soft (foam, cotton) and easy to grasp, you may gently try to remove it with two fingers. Do not push it further in.

3. **If it is an insect in the ear:**
   - Tilt the ear upward toward the light — insects often move toward light and may exit
   - If the insect does not exit within 1–2 minutes, pour a small amount of clean olive oil, cooking oil, or clean water into the ear to immobilise it, then go to a clinic for removal

4. **If you cannot see the object clearly, or it is hard, or attempts to remove it are failing** — stop. Cover the ear loosely and go to a clinic.

---

**For an object in the NOSE:**

1. **Keep the child calm and sitting upright.**

2. **Ask the child to breathe through their mouth** — not to sniff hard, as this can pull the object deeper.

3. **The "mother's kiss" technique (for young children):**
   - Gently close the unaffected nostril with one finger
   - Cover the child's mouth with your mouth completely
   - Give one gentle puff — like blowing into a balloon
   - The positive pressure can push the object out of the nose
   - Repeat up to 3 times if the first attempt does not work
   - Do not use excessive force

4. **If the object does not come out after 2–3 attempts** — stop and go to a clinic.

---

## WHAT NOT TO DO

- **Do NOT use cotton buds, tweezers, pins, or instruments** to probe the ear or nose — these push objects deeper and can damage the ear canal, eardrum, or nasal tissue.
- **Do NOT attempt repeated forceful removal** — one or two gentle attempts only. If it does not come out easily, stop.
- **Do NOT give the child anything to sniff or blow through the blocked nostril** — no pepper, snuff, or substances intended to make the child sneeze. These can cause the object to be inhaled into the airway, which is far more dangerous.
- **Do NOT put oil in the ear if you suspect a button battery** — oil does not neutralise battery damage and delays reaching hospital.
- **Do NOT leave a suspected button battery situation** — this is the one scenario where home observation is never appropriate.

---

## WHEN TO GO TO HOSPITAL

Go to a clinic or hospital **today** for ALL foreign body in ear or nose situations where:
- You cannot see or cannot easily remove the object
- The child is in pain or distress
- There is discharge (fluid, blood, or pus) from the ear or nose
- The child has difficulty breathing through the nose
- The child has a foul smell coming from the nose — this means an object has been there for some time and infection has started
- The object is a seed (seeds absorb moisture and swell, making later removal much harder)
- You are not certain whether an object is there

Go **immediately as an emergency** if:
- You suspect a **button battery** — any flat round small battery — in the ear or nose
- The child is having difficulty breathing or is distressed
- The child is very young (under 12 months)
- There is significant bleeding

---

## SAFETY NET

Children often do not tell caregivers they have put something in their ear or nose. Signs to watch for:
- Unexplained runny nose on one side only
- Foul smell from one nostril
- Child pulling at one ear
- Hearing loss in one ear
- Unexplained ear discharge

If any of these signs appear, take your child to a clinic and mention the possibility of a foreign body. Clinics in Kano that can assist include PHC centres (for initial assessment) and ENT units at AKTH and Murtala Muhammad Specialist Hospital for complex removal.
