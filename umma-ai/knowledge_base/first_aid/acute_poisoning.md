# Acute Poisoning — First Aid

## WHAT THIS IS
A child or person has swallowed, inhaled, or touched a poisonous substance. This includes household chemicals (kerosene, bleach, caustic soda, insecticide), medications taken in excess, pesticides, or unknown substances. Time matters — act immediately.

---

## IMMEDIATE ACTION

1. **Stay calm and keep the person calm** — do not shout or panic.

2. **Remove the person from danger** — if they inhaled fumes or touched a chemical, move them to fresh air immediately. If the poison is on skin or eyes, remove contaminated clothing and rinse the area with large amounts of clean water.

3. **Do NOT make the person vomit** — unless a health worker specifically instructs you to. Vomiting can cause more damage, especially with caustic substances (bleach, caustic soda, battery acid) and kerosene.

4. **If the person is unconscious but breathing** — place them on their side (recovery position): roll them onto their left side, bend the top knee forward to keep them stable, tilt the head back slightly to keep the airway open. Stay with them.

5. **If the person is not breathing** — begin CPR if you know how and get help immediately.

6. **Collect evidence** — pick up the container, packet, or bottle of whatever was swallowed. Bring it with you to hospital. If you do not know what was taken, collect a sample of any vomit in a container.

7. **Call for help and go to hospital immediately** — do not wait to see if they improve.

---

## WHAT NOT TO DO

- **Do NOT give milk, oil, or water to drink** — unless specifically advised by a health worker for that specific substance. For many poisons this does nothing or causes harm.
- **Do NOT give traditional remedies** — herbs, concoctions, or black soap do not neutralise poison and delay reaching hospital.
- **Do NOT make the person vomit** — this is the single most common dangerous mistake. Kerosene and caustic substances cause severe damage to the throat and lungs if vomited.
- **Do NOT leave the person alone** — their condition can deteriorate suddenly.
- **Do NOT wait and watch at home** — even if the person seems fine now, many poisons cause delayed effects. Hospital assessment is always required.

---

## WHEN TO GO TO HOSPITAL

Go immediately — do not wait — for ALL poisoning cases. There is no safe "wait and see" with poisoning.

Go as an **emergency** (do not delay for anything) if the person:
- Is unconscious or very drowsy
- Is having fits or convulsions
- Is having difficulty breathing
- Is vomiting uncontrollably
- Has burns around the mouth or lips (sign of caustic substance)
- Is very agitated, confused, or seeing things
- Has a very slow or very fast heartbeat

Tell the hospital staff immediately: what was taken, how much, and approximately when.

---

## SAFETY NET

Bring the container or packet of the substance to hospital — even an empty one. This single action can save the person's life by allowing health workers to give the correct treatment immediately.

If you are unsure whether something is a poisoning emergency — go to hospital. It is always better to be checked and told everything is fine.
