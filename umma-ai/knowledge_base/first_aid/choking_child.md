# Choking in a Child — First Aid

## WHAT THIS IS
A child has an object — food, a small toy, a coin, a seed, a bone — stuck in their airway and cannot breathe properly. Choking can kill a child within minutes if not treated immediately. This is a true emergency requiring immediate physical action — not a situation to observe and wait.

Choking looks different from gagging. A choking child cannot cough effectively, cannot cry or speak normally, may be turning blue around the lips, and is in obvious distress. They may clutch their throat.

---

## IMMEDIATE ACTION

**For a BABY under 1 year:**

1. **Hold the baby face-down along your forearm** — support the head, keep it lower than the chest.

2. **Give 5 firm back blows** — use the heel of your hand between the baby's shoulder blades. Firm and deliberate — not gentle taps.

3. **Turn the baby face-up on your other arm** — support the head.

4. **Give 5 chest thrusts** — place 2 fingers on the centre of the chest, just below the nipple line. Push down firmly and sharply, about one-third of the chest depth. One thrust per second.

5. **Look in the mouth** — if you can clearly SEE an object, remove it with one finger. Do not do blind finger sweeps — you may push the object deeper.

6. **Repeat — 5 back blows, 5 chest thrusts** — continue until the object comes out, the baby starts breathing and crying, or the baby becomes unconscious.

7. **If the baby becomes unconscious** — start infant CPR and call for help immediately.

---

**For a CHILD 1 year and older:**

1. **Kneel or stand behind the child** — get to their level.

2. **Give 5 firm back blows** — lean the child forward, support their chest with one hand, use the heel of your other hand to give 5 firm blows between the shoulder blades.

3. **Give 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich manoeuvre):**
   - Stand or kneel behind the child
   - Make a fist with one hand, place it just above the navel and well below the breastbone — thumb side against the body
   - Cover with your other hand
   - Pull sharply inward and upward — 5 times
   - Each thrust should be firm and distinct

4. **Look in the mouth** — if you can clearly SEE the object, remove it. Do not do blind finger sweeps.

5. **Repeat — 5 back blows, 5 abdominal thrusts** — continue until the object is expelled, the child can breathe and cough effectively, or the child loses consciousness.

6. **If the child becomes unconscious** — lay them down, start CPR, call for help immediately.

---

**If you are ALONE with a choking child:**
Perform back blows and abdominal thrusts for 1 minute before stopping to call for help. Do not stop the intervention to shout for help if you can maintain the effort — the object may come out. Shout loudly for anyone nearby while continuing.

---

## WHAT NOT TO DO

- **Do NOT do blind finger sweeps** — if you cannot see the object, putting your finger in the child's mouth can push it deeper into the airway.
- **Do NOT hold the child upside down and shake them** — this does not work and can cause injury.
- **Do NOT slap the back while the child is upright** — back blows must be given with the child leaning forward so gravity assists. Slapping an upright child drives the object deeper.
- **Do NOT give water or food** — nothing by mouth.
- **Do NOT wait and watch** — a choking child can lose consciousness in minutes. Act immediately.
- **Do NOT use abdominal thrusts on a baby under 1 year** — use chest thrusts instead (as described above).

---

## WHEN TO GO TO HOSPITAL

Go to hospital **immediately** after any choking episode where:
- You had to perform back blows or abdominal thrusts — even if the object came out and the child seems fine now
- The child is still coughing persistently after the object appeared to come out — part of it may remain
- The child has a persistent hoarse voice, wheeze, or difficulty breathing after the episode
- The child swallowed a coin, button battery, or sharp object — these need X-ray assessment
- The child lost consciousness at any point

**Even if the child recovers completely and seems fine** — hospital assessment after a significant choking episode is strongly recommended. Fragments can remain in the airway.

---

## SAFETY NET

Prevention is the best protection. Common choking hazards for children under 5 in Nigerian homes: whole groundnuts, hard sweets, coins, small toy parts, seeds, raw carrots, grapes, fish bones. Keep these away from young children.

If a child is coughing vigorously — do not intervene physically. A strong, effective cough is the body clearing the airway. Encourage the cough. Only intervene with back blows and thrusts if the cough becomes weak and ineffective and the child cannot breathe.
