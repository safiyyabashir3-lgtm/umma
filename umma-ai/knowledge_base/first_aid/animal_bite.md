# Animal Bite — First Aid

## WHAT THIS IS
A person — most commonly a child — has been bitten by an animal. The most urgent concern with any animal bite in Nigeria is **rabies**, which is fatal once symptoms appear. Dog bites are the most common source. Cat, monkey, and bat bites also carry rabies risk. Snake bites are addressed separately.

Every animal bite that breaks the skin must be treated as a potential rabies exposure until assessed by a health worker.

---

## IMMEDIATE ACTION

1. **Wash the wound immediately and thoroughly** — this is the single most important action and must happen right now, before anything else.
   - Wash under running water for a **minimum of 15 minutes**
   - Use soap — any soap — and wash the wound thoroughly, including inside the wound if it is deep
   - If no running water: pour water from a cup or bottle continuously over the wound while scrubbing with soap for 15 minutes
   - The mechanical washing action physically removes rabies virus from the wound

2. **After washing — apply an antiseptic if available:**
   - Povidone-iodine (Betadine) is the best option
   - If not available: spirit (surgical spirit / alcohol) applied to the wound
   - Do not close the wound tightly after application

3. **Do not close the wound** — animal bites should not be sutured (stitched closed) immediately, as closing traps bacteria and infection. A health worker will assess this.

4. **Control bleeding if present:**
   - Apply gentle pressure with a clean cloth
   - Elevate the affected limb if possible

5. **Go to a health facility today** — do not wait. Even if the wound looks minor, rabies post-exposure treatment (PEP) must be started as soon as possible after exposure.

---

## WHAT NOT TO DO

- **Do NOT skip the wound washing** — this is not optional. 15 minutes of soap and water washing is the most effective rabies prevention action available right now. Do it before transport, before anything else.
- **Do NOT kill the animal and discard it** — if the biting animal can be safely contained, keep it under observation for 10 days. If the animal remains healthy, rabies transmission is less likely. If it dies or becomes ill, report to health authorities immediately.
- **Do NOT apply herbs, oil, or traditional substances to the wound** — these do not prevent rabies and introduce infection.
- **Do NOT wait to see if the child develops symptoms** — rabies symptoms appear weeks to months after the bite, by which time treatment cannot save the person's life. PEP must start within hours to days of the bite, not after symptoms appear.
- **Do NOT assume a vaccinated dog cannot transmit rabies** — dog vaccination coverage in many parts of Kano is not reliable. All dog bites require assessment.
- **Do NOT dismiss a bat scratch or small wound** — even minor bat contact can transmit rabies.

---

## WHEN TO GO TO HOSPITAL

Go to a health facility **today — as soon as wound washing is complete** for every animal bite that has broken the skin.

Go **immediately as an emergency** if:
- The bite is on the face, head, neck, or hands — these areas are higher risk for rabies transmission due to proximity to the nervous system
- The wound is deep, large, or bleeding heavily
- The child is very young (under 2 years)
- The animal was behaving strangely — unprovoked attack, aggressive, disoriented, wandering, foaming at mouth
- The bite was from a bat, monkey, or unknown animal

**At the hospital ask for:**
- Wound assessment and cleaning
- **Rabies post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP)** — a series of vaccine injections that prevent rabies developing. This is available at most general hospitals and teaching hospitals in Kano. PEP is effective when started early. It is not effective after symptoms appear.
- **Tetanus toxoid** — animal bites carry tetanus risk
- Assessment for other infection and antibiotics if needed

**Rabies PEP in Kano:**
PEP is available at AKTH and Murtala Muhammad Specialist Hospital. Ask your nearest PHC for the current referral pathway for rabies exposure in your LGA.

---

## SAFETY NET

Rabies is 100% preventable before symptoms appear. It is 100% fatal after symptoms appear. The window for treatment is days to weeks after the bite — not months.

Wound washing + PEP started quickly = life saved.

No wound washing + delayed presentation = risk of death.

If you are unsure whether the bite broke the skin, go to a health facility and let them assess. It is never wrong to seek care after an animal bite.
