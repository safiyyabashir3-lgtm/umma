# Burns in a Child — First Aid

## WHAT THIS IS
A child has been burned by hot water, hot food, fire, flame, a hot surface, a chemical, or electricity. Burns are one of the most common childhood injuries in Nigeria, frequently caused by hot cooking pots, boiling water, and open fires. The first minutes of treatment make a significant difference to outcome.

---

## IMMEDIATE ACTION

1. **Remove the child from the source of the burn immediately** — pull them away from flame, hot water, or the hot object. If clothing is on fire: stop, drop, roll — or smother flames with a cloth.

2. **Remove clothing and jewellery from the burned area immediately** — do this gently. If clothing is stuck to the skin, do not pull it off — cut around it and leave the stuck part. Remove necklaces, bangles, and watches near the burn before swelling starts.

3. **Cool the burn with cool running water — immediately and for 20 minutes**
   - Use cool (not cold, not iced) running water
   - Hold the burned area under a gentle stream of water for a full 20 minutes — set a timer
   - This is the single most important first aid action for burns. It stops the burn continuing into deeper tissue.
   - If running water is not available, pour clean cool water continuously over the burn for 20 minutes

4. **While cooling — keep the rest of the child warm** — cooling a large burn area can lower the child's body temperature dangerously. Wrap unburned areas in a dry cloth or blanket while cooling the burn.

5. **After cooling — cover loosely** — cover the burned area with a clean cloth, a clean plastic bag, or cling film laid flat (not wrapped tightly). This protects from infection and reduces pain.

6. **Give the child something to drink** — if the child is conscious and can swallow, give sips of clean water or ORS (oral rehydration solution) to replace fluid lost through burns.

7. **Go to hospital** — all significant burns in children require hospital assessment.

---

## WHAT NOT TO DO

- **Do NOT use toothpaste, shea butter, egg white, oil, or any cream** — these are the most common harmful practices in Nigeria. They trap heat, cause infection, and make proper wound assessment and treatment much harder. They can cause the burn to become severely infected.
- **Do NOT use ice or very cold water** — ice causes additional tissue damage (cold injury) and causes dangerous drop in body temperature in children.
- **Do NOT break blisters** — blisters are the body's natural protective covering. Breaking them opens the wound to infection.
- **Do NOT wrap tightly** — tight bandaging on a burn causes circulation problems as the area swells.
- **Do NOT give aspirin to a child** — for pain relief, give paracetamol syrup at the correct dose for the child's weight.

---

## WHEN TO GO TO HOSPITAL

Go to hospital **immediately** for:
- Any burn on the face, hands, feet, genitals, or joints
- Any burn that goes all the way around a limb (circumferential burn)
- Any burn larger than the child's palm
- Any burn where the skin looks white, brown, or black — or where there is no pain (deep burn)
- Burns caused by electricity — always require hospital assessment even if they look small; electrical burns are always deeper than they appear
- Burns caused by chemicals — go after flushing with water
- Any burn in a child under 2 years
- Burns with inhalation of smoke or fire — if the child has a hoarse voice, is coughing, or was in an enclosed fire — this is a respiratory emergency
- If you are unsure how serious the burn is

Go to the nearest hospital emergency unit. In Kano: AKTH, Murtala Muhammad Specialist Hospital, or your nearest General Hospital.

---

## SAFETY NET

Cool water for 20 minutes is the treatment. Everything else — toothpaste, butter, oil — causes harm.

If in doubt about the severity of a burn, go to hospital. Burns in children can look misleadingly small on the surface while being deeper than they appear. A hospital assessment takes minutes and can prevent serious complications.
