# Drowning — First Aid

## WHAT THIS IS
A child or person has been submerged in water — a river, well, pond, bucket, or any water source — and has stopped breathing or is barely conscious. Drowning can occur in very small amounts of water. It is silent — drowning children rarely shout or splash. It can happen in seconds.

This is a cardiac and respiratory emergency. Speed of action determines survival and brain outcome.

---

## IMMEDIATE ACTION

1. **Get the person out of the water safely**
   - Do not jump in if you cannot swim — a drowning person can pull a rescuer under
   - Reach from the bank using a hand, cloth, stick, rope, or any long object
   - Throw something that floats if they are conscious
   - Only enter the water yourself if you are a confident swimmer and there is no other option

2. **As soon as the person is out of the water — check for response**
   - Call their name, tap their shoulder
   - If they are coughing, crying, or breathing — they are conscious — move to step 6
   - If they are not responding — begin immediately

3. **If NOT breathing — start rescue breaths immediately**
   - Tilt the head back gently, lift the chin to open the airway
   - Pinch the nose closed
   - Give 5 rescue breaths — seal your mouth over theirs completely and breathe in gently, enough to see the chest rise
   - For infants: cover both mouth and nose with your mouth

4. **Check for pulse or signs of circulation**
   - If no pulse — start CPR immediately
   - **CPR: 30 chest compressions followed by 2 rescue breaths, repeated continuously**
   - Chest compressions: place the heel of your hand on the centre of the chest, press down firmly and fast — about 100–120 times per minute — one-third depth of the chest
   - For infants: use 2 fingers on the centre of the chest
   - Do not stop for more than 10 seconds

5. **Continue CPR without stopping until:**
   - The person starts breathing on their own
   - Emergency help arrives and takes over
   - You are physically unable to continue

6. **If the person is breathing — recovery position:**
   - Roll them onto their side
   - Keep the head tilted slightly back to maintain the airway
   - Keep them warm — wet clothing loses heat rapidly; remove wet clothes and cover with a dry cloth or blanket
   - Do not leave them alone

7. **Call for help and go to hospital immediately** — even if the person has recovered and appears well.

---

## WHAT NOT TO DO

- **Do NOT hold the person upside down or over your shoulder to drain water** — this wastes critical time and does not work. The lungs cannot be emptied this way. Start rescue breaths immediately.
- **Do NOT wait for the person to "come round" on their own** — if they are not breathing, begin rescue breaths now. Every second without oxygen causes brain damage.
- **Do NOT give up quickly** — people — especially children — can survive drowning in cold water after extended periods. Continue CPR until help arrives. Drowning survivors have been resuscitated after surprisingly long periods.
- **Do NOT leave a recovered person alone** — secondary drowning (fluid in the lungs causing problems hours later) is real. Anyone who was submerged must be monitored in hospital for at least 6 hours.
- **Do NOT assume that because the person is coughing and walking they are fine** — secondary drowning can occur 4–24 hours after the event, especially in children.

---

## WHEN TO GO TO HOSPITAL

Go to hospital **immediately** after any submersion event — no exceptions.

This applies even if:
- The person appears fully recovered
- The submersion was brief
- The person is walking, talking, and insisting they are fine

**Secondary drowning** — also called delayed drowning — happens when water in the lungs causes a slow buildup of fluid and breathing failure hours after the event. Children are especially vulnerable. A child who was submerged can seem completely fine, then deteriorate and die hours later at home.

Hospital observation for a minimum of 6 hours after any submersion is required.

Go as an **immediate emergency** (CPR continuing if needed) if:
- The person is not breathing
- The person is unconscious
- The person was submerged for more than a few seconds
- The person is confused, very pale, or has blue lips

## SAFETY NET

Every second without breathing causes brain injury. Rescue breaths and CPR — started immediately — save lives and protect the brain.

Do not delay to find help before starting rescue breaths. Shout for help while you begin. If you are alone, give 5 rescue breaths and 2 minutes of CPR before stopping to run for help.

**Prevention reminder:** Children drown silently and quickly in wells, buckets, ponds, and rivers. A child can drown in less than 5 cm of water. Never leave a child under 5 unattended near any open water source — including household water storage containers.
