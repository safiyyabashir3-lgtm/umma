# Toothache / Dental Pain — Symptom Care File (Child)
**UMMA AI | Maternal & Child Health | Kano/Northern Nigeria Context**
**Target: Children 0–5 years**

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- Swelling of the face, jaw, or neck that is spreading rapidly
- Child cannot open their mouth, swallow, or breathe normally due to swelling
- Child has difficulty breathing alongside jaw or face swelling (possible airway compromise from dental abscess)
- Child has high fever above 39°C alongside face swelling
- Child has a seizure alongside dental pain
- Child is unconscious or extremely difficult to rouse
- Swelling is tracking down toward the neck (jaw and neck both swollen)

**Plain language:**
"A swollen face or neck with dental pain and fever is a serious infection that can spread to the throat and block breathing. This is an emergency. Go to the hospital immediately — not the chemist, not a traditional healer. Your child needs a doctor today. Do not delay."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Child has a swollen cheek or gum alongside toothache
- Child has fever above 38°C alongside tooth pain
- Toothache has lasted more than 2 days
- Child has a visible hole, blackened, or broken tooth causing pain
- A tooth has been knocked out or broken by injury
- Child has a gum abscess — a raised, pus-filled swelling on the gum
- Child is unable to eat or drink because of tooth pain
- Child under 2 years with visible tooth decay or gum swelling

**Plain language:**
"Your child's tooth pain needs to be seen by a health worker or dentist today. Tooth infections in children can spread quickly to the jaw and face. Go to the nearest clinic or PHC. If there is a dental clinic attached to the government hospital, go there. Tell the nurse about the pain, the swelling, and how long it has lasted."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
**Conditions for home care:**
- Pain is mild and came on recently (less than 2 days)
- No face or jaw swelling
- No fever above 38°C
- Child is still eating, drinking, and is alert
- No visible large cavity, abscess, or broken tooth
- Child is old enough to have milk teeth (older than 6 months)

**Home care instructions:**
1. Give paracetamol syrup at the correct child dose printed on the packet — this is the only safe pain medicine for children with toothache; do not exceed the recommended dose
2. Do not place aspirin or any tablet directly on the gum or tooth — this burns the gum tissue and does not help pain
3. Offer cool (not cold, not hot) fluids — warm or very cold temperatures worsen dental pain
4. Offer soft foods — pap, soft rice, yoghurt — if child is hungry but pain makes chewing difficult
5. Keep the mouth clean — gently rinse with warm clean water after eating if the child can manage this
6. Do not apply clove oil, herbal pastes, or any substance directly to the tooth or gum without dental advice
7. Arrange to see a nurse or dentist within 1–2 days even if pain improves — underlying tooth decay needs treatment

**Plain language:**
"Give your child paracetamol for the pain. Offer soft, cool food and plenty of fluids. Do not put anything on the tooth or gum. This can help tonight, but your child must see a nurse or dentist within the next day or two — toothache does not go away on its own and the tooth will need treatment."

---

### MONITOR — With clear safety net
**Conditions:**
- Very mild sensitivity — child winces occasionally but is eating and drinking normally
- No swelling, no fever
- Possible teething discomfort in child under 2 years (gum swelling, drooling, irritability)
- No visible broken or very decayed tooth

**What to watch:**
- Is the pain getting worse or more frequent?
- Is any swelling appearing in the cheek, gum, or jaw?
- Is the child developing fever?
- Is the child refusing to eat or drink?
- Is the child becoming very distressed?

**Plain language:**
"Watch your child carefully for the next 24 hours. If the pain is not improving, swelling appears, or fever develops — go to the clinic. Toothache in young children always needs dental treatment eventually — plan to take your child to a dental clinic soon."

---

### UNCERTAIN — When UMMA cannot determine severity
**Plain language:**
"I cannot tell from what you described whether this toothache is mild or becoming serious. If there is any face or jaw swelling, any fever, or the child seems very unwell — go to the clinic today. When in doubt with dental pain in a young child, it is always safer to get it checked."

---

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**
- Your child's face, cheek, jaw, or neck becomes swollen
- Your child has difficulty breathing or swallowing alongside face swelling
- Your child has a very high fever alongside dental pain
- Your child becomes unable to open their mouth
- The swelling is growing quickly — spreading from the cheek toward the neck

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE
- Tooth decay (dental caries) is the most common cause of toothache in children under 5 in Nigeria — it is caused by frequent sugar exposure and poor oral hygiene
- Dental decay in milk (baby) teeth is not harmless — it causes pain, infection, and affects the development of adult teeth beneath
- Children's teeth should be cleaned twice daily from the time the first tooth appears — use a small soft toothbrush and a tiny smear of fluoride toothpaste
- Reduce sweet drinks — including malt drinks, fruit juice, and sweetened tea — especially between meals and at bedtime
- Dental care for children is available free or at very low cost at government hospitals with dental units — prevention is far better and cheaper than extraction
- Never give aspirin to children — it is dangerous in children under 16 and does not treat dental pain safely

---

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- **Sugar exposure:** In many Nigerian households, children are given sweetened drinks, sugary snacks, and tea with sugar from a very early age. This is a major driver of childhood tooth decay. UMMA is not judging your family's choices — but reducing how often and how much sugar touches children's teeth makes a very big difference to dental health.
- **Traditional dental remedies:** Some families use local bark, clove paste, or herbal preparations on a child's painful tooth. UMMA understands these are familiar remedies. However, some of these substances burn the gum tissue, cause chemical injury, or delay treatment of a spreading infection. Paracetamol is the safest and most effective option for temporary pain relief — see a nurse or dentist as soon as possible.
- **PMVs and chemists:** Do not buy adult pain tablets (ibuprofen tablets, diclofenac, aspirin) for a child with toothache. These are not safe for young children. Paracetamol syrup in the correct dose is the only safe over-the-counter option.
- **Dental services in Kano:** Dental units are available at Murtala Muhammad Specialist Hospital and Aminu Kano Teaching Hospital. PHC nurses can also assess dental pain and refer. Many children in Kano reach dental services only when a tooth is severely infected — early visits for pain or visible decay are much safer.
- **Fear and stigma:** Many families delay dental visits due to fear of extraction. Reassure your child that the nurse or dentist is there to stop the pain. Early treatment often means the tooth can be saved — but delay usually means extraction becomes the only option.
- **Malnutrition and dental health:** Children who are malnourished develop weaker tooth enamel and are more susceptible to decay. If your child is also growing poorly, mention this at the PHC — nutrition support and dental care can be provided together.
