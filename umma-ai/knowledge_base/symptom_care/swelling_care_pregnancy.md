# SYMPTOM CARE FILE: SWELLING IN PREGNANCY
## Nigerian Context — Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
Triggered by any of:
- Swelling with seizure or convulsion
- Swelling with severe headache and visual disturbance
- Swelling with confusion or loss of consciousness
- Swelling with no fetal movement
- Swelling with severe difficulty breathing
- Sudden severe swelling of face and hands appearing rapidly

Plain language: "This swelling is a serious warning sign. Please go to hospital immediately — do not wait. Get someone to take you now."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
Triggered by any of:
- Any new swelling of face or hands after 20 weeks
- Swelling with headache — even mild
- Swelling with visual symptoms — even mild
- Swelling with reduced urine output
- Swelling that appeared suddenly or is getting worse rapidly
- Swelling with high blood pressure history
- Any swelling where blood pressure has not been checked recently
- Any uncertainty about cause

Plain language: "This swelling needs to be checked by a doctor today. Please go to your nearest clinic — your blood pressure must be measured."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
- Mild swelling of feet and ankles only
- Present since early or mid pregnancy and not worsening
- No swelling of face or hands
- No headache
- No visual symptoms
- Baby moving normally
- Blood pressure checked recently and normal

Home care instructions:
- Elevate legs when resting — lie down and raise feet above the level of the heart
- Avoid standing for long periods
- Rest during the hottest part of the day
- Drink adequate clean water — do not restrict fluids
- Avoid tight clothing around legs
- Wear comfortable flat shoes
- Sleep on left side — improves blood flow
- Attend next antenatal appointment as scheduled

Plain language: "Mild foot swelling is common in pregnancy. Rest with your feet raised, drink water, and sleep on your left side. But please go to the clinic today if the swelling spreads to your face or hands, or if you develop a headache."

---

### MONITOR — With very clear safety net
- Mild ankle swelling only, long-standing, not worsening
- No associated symptoms
- Reassess at every antenatal visit
- Any change — go to clinic immediately

---

## SAFETY NET — Attach to EVERY response about swelling in pregnancy
"Please watch carefully. Go to hospital immediately if swelling spreads to your face or hands, if you develop a severe headache, if your vision becomes blurry or you see spots, if you have a fit, or if your baby stops moving. Swelling can be a sign of a serious blood pressure condition — do not ignore it."

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE FOR SWELLING IN PREGNANCY
- Never take water tablets or diuretics in pregnancy without medical prescription
- Do not restrict water intake — drinking less does not reduce swelling and is dangerous
- Elevate legs when resting — this is the safest and most effective home measure
- Sleep on left side to improve circulation
- Avoid salty and processed foods where possible
- Attend all antenatal appointments — blood pressure and urine must be checked regularly
- Swelling of face and hands is always more serious than swelling of feet alone

---

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- Hot climate in northern Nigeria worsens swelling — rest indoors during hottest hours
- Cultural belief that swelling is normal in pregnancy is partially true for feet but dangerous for face and hands — educate clearly
- Some traditional remedies may contain harmful substances — discourage all local remedies for swelling
- Blood pressure check is available at all primary health centres — essential for any swelling above the ankles
- Pre-eclampsia is a leading killer of pregnant women in northern Nigeria — swelling with headache must be treated as emergency until proven otherwise
- Many women present late — if swelling has been present for days with no check, go to clinic today regardless
