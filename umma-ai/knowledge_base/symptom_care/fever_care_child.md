# UMMA AI — Symptom Care File
# Symptom: FEVER IN A CHILD (under 5 years)
# Context: Nigeria / Northern Nigeria (Kano)

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- Child is having a seizure (convulsion) right now or has had one today
- Child is unconscious, cannot be woken, or is very difficult to rouse
- Child is breathing very fast, with nostrils flaring, or chest pulling in with each breath
- Child has a stiff neck or is arching their back
- Child has a rash that looks like small dark red or purple spots that do not fade when you press on them
- Child is under 3 months old with any fever
- Child looks very sick — limp, pale, grey, or blue lips
- Fever has lasted more than 5 days
- Child has not urinated in more than 8 hours (sign of severe dehydration)

**Plain language:**
"This is very serious. Please take your child to hospital or an emergency health facility right now. Do not wait. If you cannot go yourself, please send someone with the child immediately. While travelling, keep the child cool and offer small sips of water or ORS if they are awake and able to swallow."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Fever in a child aged 3–6 months
- High fever (feels very hot to touch) that has not come down after giving paracetamol
- Child is refusing all fluids and has not passed urine in 6 hours
- Child has had the fever for more than 2 days (48 hours)
- Child seems confused, unusually drowsy, or cannot stop crying
- Fever with vomiting and diarrhoea together — risk of fast dehydration
- Child has returned from or lives in an area with known malaria and has fever
- You are not sure what is causing the fever

**Plain language:**
"Your child needs to be seen by a health worker today — please go to your nearest Primary Health Centre or clinic before evening. Do not wait until tomorrow. While you prepare to go, give paracetamol syrup in the correct dose for weight, and offer the child fluids."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
**Conditions for home care:**
- Child is over 6 months old
- Fever is mild — child feels warm but is alert, playing, drinking, and urinating normally
- No seizures, no stiff neck, no rash, no difficulty breathing
- Fever has lasted less than 48 hours
- Child is known to be otherwise well — no recent illness, no malaria exposure

**Home care instructions:**
1. Give paracetamol syrup — use the correct amount for the child's weight, as written on the bottle. Give every 4–6 hours as needed. Do NOT give aspirin or ibuprofen.
2. Offer fluids often — breastmilk, water, ORS, or thin porridge. Fever causes water loss quickly.
3. Remove extra clothing and blankets. Keep the room ventilated.
4. Wipe the child's forehead, neck, and armpits with a clean cloth soaked in lukewarm (not cold) water. Do NOT use ice-cold water — this can cause shivering and raise temperature further.
5. Keep the child resting and comfortable.
6. Check the child often — especially at night.
7. Do NOT put the child under a fan blowing directly on them.

**Plain language:**
"You can care for your child at home for now. Give paracetamol syrup in the right amount for their weight, offer them plenty of fluids, and keep them cool and rested. Check on them often. If anything changes or the fever does not come down, go to the clinic."

---

### MONITOR — With clear safety net
**Conditions:**
- Mild fever in a child over 6 months, improving with paracetamol
- Child is alert, drinking, and passing urine
- No worrying signs present

**What to watch:**
- Temperature rising despite paracetamol
- Child becoming more drowsy, refusing fluids
- Seizure or stiffness
- New symptoms appearing — rash, fast breathing, vomiting
- Fever lasting beyond 48 hours

**Plain language:**
"Your child's fever seems mild right now. Watch them closely for the next few hours. If any new signs appear, or if they seem worse, do not wait — go to the clinic or hospital."

---

### UNCERTAIN — When UMMA cannot determine severity
**Plain language:**
"I'm not able to tell from what you have described how serious this fever is. To be safe, please take your child to your nearest health facility today so a health worker can examine them properly. It is always better to check."

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**

- Your child has a seizure (shaking or jerking of the body)
- Your child cannot be woken up or is very limp
- Your child's lips or skin look blue or grey
- Your child stops breathing normally or is breathing very fast with chest pulling in
- Your child has not passed urine for 8 hours or more
- A rash appears — especially dark red or purple spots
- Your child's neck becomes stiff
- The fever lasts more than 5 days
- You feel something is very wrong — trust your instinct and go

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE

- **Paracetamol only** — this is the only safe fever medicine. Always check the bottle for the right amount based on the child's weight, not age.
- **Do not give aspirin** — it is dangerous for children and can cause a serious illness called Reye's syndrome.
- **Do not give ibuprofen** — it is not recommended for young children with fever in this setting.
- **ORS for dehydration** — if the child has diarrhoea or vomiting with the fever, start ORS immediately. It is available at most patent medicine stores and Primary Health Centres.
- **Breastfeeding** — continue breastfeeding a breastfed infant with fever. It provides fluids and comfort.
- **Tepid sponging** — lukewarm water on the forehead, neck, and armpits helps bring temperature down gently.
- **Do not bundle up** — many families dress a feverish child in extra clothes or wrap them tightly. This traps heat and makes the fever worse.

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE

**Heat and environment:**
Northern Nigeria is very hot, especially in dry season (harmattan). High ambient temperatures make fever worse and dehydration faster. Keep the child in the coolest room in the house, use shade, and ventilate the space. Avoid going out in peak afternoon heat if possible.

**Transport barriers:**
If you are far from a health facility, start home care immediately while arranging transport. Do not delay fluids and paracetamol while waiting for a vehicle. Community health workers (CHEWs) or village health volunteers can sometimes assist with referral.

**Patent medicine vendors (PMVs):**
PMVs are often the first point of contact. They can provide paracetamol syrup and ORS sachets, which are appropriate. However, PMVs should not replace a clinic visit if the child has had fever for more than 2 days, is under 6 months, or has any danger signs. For malaria-suspected fever, only take antimalarials prescribed by a trained health worker — do not self-treat.

**Malaria:**
Fever in northern Nigeria, especially during and after rainy season, must always be considered as possible malaria until tested. If a rapid malaria test (RDT) is available at a nearby PHC, this is strongly recommended for any fever lasting more than 24 hours. Do not assume fever is "just teething" or a cold — malaria kills children quickly.

**Herbal remedies:**
It is understood that many families use traditional or herbal remedies for fever — including herbs given by mouth or rubbed on the body. UMMA respectfully advises against this. Some herbal preparations can be harmful to young children, may delay treatment, or may interact with medicines. The safest treatment for fever is paracetamol and fluids. If you have already given an herbal remedy, please tell the health worker at the clinic.

**Teething:**
Teething does not cause high fever. A child who is teething may have a very slightly raised temperature, but a true fever (feeling very hot) in a teething child should be treated the same as any fever — with paracetamol and a clinic visit if it does not resolve quickly.

**Family support:**
Ask a family member or neighbour to help you monitor the child overnight. Two people watching are better than one — especially if the child is very young.

**Nearest PHC:**
Identify your nearest Primary Health Centre before your child becomes sick. Know the opening hours. Some PHCs in Kano State offer extended hours or 24-hour maternity services.
