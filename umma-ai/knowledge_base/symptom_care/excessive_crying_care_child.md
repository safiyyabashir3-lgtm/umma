# Excessive Crying — Symptom Care File (Child)
**UMMA AI | Maternal & Child Health | Kano/Northern Nigeria Context**
**Target: Children 0–5 years, with emphasis on infants 0–6 months**

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- Child has a high-pitched, unusual, or weak cry (different from their normal cry)
- Child is inconsolable for more than 3 hours with no pause
- Child has a bulging fontanelle (the soft spot on the baby's head is raised or bulging)
- Child has a seizure (convulsion) before, during, or after crying
- Child becomes floppy, limp, or unresponsive
- Child has purple, blue, or grey discoloration of the lips or skin
- Crying follows a fall, injury, or a time when the child was out of sight
- Child has a rapidly distending (swelling) abdomen alongside crying
- Child is under 2 months with inconsolable crying and fever above 38°C
- Child has a lump or swelling in the groin or scrotum (possible hernia)
- Crying is accompanied by episodes where the child goes very still and pale ("attacks")

**Plain language:**
"This level of crying is not normal and is a warning sign that something may be very wrong. Your child needs to be seen by a doctor right now — not the chemist, not a traditional healer. Go to the nearest hospital immediately. If the child is floppy, not waking, or has a bulging head — this is a life-threatening emergency. Go now."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Crying has lasted more than 2 hours without any calm period
- Child has fever alongside excessive crying, especially under 3 months
- Child is not feeding or is refusing the breast/bottle
- Child pulls up legs to the chest repeatedly during crying (possible abdominal pain or intussusception in infants)
- Child has vomited more than twice during the crying episode
- Child cries during or after urination (possible urinary tract infection)
- Crying started suddenly after the child seemed well
- Child has not passed stool in more than 3 days (in infants who normally stool regularly)
- Any excessive crying in a newborn under 4 weeks

**Plain language:**
"Your baby's crying needs to be checked by a nurse today. It could be a simple problem like hunger, gas, or an ear infection — or it could be something that needs treatment. Do not guess. Go to the PHC or clinic this morning or afternoon and tell them how long the baby has been crying and what other signs you have noticed."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
**Conditions for home care:**
- Child is older than 3 months
- Crying comes and goes — child has calm periods
- No fever, or fever is low (under 38°C)
- Child is feeding, even if less than usual
- No vomiting, no swollen belly, no leg-pulling
- Child can be partially soothed (picking up, feeding, rocking helps)
- No injury, fall, or unusual event before crying started
- Fontanelle is normal (not bulging)

**Home care instructions:**
1. Check for obvious causes first: Is the child hungry? Is the nappy soiled or wet? Is the child too hot or too cold? Is there a hair or thread wrapped tightly around a finger or toe? (Check carefully)
2. Pick up and hold the child close — skin-to-skin contact calms many babies
3. Offer the breast if breastfeeding — suckling soothes infants beyond just feeding
4. Rock the child gently or walk with them
5. Check for any visible rash, swelling, injury, or anything on the body that looks wrong
6. Keep the environment quiet and dim — overstimulation can worsen crying in young infants
7. Do not shake the baby — shaking causes serious brain injury
8. Do not give any medicine, herb, or gripe water without consulting a nurse first
9. If you have tried all of the above for 1 hour and the child is still crying — go to the clinic

**Plain language:**
"Check if your baby is hungry, wet, or too hot. Hold them close, offer the breast, and check all over the body for anything that looks wrong — including around the fingers and toes. If your baby calms down, watch carefully. If the crying continues for more than 1 hour or anything below happens — go to the clinic today."

---

### MONITOR — With clear safety net
**Conditions:**
- Crying episode has just started (less than 30 minutes)
- Child has a known pattern of fussiness at a particular time of day (e.g. evening colic)
- Child is feeding and can be partially soothed
- No other symptoms present
- Child is older than 3 months and gaining weight normally

**What to watch:**
- Is the crying getting louder, higher-pitched, or more distressed?
- Is the baby not consolable at all?
- Is the baby refusing to feed?
- Is the belly becoming swollen?
- Is there any fever?
- Has the cry changed from normal?

**Plain language:**
"Watch your baby for the next 30–60 minutes. If this is their normal fussy time and they settle after feeding or holding, that is reassuring. But if the crying becomes harder, more intense, or the signs below appear — go to the clinic."

---

### UNCERTAIN — When UMMA cannot determine severity
**Plain language:**
"I cannot tell from what you have described whether this crying is normal fussiness or a sign of something that needs treatment. When I am uncertain about a crying baby — especially a young infant — I always recommend going to the clinic. A nurse can check the baby properly and find the cause. Please go today."

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**
- The soft spot on your baby's head is raised, tight, or bulging
- Your baby has a seizure (shaking, stiffening, eyes rolling)
- Your baby becomes limp, floppy, or unresponsive
- The cry becomes very high-pitched or very weak — different from normal
- Your baby's lips or fingers turn blue or grey
- You see a lump in the groin or between the legs
- The belly becomes hard and swollen
- Your baby was dropped, fell, or was shaken before the crying started

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE
- In the first 3 months of life, some babies cry for long periods without an obvious cause — this is sometimes called "colic." It is distressing for the mother but usually not dangerous. However, always rule out other causes first.
- A "hair tourniquet" — a strand of hair wrapped around a toe, finger, or penis — can cause severe pain and is easy to miss. Always undress and check the whole baby carefully when crying has no obvious cause.
- NEVER shake a baby — even gentle shaking can cause severe brain bleeding. If you are overwhelmed, put the baby down safely on their back and take a short break. Ask for help from another family member.
- Breastfeeding on demand reduces many common causes of infant crying: hunger, thirst, and comfort
- Gripe water sold at chemists is not proven safe or effective and some contain alcohol or harmful additives — do not use without nurse advice

---

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- **Herbal remedies for colic:** Many families give 'agbo,' herbal gripe mixtures, local bitters, or 'ori' (shea butter) preparations for crying infants. UMMA understands these are part of cultural caregiving traditions. However, these have caused liver damage, convulsions, and deaths in young children. Please do not give herbal mixtures to infants under 6 months for crying — bring the baby to the PHC instead.
- **Gripe water:** Some gripe water products sold in Nigerian pharmacies and patent medicine shops contain alcohol and unsafe additives. Do not give gripe water to newborns or infants under 4 months without a nurse's advice.
- **Family pressure:** Mothers of crying babies often face pressure from mothers-in-law and family members to use traditional remedies. UMMA asks that you protect your baby by seeking the nurse's opinion first.
- **PHC under-5 clinic:** PHC nurses see excessive crying frequently. You will not be judged for coming — this is exactly the right place to bring a crying baby you cannot settle.
- **Heat:** In Kano's hot climate, overheating is a real cause of infant distress and crying. Check if the baby is sweating, dressed too warmly, or in a hot room. Remove excess clothing and move to a cooler, ventilated space.
- **Exhaustion:** Caring for a constantly crying baby is exhausting and distressing. Your wellbeing matters too. Ask a trusted family member to take the baby for a short period so you can rest. A rested mother is better able to care for her child.
