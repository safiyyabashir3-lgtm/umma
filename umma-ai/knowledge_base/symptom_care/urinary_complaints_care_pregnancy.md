# Urinary Complaints — Symptom Care File (Pregnancy)
**UMMA AI | Maternal & Child Health | Kano/Northern Nigeria Context**
**Target: Pregnant women, all trimesters**
**Covers: Painful urination, frequent urination, reduced urination, blood in urine, inability to urinate**

---

## CRITICAL NOTE FOR UMMA
Urinary tract infections (UTIs) in pregnancy are significantly more dangerous than in non-pregnant women. Asymptomatic bacteriuria (bacteria in the urine without symptoms) in pregnancy carries a high risk of progression to pyelonephritis (kidney infection), which can trigger preterm labour, sepsis, and maternal death. Frequency of urination is also a normal symptom of pregnancy — UMMA must distinguish between physiological frequency and symptomatic UTI. Reduced urine output or blood in urine in a pregnant woman must be treated with high urgency.

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- No urine output for 8 or more hours in a pregnant woman
- Blood visible in the urine — pink, red, or dark brown/cola-coloured
- Severe back or loin pain with high fever and rigors (shaking chills) — possible severe pyelonephritis or sepsis
- Urinary symptoms with confusion or altered consciousness — possible urosepsis
- Unable to urinate with severe pain in the lower abdomen — possible acute urinary retention
- Urinary symptoms with swelling of face and hands and severe headache — possible pre-eclampsia with renal involvement
- Fever above 39°C with urinary complaints — possible kidney infection or sepsis
- Urinary symptoms in a woman in preterm labour or with vaginal fluid leakage

**Plain language:**
"This is serious. A kidney infection in pregnancy can cause premature birth and blood poisoning very quickly. Go to the hospital immediately. Do not take any medicine at home before going. Take your antenatal card."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Burning or pain when passing urine — at any stage of pregnancy, always needs a urine test
- Frequent urination with pain, burning, or unusual smell — as distinct from normal pregnancy frequency
- Urine that smells strongly or looks cloudy or dark
- Fever at or above 37.5°C with any urinary symptom
- Lower back or loin pain that is new or worsening — especially if one-sided
- Urinary symptoms with nausea and vomiting
- Any urinary complaint in a woman with diabetes in pregnancy — she is at particularly high risk of serious UTI
- Urinary symptoms in a woman who has had a UTI in this pregnancy already — recurrence needs assessment and possibly different antibiotic
- Urinary symptoms with vaginal discharge — possible combined infection
- Reduced urine output — passing much less urine than normal, or urine looks dark and concentrated despite drinking

**Plain language:**
"Any burning or pain when you pass urine during pregnancy needs a urine test today. UTIs in pregnancy can quickly spread to the kidneys if not treated with the right antibiotic. Go to the PHC or ANC clinic and ask for a urine test. Do not buy antibiotics from the chemist without being tested and getting a prescription — the wrong antibiotic will not treat the infection and can make it worse."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
**Conditions for home care:**
- Urinary frequency only — passing urine more often than usual, but no pain, burning, or smell
- This is clearly the normal frequency of pregnancy — common in first trimester (uterus pressing on bladder) and third trimester (baby's head pressing on bladder)
- No blood in urine
- No fever
- No back or loin pain
- No burning or discomfort
- Urine looks clear and pale yellow — normal colour
- No other symptoms

**Home care instructions for normal pregnancy urinary frequency:**
1. Continue drinking adequate fluids — do not reduce water intake to try to urinate less; this concentrates the urine and increases the risk of UTI
2. Empty the bladder completely each time — lean slightly forward when sitting on the toilet to fully empty
3. Do not hold urine for long periods — go when you feel the urge
4. Wear comfortable, breathable cotton underwear
5. Clean the genital area from front to back after using the toilet
6. If night-time urination is very disruptive: reduce fluid intake in the 1–2 hours before bed; ensure fluid intake is adequate during the daytime
7. Report any change in urine smell, colour, or any appearance of burning at your next ANC visit or sooner if it develops

**Plain language:**
"Needing to pass urine more often is normal in pregnancy — the growing uterus and baby press on the bladder. Keep drinking water, empty the bladder fully each time, and do not hold it in. If any burning, pain, bad smell, or blood appears — go to the clinic immediately."

---

### MONITOR — With clear safety net
**Conditions:**
- Frequency is the only symptom — no burning, no blood, no fever, no smell
- Has been consistent throughout pregnancy and previously assessed at ANC
- Urine looks normal
- Baby is moving normally (after 28 weeks)

**What to watch:**
- Has any burning or pain started when passing urine?
- Has the urine developed an unusual or strong smell?
- Has the colour changed — dark, pink, red, or cloudy?
- Has fever developed?
- Has back or loin pain appeared?
- Is the woman passing much less urine than usual despite drinking normally?

**Plain language:**
"Watch for any changes — pain, smell, colour, or fever. These mean go to the clinic today. Normal frequency without any of these changes is expected in pregnancy."

---

### UNCERTAIN — When UMMA cannot determine severity
**Plain language:**
"I am not sure from what you described whether this is the normal urinary frequency of pregnancy or a sign of a urine infection. A urine test at the PHC will answer this quickly and safely. Please go today — it takes only a few minutes and will tell you whether treatment is needed. Do not self-treat with antibiotics."

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**
- You have not passed urine for 8 hours
- You see blood in your urine — any pink, red, or dark colour
- You develop a high fever with shaking chills and back pain
- You develop severe pain in your back or side alongside urinary symptoms
- You feel confused or very unwell alongside urinary symptoms
- You cannot pass urine despite needing to urgently — and are in pain
- Your face and hands swell alongside reduced urine output

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE
- UTIs are among the most common infections in pregnant women — up to 10% of pregnant women have bacteria in their urine without symptoms (asymptomatic bacteriuria), which progresses to kidney infection in up to 40% if untreated; this is why urine is tested at every ANC visit even without symptoms
- The correct antibiotic for a UTI in pregnancy depends on the type of bacteria — this requires a urine test; nitrofurantoin, amoxicillin, and cephalosporins are commonly used in pregnancy; trimethoprim is avoided especially in the first trimester; the nurse will prescribe the right one
- Drinking adequate clean water is the single most important preventive measure for UTIs — aim for at least 8 cups per day; more in hot weather
- Wiping from front to back after using the toilet prevents bowel bacteria from entering the urethra
- Constipation — very common in pregnancy — can put pressure on the bladder and worsen urinary symptoms; increasing fibre and fluid intake and walking gently can help
- Complete the full course of any prescribed antibiotic — stopping early allows the infection to return and become harder to treat

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- **Self-treatment with antibiotics:** In Nigeria, it is very common to purchase antibiotics — tetracycline, ampicillin, ciprofloxacin — from patent medicine vendors without a prescription. In pregnancy, this is particularly dangerous: tetracycline and ciprofloxacin (fluoroquinolones) are contraindicated in pregnancy as they can harm the baby's bone and cartilage development and teeth formation. Please do not buy antibiotics from a PMV during pregnancy without a nurse's prescription.
- **UTI and preterm labour:** Many women in northern Nigeria are admitted in preterm labour without knowing they had a UTI — the bacteria can irritate the uterus and trigger contractions. This is preventable with ANC urine testing and treatment. Attending ANC regularly and having urine checked is not just bureaucracy — it prevents preterm birth.
- **Water source and quality:** Many households in Kano rely on boreholes, wells, or stored water. Contaminated water is a significant source of UTI-causing bacteria. Boil water for drinking if you are uncertain of the source. Use a clean container with a lid for storing drinking water.
- **Diabetes in pregnancy:** Gestational diabetes and pre-existing diabetes dramatically increase the risk of severe UTI. If you have been told your blood sugar is high in this pregnancy, any urinary symptoms must be assessed on the same day — not managed at home.
- **Reduced urine and pre-eclampsia:** A reduction in urine output in the second or third trimester — especially when combined with face swelling, headache, or high blood pressure — can be a sign of kidney involvement in pre-eclampsia. This must be assessed urgently at the hospital. Do not assume reduced urine is simply because you have not drunk enough water.
- **Postpartum UTI:** Urinary tract infections are common after delivery — especially after catheterisation during labour. Any burning, frequency, or fever in the postpartum period must be assessed at the PHC or hospital the same day.
- **PHC urine testing:** Most PHCs in Kano can perform urine dipstick testing. This is quick, free or very low cost, and identifies whether infection is present. A urine culture (to identify the exact bacteria and the best antibiotic) is available at higher-level facilities. Ask for a urine test if you have urinary symptoms — do not accept treatment without being tested.
