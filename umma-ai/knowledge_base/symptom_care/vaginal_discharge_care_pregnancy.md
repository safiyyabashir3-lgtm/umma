# Vaginal Discharge — Symptom Care File (Pregnancy)
**UMMA AI | Maternal & Child Health | Kano/Northern Nigeria Context**
**Target: Pregnant women, all trimesters**

---

## CRITICAL NOTE FOR UMMA
Vaginal discharge is common in pregnancy due to increased oestrogen and cervical secretions. Normal discharge (leucorrhoea) is white or clear, odourless or mildly scented, and not associated with itch or pain. Abnormal discharge — yellow, green, grey, or foul-smelling — indicates infection and must be treated. Sexually transmitted infections (STIs) in pregnancy cause preterm labour, neonatal eye infection, and vertical transmission to the baby. A sudden gush of clear fluid must always be considered rupture of membranes until proven otherwise. UMMA must distinguish carefully between normal leucorrhoea, infection, and membrane rupture.

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- Sudden gush of clear or pale fluid from the vagina — this is rupture of membranes until proven otherwise; this is an emergency at any gestation
- Continuous leaking of watery fluid that does not stop (soaking pad after pad with clear fluid)
- Discharge with heavy vaginal bleeding
- Discharge with fever above 38°C and abdominal pain — possible chorioamnionitis (infection of the membranes) — life-threatening for mother and baby
- Foul-smelling discharge with fever and uterine tenderness in the postpartum period (sepsis)
- Discharge with signs of preterm labour — regular painful contractions before 37 weeks alongside fluid or discharge

**Plain language:**
"If you have a sudden gush of fluid from your vagina — or if you are leaking fluid continuously — go to the hospital immediately. This may be your waters breaking, which is an emergency at any stage before your due date. Do not wait to see if it stops. Go now. Take your antenatal card."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Yellow or green discharge at any stage of pregnancy
- Grey discharge with fishy smell — possible bacterial vaginosis (associated with preterm labour)
- Discharge with intense itching and thick cottage-cheese appearance — possible thrush (Candida), which is very common in pregnancy and requires treatment
- Discharge with burning, pain, or soreness in the vaginal area
- Any discharge with ulcers or sores in or around the vagina
- Discharge with dysuria (pain when passing urine)
- Frothy or bubbly discharge — possible Trichomonas
- Discharge with lower abdominal pain or pelvic discomfort
- Any change from the woman's normal pregnancy discharge pattern that concerns her
- Discharge in a woman whose partner has been recently diagnosed with or treated for an STI
- Increased discharge with a new smell in the third trimester — possible early membrane changes or infection

**Plain language:**
"This type of discharge needs to be checked by a nurse today. Infections in the vagina during pregnancy can spread to the womb and cause preterm labour or harm the baby. Go to the nearest PHC or ANC clinic. Tell the nurse about the colour, smell, and how long it has been present. You may need a swab test and medicine. Do not treat it yourself with any cream or herbal preparation before being seen."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
**Conditions for home care:**
- Discharge is white or clear, not yellow, green, or grey
- No foul or unusual smell — mild or no odour only
- No itching, burning, or soreness
- No abdominal pain
- No blood mixed with discharge
- No fever
- Discharge has been present throughout the pregnancy in this same pattern, previously assessed as normal at ANC
- No sudden change in amount or character
- This is NOT a gush or continuous leaking of fluid

**Home care instructions:**
1. Wear clean, breathable cotton underwear — avoid synthetic fabrics that trap heat and moisture
2. Change underwear daily or more often if discharge is heavy
3. Clean the external vaginal area with clean water only — do not use soap, disinfectant, or any product inside the vagina
4. Do NOT douche — inserting water or any substance into the vagina is harmful in pregnancy and disrupts the normal protective bacterial balance
5. Do not use panty liners that are heavily perfumed or treated with chemicals — use unscented pads or clean cotton cloths
6. Avoid very tight clothing around the pelvis
7. If discharge increases, changes colour or smell, or you develop any itching or pain — go to the clinic

**Plain language:**
"Clear or white discharge without smell or itch is normal in pregnancy — the body produces more discharge to protect the cervix. Keep clean with water only, wear cotton underwear, and do not put anything inside the vagina. If anything changes — colour, smell, itch, or a sudden gush of fluid — go to the clinic the same day."

---

### MONITOR — With clear safety net
**Conditions:**
- White or clear, odourless or very mild discharge that has been stable throughout pregnancy
- No associated symptoms
- Previously assessed as normal at ANC
- Baby is moving normally (after 28 weeks)

**What to watch:**
- Has the discharge changed colour — becoming yellow, green, or grey?
- Has a new smell appeared — fishy, sour, or foul?
- Has there been any sudden increase in amount?
- Has any itching, burning, or soreness started?
- Is there any fluid that soaks the pad and keeps coming — could this be amniotic fluid?
- Has any abdominal pain or fever appeared?

**Plain language:**
"Your discharge sounds normal for now. Watch for any changes in colour, smell, or amount. If you notice a sudden gush of fluid or continuous leaking — go to hospital immediately. If itching, smell, or colour changes — go to the clinic the same day."

---

### UNCERTAIN — When UMMA cannot determine severity
**Plain language:**
"I am not sure from your description whether this discharge is normal or a sign of infection or membrane rupture. When I am uncertain about vaginal discharge in pregnancy — especially if there is any possibility it could be amniotic fluid — I always advise going to the clinic today. Please go and have a nurse examine you and do a swab if needed."

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**
- You have a sudden gush of clear or watery fluid from your vagina
- You are continuously leaking watery fluid — soaking pad after pad
- The discharge becomes heavy and foul-smelling with fever
- You develop bleeding alongside any discharge
- You have fever above 38°C with abdominal pain and discharge
- You go into labour before 37 weeks alongside any fluid or discharge changes

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE
- Normal pregnancy discharge (leucorrhoea) is clear to white, mild or odourless, and increases throughout pregnancy — this does not require treatment
- Bacterial vaginosis (BV) is associated with preterm labour and low birth weight — it requires antibiotic treatment prescribed by a nurse; it is common and not a sign of infidelity but of a normal bacterial imbalance
- Thrush (Candida) is extremely common in pregnancy due to hormonal changes — it causes thick white cottage-cheese discharge with intense itching; it is treated with antifungal pessaries or cream prescribed by the nurse; it is not dangerous to the baby but is very uncomfortable and should be treated
- Gonorrhoea and chlamydia in pregnancy cause preterm labour and serious neonatal eye infection (ophthalmia neonatorum) — both are treatable with antibiotics when detected early at ANC
- Trichomonas is a sexually transmitted infection that causes frothy yellow-green discharge and is associated with preterm birth — it must be treated alongside the partner
- Every woman should attend ANC for STI screening — many infections cause no symptoms and are only detected by swab test

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- **Douching and internal washing:** Vaginal douching — washing inside the vagina with water, soap, alum (tawas), or herbal preparations — is a common practice across Nigeria, believed to maintain cleanliness and hygiene. UMMA understands this practice and the intention behind it. However, douching in pregnancy disrupts the protective bacterial balance of the vagina, increases the risk of bacterial vaginosis and ascending infection, and can trigger preterm labour. Please use clean water for external cleaning only.
- **Alum (tawas/potassium alum) use:** In some communities in northern Nigeria, alum is used intravaginally for hygiene or to "tighten" the vagina. This practice is harmful in pregnancy — it dries and irritates delicate tissue, disrupts normal flora, and can cause chemical burns. Please do not use alum internally during pregnancy.
- **STI stigma:** Sexually transmitted infections carry significant social stigma in Nigerian communities. Women may delay seeking care out of fear of judgment or accusation. UMMA wants every woman to know: STIs are medical conditions that are treated, not judged. Getting treatment during pregnancy protects your baby. PHC nurses are there to help, not to shame you.
- **Partner treatment:** For STIs like trichomonas, chlamydia, and gonorrhoea — if you are treated but your partner is not, reinfection occurs. Ask the nurse about partner treatment — in many cases, partner medicine can be provided or referred at the same visit.
- **Amniotic fluid vs discharge:** Many women cannot tell the difference between increased normal discharge and leaking amniotic fluid (waters breaking). A simple way to check: put on a clean dry pad and lie down for 30 minutes. If the pad is wet with clear, odourless fluid when you get up — especially if it keeps coming — this could be amniotic fluid. Go to the hospital. If you are unsure — go to the hospital. It is always better to check.
- **PHC swab testing:** Many PHCs in Kano can perform high vaginal swabs and test for common infections. This service is free or low cost and takes a few minutes. If you have unusual discharge, a swab is the only way to know the cause and choose the right treatment. Do not treat with over-the-counter vaginal creams without knowing the cause — the wrong treatment will not work and may delay proper care.
