# SYMPTOM CARE FILE: HEADACHE IN PREGNANCY
## Nigerian Context — Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
Triggered by any of:
- Headache with seizure or convulsion
- Headache with loss of consciousness or confusion
- Headache with sudden vision loss or significant visual disturbance
- Headache with no fetal movement
- Headache with heavy vaginal bleeding
- Severe headache described as worst ever experienced
- Headache with inability to stand, walk, or speak normally
- Headache with stiff neck and fever

Plain language: "This headache is a serious warning sign. Please go to the hospital immediately — do not wait, do not try any medicine first. Get help to take you now."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
Triggered by any of:
- Any headache after 20 weeks of pregnancy with swelling of face or hands
- Headache with swelling of feet that is new or worsening
- Headache with reduced urine output
- Headache lasting more than 6 hours that is not improving
- Headache with vomiting that will not stop
- Headache with high fever
- Headache returning repeatedly over days
- Any headache where blood pressure has not been checked recently
- Any uncertainty about cause

Plain language: "This headache needs a doctor to check you today. Please go to your nearest clinic or health centre — do not wait until tomorrow. Your blood pressure needs to be checked."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
- Mild headache, clearly related to tension or tiredness
- Under 20 weeks of pregnancy
- No swelling of face or hands
- Baby moving normally
- No fever
- No visual symptoms
- Headache improving with rest
- Adequate fluids being taken

Home care instructions:
- Rest in a quiet, cool room
- Drink plenty of clean water — dehydration is a common cause of headache in pregnancy
- Paracetamol only if needed — do not take aspirin or ibuprofen in pregnancy
- Do not take any herbal remedies or local medicines
- Lie down and rest
- Apply cool cloth to forehead if comfortable
- Monitor baby movements carefully
- Return to clinic immediately if headache worsens, vision changes, or swelling appears

Plain language: "This headache sounds mild. Please rest, drink water, and take paracetamol if needed. Watch carefully and go to the clinic immediately if anything changes or the headache gets worse."

---

### MONITOR — With very clear safety net
- Mild headache, early pregnancy, no risk features
- Improving with rest and fluids within 1 to 2 hours
- Must reassess — if not clearly better within 2 hours, go to clinic
- Any new symptom appearing — go to clinic immediately

---

## SAFETY NET — Attach to EVERY response about headache in pregnancy
"Please watch carefully. Go to hospital immediately if your headache becomes very severe or feels like the worst headache you have ever had, if your vision becomes blurry or you see spots, if your face or hands start swelling, if you have a fit, if your baby stops moving, or if you feel confused. Do not wait if you are worried — it is always safer to be seen by a doctor."

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE FOR HEADACHE IN PREGNANCY
- Paracetamol is the only safe painkiller in pregnancy — use correct dose
- Do not take aspirin at any stage of pregnancy
- Do not take ibuprofen or diclofenac in pregnancy
- Do not take any herbal or traditional remedies for headache in pregnancy
- Drink at least 8 glasses of clean water daily — dehydration worsens headache
- Rest in a cool, quiet place
- Avoid bright light and noise if headache is severe
- Attend all antenatal appointments — blood pressure must be checked regularly
- Do not ignore a headache in pregnancy — it can be a warning sign of a serious condition

---

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- In hot northern Nigerian climate — dehydration is very common and worsens headache, ensure adequate fluid intake at all times
- Cultural tendency to use local remedies or analgesics bought from roadside sellers — strongly discourage in pregnancy
- Paracetamol is available at all primary health centres and is safe
- Blood pressure check is available at all antenatal clinics — this is the most important test for headache in pregnancy
- Pre-eclampsia is a leading killer of pregnant women in northern Nigeria — headache must never be dismissed
- If husband or family is reluctant to allow clinic visit for headache — explain that blood pressure can rise silently and cause fits without warning
- Primary health centre is appropriate first point of care — no need to go directly to teaching hospital unless emergency signs present
