# Headache — Symptom Care File (Pregnancy)
**UMMA AI | Maternal & Child Health | Kano/Northern Nigeria Context**
**Target: Pregnant women, all trimesters**

---

## CRITICAL NOTE FOR UMMA
Headache in pregnancy carries a different risk profile from headache outside pregnancy. In the second half of pregnancy and postpartum period, new or severe headache must be treated as a symptom of pre-eclampsia or eclampsia until proven otherwise. Pre-eclampsia headache is one of the warning signs that precedes eclamptic seizure — which carries a significant risk of maternal death. UMMA must always assess for accompanying symptoms of pre-eclampsia: visual disturbance, swelling of face and hands, epigastric pain, and high blood pressure. Headache with any of these is an emergency.

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- Severe headache — the worst headache the woman has ever had, or a headache she describes as "like something exploded"
- Headache with visual disturbance — blurred vision, flashing lights, seeing spots, loss of vision in one or both eyes
- Headache with swelling of the face or hands
- Headache with upper abdominal pain (epigastric or right upper quadrant)
- Headache with confusion, agitation, or altered consciousness
- Headache followed by or occurring alongside a seizure (convulsion)
- Headache with fever, stiff neck, and sensitivity to light — possible meningitis
- Headache with sudden weakness or numbness of one side of the face, arm, or leg
- Headache with difficulty speaking or understanding speech
- Headache at any point after 20 weeks in a woman who has not had her blood pressure checked recently
- Headache with high blood pressure reading (140/90 or above) at home or at facility
- Any headache in the postpartum period (within 6 weeks of delivery) — especially if new or severe
- Sudden onset of the worst headache in a woman who was previously well

**Plain language:**
"A severe headache in pregnancy — especially with blurred vision, face swelling, or fits — is one of the most serious warning signs. This could be pre-eclampsia or eclampsia. Both can kill a mother and her baby within hours if not treated. Go to the hospital right now. Do not take any medicine at home and wait. Do not go to the chemist. Go directly to the hospital and tell them you are pregnant and have a severe headache. This is an emergency."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Headache that has lasted more than 4 hours and is not relieved by paracetamol
- New headache in the second or third trimester in a woman who does not normally get headaches
- Headache with any swelling — even mild swelling of ankles and feet alongside head pain
- Headache with nausea and vomiting in the second or third trimester
- Headache with fever at or above 37.5°C
- Recurrent headaches over the past week that are different from usual
- Headache in a woman who has been told she has high blood pressure in pregnancy
- Headache with reduced fetal movements (after 28 weeks)
- Headache with any urinary symptoms (possible severe UTI or kidney infection)

**Plain language:**
"This headache needs to be checked by a nurse today. Your blood pressure must be measured and your urine must be tested. Go to the nearest PHC or ANC clinic and take your antenatal card. Tell the nurse exactly when the headache started, how severe it is, and all other symptoms you have. Do not wait until tomorrow."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
**Conditions for home care:**
- Headache is mild and clearly fits a known pattern — tension headache or dehydration headache
- Headache is in the first trimester and is consistent with the hormonal headaches of early pregnancy
- No visual disturbance, no face or hand swelling, no epigastric pain
- No fever
- Blood pressure was confirmed normal at a recent ANC visit (within the past 1–2 weeks)
- Baby is moving normally (after 28 weeks)
- Headache responds to paracetamol and rest
- No neurological symptoms — no weakness, no speech difficulty, no confusion

**Home care instructions:**
1. Take paracetamol at the recommended adult dose — this is the only safe pain medicine for headache in pregnancy; take with water and food if your stomach is empty
2. Do NOT take aspirin, ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac, or any NSAID — these are dangerous in pregnancy
3. Do NOT take codeine-based pain medicines — these are opioids and are not safe to take regularly in pregnancy without medical supervision
4. Drink a full glass of clean water immediately — dehydration is a very common cause of headache in pregnancy, especially in Kano's heat
5. Rest in a cool, quiet, darkened room
6. Apply a cool damp cloth to the forehead and back of the neck
7. If the headache is related to tension or posture: gently stretch the neck, relax the shoulders, and avoid looking down at a phone or working in a hunched position for long periods
8. Sleep if possible — rest is one of the most effective remedies for tension and hormonal headache
9. If the headache is not improving within 1–2 hours of paracetamol — go to the clinic
10. Check your face and hands in a mirror — if any puffiness appears, go to the hospital

**Plain language:**
"Take paracetamol, drink water, rest in a cool quiet place, and use a cool cloth on your forehead. These help with mild headaches. But if the headache is not getting better within 1–2 hours, or you notice any swelling in your face or vision changes — go to the hospital immediately. Do not manage a headache in pregnancy for more than a few hours at home."

---

### MONITOR — With clear safety net
**Conditions:**
- Headache is very mild and has just started
- Woman has a known history of tension headaches and this feels the same
- No other symptoms at all
- First trimester, blood pressure confirmed normal recently
- Baby moving normally

**What to watch — every hour:**
- Is the headache getting worse rather than better?
- Has paracetamol helped?
- Has any vision change appeared — blurring, spots, flashing lights?
- Has any face or hand swelling appeared?
- Has fever, stiff neck, or light sensitivity developed?
- Is there any new weakness, confusion, or difficulty with speech?
- Is the baby moving normally?

**Plain language:**
"Rest, drink water, and take paracetamol. Check yourself every hour. Look in a mirror — check your face for puffiness. If the headache worsens, vision changes, or any of the signs below appear — go immediately. Do not monitor a headache in pregnancy for more than 2–3 hours without seeking care."

---

### UNCERTAIN — When UMMA cannot determine severity
**Plain language:**
"I am not sure whether this headache is the normal kind that many pregnant women get or a warning sign of something serious. In the second half of pregnancy, a new or worsening headache must always be checked. Please go to the nearest PHC or clinic today to have your blood pressure and urine checked. Take paracetamol for now and do not delay going."

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**
- Your headache becomes very severe — the worst you have ever had
- You see blurred vision, flashing lights, or spots before your eyes
- Your face or hands become swollen
- You have pain in your upper abdomen alongside the headache
- You feel confused, very agitated, or unlike yourself
- You have a seizure or fit
- You develop weakness on one side of your body or face, or difficulty speaking
- You have a fever with stiff neck and sensitivity to light
- Your headache is not helped by paracetamol and is getting worse
- Any headache in the 6 weeks after delivery that is new or severe

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE
- Pre-eclampsia headache is typically felt at the front or back of the head, is persistent, and does not fully respond to paracetamol — this is different from a typical tension headache which eases with rest and pain relief
- Dehydration headache is the most common benign cause of headache in pregnant women in hot climates — drinking adequate clean water throughout the day prevents most of these
- Hormonal headaches in the first trimester are caused by rapid hormonal changes — they usually improve after the first trimester and are managed with paracetamol and rest
- Migraine can worsen in pregnancy — if a woman has a history of migraine, she should inform her doctor at ANC as some migraine medicines are not safe in pregnancy
- Blood pressure must be checked at every ANC visit — a woman cannot feel her own blood pressure; high blood pressure has no symptoms until it is severely elevated
- Regular ANC attendance is the most important protection against pre-eclampsia becoming a fatal emergency

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- **Pre-eclampsia awareness:** Pre-eclampsia is responsible for a significant proportion of maternal deaths in northern Nigeria. The headache that precedes eclamptic seizure is often described by women as "a pressure inside the head" or "something pressing the head from inside." Any new severe or persistent headache in a pregnant woman after 20 weeks must be treated as pre-eclampsia until the blood pressure is checked and found to be normal.
- **Herbal headache remedies:** Many families in Kano use local preparations for headache — menthol rubs, herbal teas, scalp massage with oils, or medicinal snuff. UMMA understands these are familiar and accessible. A cool cloth on the forehead is safe. However, oral herbal preparations — teas, bitter roots, leaves — should not be taken during pregnancy as safety is not established. Paracetamol is the safe and effective choice.
- **Heat and Harmattan:** Kano's extreme heat causes dehydration, which is a leading driver of headache in pregnancy. During harmattan, the dryness worsens this. Pregnant women should drink at least 8–10 cups of clean water daily. Staying indoors during the hottest part of the day reduces headache frequency.
- **Blood pressure monitoring:** Some women in urban Kano have access to home blood pressure monitors through pharmacies or family members. If a blood pressure machine is available, take a reading when a headache occurs. A reading of 140/90 or above during headache means go to the hospital immediately. A normal reading is reassuring but does not eliminate the need to seek care if other symptoms are present.
- **Postpartum headache:** Headache after delivery is taken seriously in northern Nigeria contexts because of the culturally common practice of "kadediya" (lying on a hot bed with high salt intake postpartum). This practice can worsen blood pressure and trigger postpartum pre-eclampsia. Any severe headache after delivery — even days or weeks after — must be assessed at hospital urgently.
- **Distance and night travel:** Pre-eclampsia can worsen suddenly at night. If a pregnant woman has a severe headache at night, she should not wait until morning. Go to the hospital now. Prepare transport plans in advance so that night emergencies are not delayed by lack of transport.
