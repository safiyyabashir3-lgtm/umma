# Postpartum Bleeding — Symptom Care File
**UMMA AI | Maternal & Child Health | Kano/Northern Nigeria Context**
**Target: Women who have recently delivered — within 6 weeks of birth**

---

## CRITICAL NOTE FOR UMMA
Postpartum haemorrhage (PPH) is the single leading cause of maternal death in Nigeria and globally. Primary PPH occurs within 24 hours of delivery. Secondary PPH occurs between 24 hours and 6 weeks postpartum. Both are emergencies when bleeding is heavy. UMMA must treat heavy postpartum bleeding as a life-threatening emergency without exception. There is no safe "home management" option for heavy postpartum bleeding — all cases require immediate hospital care. Even moderate bleeding with signs of deterioration must be escalated urgently.

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- Heavy vaginal bleeding at any point after delivery — soaking more than one pad per hour, passing large clots, or bleeding that is clearly more than a normal period
- Any heavy bleeding in the first 24 hours after delivery
- Bleeding with signs of shock — feeling faint, dizziness, cold sweating, pale skin, rapid weak heartbeat, confusion
- Bleeding that has suddenly increased after it was reducing
- Bleeding with severe lower abdominal pain
- Bleeding with foul smell — even if not very heavy (possible uterine infection)
- Bleeding with high fever (possible postpartum sepsis)
- Bleeding in a woman who has not delivered the placenta
- Retained placenta — placenta not delivered within 1 hour of baby
- Woman feels the uterus is not firm and hard (if she can feel her own abdomen)
- Any bleeding that frightens the woman — if the amount feels wrong to her, trust this

**Plain language:**
"Heavy bleeding after delivery is a life-threatening emergency. Women can die from this very quickly — within minutes to hours. Go to the hospital right now. Do not wait, do not see a traditional healer, do not take any herbal medicine. While going, lie down flat, stay warm, and have someone press on the lower abdomen firmly if bleeding is very heavy. Go immediately."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Lochia (normal postpartum discharge) that has changed from pink or brown back to bright red after seeming to reduce
- Bleeding that is heavier than a normal period but not gushing — moderate but persistent
- Bleeding with any fever above 37.5°C in the postpartum period
- Bleeding with tenderness of the uterus on the lower abdomen
- Foul-smelling discharge at any point in the postpartum period — with or without bleeding
- Bleeding with headache or visual disturbance (possible postpartum pre-eclampsia)
- Bleeding in a woman who had any concerns about placenta delivery at birth
- Any unusual or increased vaginal discharge in the first 6 weeks postpartum that the woman is concerned about
- Woman has not been assessed since delivery and has questions about her bleeding pattern

**Plain language:**
"This bleeding needs to be checked today by a nurse or doctor. Go to the PHC or hospital and tell them how many days since you delivered and that your bleeding has increased or changed. Take your delivery card or any paperwork from your birth. Do not wait until tomorrow — postpartum problems can worsen quickly."

---

### HOME CARE — Lochia only (normal postpartum discharge)
**This section applies ONLY to normal lochia — not to heavy or abnormal bleeding**

**Normal lochia pattern:**
- Days 1–4: Bright red blood, similar to a heavy period — lochia rubra
- Days 4–10: Pink or brown discharge — lochia serosa
- Days 10–28 (up to 6 weeks): White or yellowish discharge — lochia alba
- Amount: Should be decreasing over time, not increasing
- Smell: Fleshy or mild — not foul or offensive

**Home care for normal lochia:**
1. Use clean sanitary pads — change every 3–4 hours; do not use internal tampons or local cloths that cannot be kept clean
2. Wash the genital area with clean water from front to back after each pad change — do not use soap inside the vagina
3. Rest as much as possible — physical activity increases lochia flow temporarily
4. Breastfeeding causes the uterus to contract and may cause temporary increase in lochia and cramping — this is normal
5. Report any change in smell, colour (back to bright red), or amount to a nurse at your postpartum visit
6. Attend postpartum care at the PHC — first visit within 24–48 hours if delivered at home, then at 1 week and 6 weeks minimum

**Plain language:**
"Normal blood loss after birth starts heavy and bright red, then becomes lighter and changes to pink, then white, over 4–6 weeks. This is normal. Keep clean, rest, and change pads regularly. But if the blood becomes heavy again, bright red again after it was pink, smells bad, or you feel unwell — go to the clinic or hospital the same day."

---

### MONITOR — With clear safety net
**Conditions:**
- Lochia is following the expected pattern above
- No foul smell
- No fever
- Woman feels well, is feeding her baby, and is not in pain
- No clots larger than a 50 naira coin

**What to watch — every day:**
- Is the bleeding getting heavier instead of lighter?
- Has bright red bleeding returned after it had become pink or brown?
- Is there any foul smell?
- Has fever appeared?
- Are there clots larger than a 50 naira coin?
- Is there any dizziness, fainting, or feeling of weakness?

**Plain language:**
"Watch your bleeding every day. It should be getting lighter over time, not heavier. If it gets heavier, smells bad, or you feel unwell — go to the clinic that day. Do not wait."

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**
- You are soaking more than one pad per hour
- You are passing large clots — larger than your palm or fist
- The bleeding becomes gushing or you feel it increasing rapidly
- You feel faint, dizzy, very weak, or like you might collapse
- You have a fever with heavy or smelly bleeding
- The blood has a foul or very unpleasant smell
- The bleeding had reduced and then suddenly became heavy bright red again
- You have severe pain in the lower abdomen with heavy bleeding
- Your skin feels cold and clammy and your heart is racing

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE
- Postpartum haemorrhage kills more Nigerian women than any other single cause in the postpartum period — early recognition and immediate hospital transfer saves lives
- Uterine massage — firm massage of the lower abdomen where the uterus sits — can temporarily slow heavy bleeding while transporting to hospital; a traditional birth attendant, family member, or the woman herself can do this
- Oxytocin injection given at delivery (active management of third stage of labour) dramatically reduces PPH — every woman should receive this at the time of delivery; ask your birth attendant or nurse about this
- Retained placenta (placenta not delivered within one hour of the baby) is a medical emergency — do not pull on the cord; go to hospital immediately
- Iron-deficiency anaemia from pregnancy makes postpartum haemorrhage more dangerous — the blood is already depleted; all the more reason to seek immediate care for heavy bleeding
- Do not take any herbal preparations to "stop the bleeding" at home — they delay proper treatment and some cause dangerous uterine contractions

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- **Home delivery:** A significant proportion of deliveries in northern Nigeria occur at home or with traditional birth attendants (TBAs). Women who deliver at home are at higher risk of unrecognised PPH because skilled staff are not present to assess blood loss. If you deliver at home and bleeding seems heavy — go to the hospital immediately, even if the TBA says it is normal.
- **Postpartum herbal practices:** In Kano and northern Nigeria, it is common to give postpartum women various herbal preparations — to "clean the womb," "stop bleeding," or "restore strength." Some of these contain compounds that cause uterine contraction and can worsen bleeding; others cause liver toxicity in a woman who is already depleted. Please do not take herbal preparations in the postpartum period and seek the nurse's advice instead.
- **Kadediya practice:** The traditional postpartum practice of kadediya — lying on a heated bed (daba) and consuming large amounts of salt and kanwa (potash) — is associated with severe postpartum hypertension and fluid shifts. If you are observing this practice, monitor carefully for severe headache, visual disturbance, or swelling — these need hospital assessment immediately.
- **Postpartum ANC gap:** Many women in northern Nigeria do not return to the health facility after delivery, especially if they feel well. Postpartum visits at 24 hours, 1 week, and 6 weeks are critical — they are when late PPH, wound infections, and postpartum hypertension are most often detected. Please attend these visits even if you feel you are recovering well.
- **Financial barriers to emergency care:** Cost of transport and hospital care is a real barrier for many families in Kano. The Kano State Government and NHIS provide free or subsidised maternal care at government hospitals — including emergency obstetric care. Do not let cost delay you from going to the hospital for heavy postpartum bleeding. A woman's life is worth more than the cost of transport.
- **Secondary PPH:** Postpartum bleeding that occurs after the first 24 hours — sometimes as late as 3–4 weeks after delivery — is less well recognised by families. A woman who starts bleeding heavily again 2 or 3 weeks after what appeared to be a normal delivery must go to hospital immediately. This can be caused by retained placenta fragments or infection.
