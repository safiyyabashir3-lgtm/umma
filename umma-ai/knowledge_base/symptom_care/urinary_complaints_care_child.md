# Urinary Complaints — Symptom Care File (Child)
**UMMA AI | Maternal & Child Health | Kano/Northern Nigeria Context**
**Target: Children 0–5 years**
**Covers: Painful urination, frequent urination, reduced urination, blood in urine, inability to urinate**

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- Child has not passed urine in 12 hours (or 8 hours for infants under 6 months)
- Child is in severe pain and straining to pass urine but nothing comes out (acute urinary retention)
- Blood is visible in the urine — pink, red, or dark brown/cola-coloured urine
- Child has high fever above 39°C with urinary symptoms — possible kidney infection (pyelonephritis)
- Child has swollen face, puffy eyes, AND reduced or dark urine (possible kidney disease)
- Child is vomiting, cannot keep fluids down, and has urinary symptoms
- Newborn has not passed urine in the first 24 hours of life
- Child is floppy, unconscious, or very difficult to rouse alongside urinary symptoms
- Severe pain in the back (loin) or side alongside urinary symptoms

**Plain language:**
"This is serious. Your child needs to be seen by a doctor right now. A child who cannot pass urine, has blood in their urine, or has high fever with urinary pain can develop kidney damage if not treated quickly. Do not go to a chemist — go to the nearest hospital or PHC immediately."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Child is crying or showing obvious pain when passing urine
- Child is passing urine much more often than normal — going every 15–30 minutes
- Child has fever above 38°C with any urinary complaint
- Child has started wetting themselves again after being previously dry (regression in a toilet-trained child)
- Urine smells very strongly or looks cloudy/murky
- Child who normally passes urine regularly has significantly reduced output for more than 8 hours
- Any urinary complaint in a child under 6 months
- Child has a known kidney or urinary problem and symptoms are worsening
- Girl child with urinary complaints who also has vaginal discharge or soreness (possible combined infection or safeguarding concern)
- Child has recently had a sore throat or skin infection (impetigo) and now has reduced or dark urine — possible post-streptococcal kidney disease

**Plain language:**
"Urinary tract infections in young children are common but can spread to the kidneys quickly if not treated with antibiotics. Your child needs to be seen today. Go to the PHC or clinic, explain the symptoms, and ask for a urine test if possible. Do not give antibiotics from a chemist without seeing a nurse first — the wrong antibiotic will not work."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
**Conditions for home care:**
- Child is older than 6 months
- Pain is mild and child is still passing urine
- No fever, or very low fever under 38°C
- No blood in urine
- Child is drinking well and passing urine at least every 4–6 hours
- Symptoms began less than 24 hours ago
- Child is alert and well in themselves

**Home care instructions:**
1. Push fluids — offer clean water, ORS, or breast milk very frequently; good fluid intake flushes bacteria from the urinary tract and eases pain
2. Keep the genital area clean and dry — clean from front to back after each toilet visit (especially for girls)
3. Do not use soap directly inside the genital area — clean water is sufficient
4. Change wet nappies promptly — leaving a wet nappy causes skin irritation and can worsen infection
5. Do not give any antibiotic without a nurse's prescription — partial antibiotic treatment is dangerous and allows resistant bacteria to grow
6. Paracetamol syrup at the correct child dose can help if there is associated fever or discomfort
7. Go to the clinic the same day or the next morning — urinary complaints almost always need a urine test and, if confirmed as infection, antibiotics

**Plain language:**
"Give your child plenty of fluids and keep the area clean. Paracetamol can help with discomfort. But this needs to be seen by a nurse — today if possible, or first thing tomorrow morning. A urine infection in a child will not clear on its own without the right treatment."

---

### MONITOR — With clear safety net
**Conditions:**
- Child has passed urine recently and it appeared normal
- Child seemed briefly uncomfortable but is now settled
- No fever, no crying with urination, urine looked clear
- Possible dietary cause — eating foods that change urine colour (e.g. large amounts of red palm oil, certain fruits)

**What to watch:**
- Is the child crying when passing urine?
- Is the child passing urine much more or much less than normal?
- Does the urine look pink, red, dark, or very cloudy?
- Does the child develop fever?
- Is the child becoming more distressed or refusing to drink?

**Plain language:**
"Watch your child carefully over the next few hours. Offer plenty of clean fluids. If you notice any of the signs below — especially blood in the urine, fever, or inability to pass urine — go to the clinic immediately. Do not wait overnight if symptoms are getting worse."

---

### UNCERTAIN — When UMMA cannot determine severity
**Plain language:**
"I am not certain from what you have described whether your child's urinary symptoms are mild or serious. Urinary problems in young children — especially girls and babies — can be difficult to identify without a urine test. Please take your child to the PHC today for proper assessment. Do not give antibiotics at home without this assessment."

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**
- Your child has not passed urine for 8 hours (baby) or 12 hours (older child)
- You see blood in the urine — any pink, red, or cola-brown colour
- Your child's face or eyes become puffy alongside urinary symptoms
- Your child has high fever with back or side pain and urinary symptoms
- Your child is straining to urinate but nothing is coming out
- Your child becomes very weak, vomits everything, or is very difficult to rouse

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE
- Urinary tract infections (UTIs) are more common in girls than boys in young children, but boys under 1 year are also at significant risk
- A UTI that is not treated can spread to the kidneys (pyelonephritis) within days — kidney infections can cause permanent kidney scarring
- Blood in the urine is never normal and always needs investigation — do not assume it is caused by food without a nurse confirming this
- Good fluid intake — especially clean water — is the single most important prevention for UTIs in children
- Always wipe girls from front to back — wiping back to front introduces bowel bacteria into the urinary tract
- Do not use scented soap, powder, or disinfectant on a child's genital area — these cause irritation and infection
- Circumcision in male infants reduces the risk of UTI in the first year of life — if your son was circumcised, the area should be kept clean and healing monitored

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- **Antibiotic access:** In Nigeria, antibiotics are widely sold at patent medicine vendors without a prescription. For urinary complaints, families often buy antibiotics directly. UMMA strongly advises against this — the correct antibiotic depends on the type of bacteria causing the infection, which can only be identified by urine culture or clinical assessment. Taking the wrong antibiotic allows the infection to continue and creates resistance.
- **Water quality:** Clean water is essential for urinary health. If your water source is a well, borehole, or shared tap, boil water before giving to children under 5 for drinking. Contaminated water can introduce bacteria into the urinary and digestive system.
- **Schistosomiasis (bilharzia):** In some parts of northern Nigeria near water bodies, blood in the urine in children may be caused by schistosomiasis (a parasitic infection from contact with contaminated water). This is different from a bacterial UTI and requires a different treatment. Always see a nurse for blood in the urine — do not self-treat.
- **Nappy hygiene:** In hot Kano weather, wet nappies left on for long periods cause skin breakdown and allow bacteria to reach the urinary tract. Change nappies frequently and allow the skin to air-dry when possible.
- **Safeguarding:** If a young girl has repeated urinary infections combined with vaginal discharge, bruising, or the mother reports she cannot explain why the child is in pain in the genital area — UMMA will provide safeguarding-appropriate advice to seek care at a hospital where the child can be properly examined. UMMA does not investigate this but will always advise care when the combination of symptoms raises concern.
- **PHC urine testing:** Many PHCs in Kano can perform a basic urine dipstick test. This is quick, free or very low cost, and can confirm whether a urinary infection is present. Ask for this test when you attend.
