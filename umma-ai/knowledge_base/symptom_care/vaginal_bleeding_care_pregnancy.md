# Vaginal Bleeding — Symptom Care File (Pregnancy)
**UMMA AI | Maternal & Child Health | Kano/Northern Nigeria Context**
**Target: Pregnant women, all trimesters**

---

## CRITICAL NOTE FOR UMMA
Vaginal bleeding at any stage of pregnancy is abnormal and must be taken seriously. It ranges from implantation spotting (benign) to placenta praevia haemorrhage and placental abruption — both of which can kill a mother and baby within minutes. UMMA must never reassure a pregnant woman that bleeding is "probably fine." UMMA must always escalate bleeding to at minimum urgent clinic, and to emergency if there is any significant amount of bleeding, pain, or accompanying symptoms. Ectopic pregnancy must always be considered in the first trimester.

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- Heavy vaginal bleeding at any stage of pregnancy — soaking a pad, passing clots, or gushing
- Any vaginal bleeding with severe abdominal pain — possible placental abruption or ectopic pregnancy
- Bleeding in early pregnancy with one-sided pain and dizziness or shoulder-tip pain — possible ruptured ectopic pregnancy (life-threatening)
- Bleeding with signs of shock — pale, cold sweating, faint, rapid weak heartbeat, confusion
- Painless heavy bright red bleeding in the third trimester — possible placenta praevia
- Any bleeding after 28 weeks of pregnancy
- Bleeding with fever and foul smell — possible septic miscarriage
- Bleeding with passage of tissue or products (possible miscarriage)
- Bleeding with reduced or absent fetal movements (after 28 weeks)
- Bleeding that has soaked through clothing
- Any amount of bleeding in a woman with a known placenta praevia diagnosis

**Plain language:**
"Vaginal bleeding in pregnancy is always a warning sign that needs hospital attention immediately. Heavy bleeding can be life-threatening for you and your baby. Go to the hospital right now. Lie down in the car or vehicle on your way. Do not put anything inside the vagina. Do not take any medicine before you are seen. Take your antenatal card. Go now."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Any vaginal bleeding in the first trimester — even light spotting that has not been assessed before
- Light spotting or brown discharge that has been present for more than 24 hours
- Bleeding after sexual intercourse — even if light (possible cervical ectropion or placenta praevia)
- Bleeding in a woman who has had a previous miscarriage or ectopic pregnancy
- Any bleeding in a woman who has not had an ultrasound to confirm intrauterine pregnancy
- Light bleeding with cramping or lower abdominal tightening
- Spotting in a woman with multiple pregnancy
- Any new bleeding in a woman who was previously told she has a low-lying placenta

**Plain language:**
"Any bleeding in pregnancy must be checked today — even if it is light or has stopped. You need an examination and possibly an ultrasound scan. Go to the nearest ANC clinic or hospital and take your antenatal card. Tell the nurse exactly when the bleeding started, how much there was, what colour it was, and whether you have any pain."

---

### HOME CARE
**There is no safe home management for active vaginal bleeding in pregnancy.**

If a woman is waiting for transport to hospital, the following supportive steps are appropriate:
1. Lie down and rest — do not walk around unnecessarily
2. Use a clean pad to monitor the amount of bleeding — count how many pads you are soaking and keep the used pads to show the nurse (helps estimate blood loss)
3. Do not insert anything into the vagina — no cloth, no traditional preparation
4. Do not take any herbal or traditional remedy to stop the bleeding
5. Paracetamol can be taken for pain while waiting — no other medicine
6. Have someone stay with you and monitor your condition
7. If you feel faint, lie flat with your feet slightly raised
8. Get to hospital as quickly as possible — do not wait to see if bleeding stops

**Plain language:**
"While you wait for transport: lie down, use a clean pad, and have someone stay with you. Count the pads you soak through. Do not put anything inside your vagina. Do not take herbal medicine. Get to the hospital as fast as you can. Do not wait to see if the bleeding stops on its own."

---

### MONITOR
**Monitoring is NOT appropriate for active vaginal bleeding in pregnancy.**

The only situation where brief monitoring may occur is:
- Spotting that has completely stopped (not even on wiping), with no pain, in a woman who has already been assessed today by a nurse who confirmed no emergency cause, and who can reach the hospital within 30 minutes if symptoms recur.

Even in this case — if spotting returns, go immediately.

---

### UNCERTAIN — When UMMA cannot determine severity
**Plain language:**
"Any vaginal bleeding in pregnancy is something I always recommend getting checked at the clinic or hospital — even if I am not sure how serious it is. The only safe approach is to be examined by a nurse or doctor today. Please go now and take your antenatal card."

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**
- The bleeding becomes heavier or you are soaking a pad per hour
- You pass any clots or tissue
- You develop pain on one side of the lower abdomen — especially in early pregnancy
- You feel faint, dizzy, or very weak
- Your heart is racing and you feel cold and sweaty
- You develop a fever alongside the bleeding
- The bleeding has a foul or unpleasant smell
- Your baby's movements reduce or stop after 28 weeks
- Any amount of bleeding after 28 weeks

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE
- Ectopic pregnancy — a pregnancy growing outside the uterus, usually in the fallopian tube — is life-threatening and causes internal bleeding that can kill within hours; symptoms include one-sided pain, light bleeding, dizziness, and shoulder-tip pain; any woman with early pregnancy and these symptoms must go to hospital immediately
- Miscarriage (loss of the baby before 24 weeks) is the most common cause of first trimester bleeding — it is not the woman's fault and cannot be prevented with bed rest at home once it has started; it requires medical assessment and sometimes treatment
- Placenta praevia — when the placenta covers the cervix — causes painless bright red bleeding and is diagnosed on ultrasound; all women with this diagnosis must go to hospital for any bleeding immediately
- Placental abruption — when the placenta separates from the wall of the uterus — causes painful bleeding and is a life-threatening emergency for both mother and baby
- All pregnant women should know their blood group — in emergency bleeding, transfusion may be needed and blood group is critical information

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- **Miscarriage and stigma:** In many communities in Nigeria, miscarriage is associated with shame, blame, or spiritual causes. This can lead women to delay seeking care or to seek traditional management rather than hospital care. UMMA wants every woman to know: miscarriage is a medical event that is not caused by anything you did wrong. It requires medical care to ensure the womb is completely empty and to prevent infection. Please go to the hospital.
- **Incomplete miscarriage:** A common and preventable cause of maternal death in Nigeria is incomplete miscarriage — where some of the pregnancy tissue remains in the uterus and causes infection or ongoing bleeding. This requires treatment (manual vacuum aspiration or medication) at a health facility. If you have had a miscarriage at home, please go to the PHC or hospital to be assessed even if the bleeding has slowed.
- **Herbal abortifacients:** UMMA is aware that some women use herbal preparations or other methods to end an unwanted pregnancy outside the formal health system. These methods are associated with severe bleeding, uterine perforation, sepsis, and death. If a woman has used any substance to end a pregnancy and has bleeding — this is a medical emergency. She should go to the hospital immediately. She will receive care without judgment.
- **Traditional birth attendants and bleeding:** TBAs in northern Nigeria are important members of the birth community. However, heavy vaginal bleeding in pregnancy is beyond the scope of what any TBA can safely manage at home. If a TBA is present and a woman is bleeding heavily — she must be transported to hospital immediately.
- **ANC and ultrasound:** Ultrasound in early pregnancy can confirm whether the pregnancy is in the uterus (ruling out ectopic) and identify placenta position. This is available at government hospitals and many PHCs in Kano. Attend ANC early — ideally before 12 weeks — to have this assessment.
- **Blood transfusion:** Government hospitals in Kano — including AKTH and Murtala Muhammad Specialist Hospital — have blood banks. Severe bleeding from obstetric haemorrhage can be treated with transfusion. Getting to hospital quickly is what makes the difference.
