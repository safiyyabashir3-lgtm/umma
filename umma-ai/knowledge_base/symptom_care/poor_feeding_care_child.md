# Poor Feeding — Symptom Care File (Child)
**UMMA AI | Maternal & Child Health | Kano/Northern Nigeria Context**
**Target: Children 0–5 years, with emphasis on infants 0–12 months**

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- Child is completely unable to feed or drink — refuses breast, bottle, cup, and cannot swallow
- Child is unconscious, floppy, or extremely difficult to rouse
- Child has sunken eyes, very dry mouth, no tears when crying, and skin that stays up like a tent when pinched (severe dehydration)
- Newborn under 7 days who has not fed in 4 hours and cannot be woken
- Child is vomiting everything immediately after every feed
- Child has blood in vomit or stool alongside poor feeding
- Child has a distended (swollen, hard) abdomen and is not feeding
- Child has a seizure alongside poor feeding
- Child is breathing very fast or working hard to breathe and is not feeding

**Plain language:**
"Your child is too sick to feed and this is very serious. They need hospital care right now. A child who cannot drink at all can become critically ill within hours — especially a baby. Go to the nearest hospital immediately. If you are breastfeeding, do not stop trying on the way — even small drops help. But go now."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Child has been feeding significantly less than normal for more than 24 hours
- Newborn under 4 weeks feeding poorly — taking less than 8 feeds in 24 hours, very short feeds, or falling asleep immediately at the breast without swallowing
- Child has fever above 38°C alongside poor feeding
- Child has not passed urine in 8 hours (infant) or 12 hours (older child)
- Child has vomited more than twice and is feeding poorly
- Child is losing weight visibly or clothes that used to fit are now loose
- Child has painful mouth sores, white patches in the mouth (thrush), or visible tooth/gum problem that is preventing feeding
- Child who was previously feeding well suddenly refuses all food and drink
- Child under 6 months who has dropped more than one feed in 24 hours without explanation

**Plain language:**
"Your child needs to be seen today. Poor feeding in young children — especially babies — can lead to dangerous dehydration and malnutrition very quickly. Go to the PHC or clinic this morning or afternoon. Tell the nurse how long the feeding problem has lasted, how many feeds the child has had in the last 24 hours, and whether they are passing urine normally."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
**Conditions for home care:**
- Child is older than 4 weeks
- Feeding is reduced but child is still taking some feeds — not completely refusing
- Child is still passing urine at least every 6–8 hours
- Child is alert when awake — not floppy or unresponsive
- No fever above 38°C, or fever is low and child is otherwise well
- No vomiting, or vomited only once
- No signs of dehydration
- Reduced feeding began less than 24 hours ago

**Home care instructions:**

*For breastfed infants:*
1. Offer the breast more frequently — every 1–2 hours — even if the baby fed recently
2. Try different positions — some babies feed better in certain positions
3. Feed in a quiet, calm place away from noise and distraction
4. Express breast milk and offer by cup or spoon if baby is refusing the breast directly
5. Do not give water, herbal tea, or glucose water — breast milk is sufficient
6. Do not give formula without a nurse's advice — it can reduce breast milk supply

*For older children (6 months and above):*
1. Offer small amounts of food and fluid frequently — every 30–60 minutes — rather than large meals
2. Offer favourite foods in small portions to encourage interest
3. If child has a sore throat or mouth pain, offer softer foods — pap, soft rice, yoghurt
4. Offer ORS or clean water in small sips frequently
5. Do not force-feed — forced feeding increases distress and refusal
6. Avoid strong-tasting medicines or herbs near mealtimes

*For all children:*
- If the child has a fever, treat with paracetamol syrup (correct child dose on the packet) — fever itself suppresses appetite
- Keep the child cool and comfortable — a hot, uncomfortable child will not feed well
- Do not give local herbal mixtures to stimulate appetite — they are not safe

**Plain language:**
"Offer food or breast milk more often in small amounts. Keep the child comfortable. If they have fever, give paracetamol. Watch carefully — if they stop passing urine, become very weak, or refuse everything — go to the clinic today. Do not wait more than 24 hours if things are not improving."

### MONITOR — With clear safety net
**Conditions:**
- Mild reduction in feeding that started today
- Child is alert, playing between feeding attempts
- Child is passing urine normally
- No fever or other symptoms
- Child is teething (common cause of feeding disruption at 4–12 months)
- Child is recovering from a recent mild illness and appetite is slowly returning

**What to watch:**
- Is the child passing urine at least every 6–8 hours?
- Is the child becoming weaker, less alert, or more difficult to rouse?
- Is the child's mouth, lips, or eyes becoming dry?
- Is the feeding refusal getting worse, not better?
- Is the child developing fever?
- Is the child losing interest in everything — not just food?

**Plain language:**
"Watch your child closely over the next few hours. Offer food and fluids gently and regularly. Count their wet nappies or times they pass urine. If anything changes or gets worse — go to the clinic. Do not wait longer than 24 hours even if you are unsure."

### UNCERTAIN — When UMMA cannot determine severity
**Plain language:**
"I am not sure from what you have described whether your child's feeding problem is mild or serious. In young children — especially babies — poor feeding can become dangerous quickly. Please take your child to the PHC or clinic today so a nurse can weigh them, check for dehydration, and find out why they are not feeding well."

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**
- Your child completely refuses to drink or swallow anything
- Your child has not passed urine in 8 hours (baby) or 12 hours (older child)
- Your child's eyes become sunken or mouth becomes very dry
- The skin on your child's belly stays up when you pinch and release it
- Your child becomes floppy, very weak, or very difficult to wake
- Your newborn (under 4 weeks) has not fed in 4 hours and cannot be woken

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE
- Dehydration is the most dangerous immediate consequence of poor feeding in young children — learn the signs: sunken eyes, dry mouth, no tears, reduced urine, and skin that does not bounce back when pinched
- ORS (oral rehydration salts) is the correct treatment when a child is dehydrated — it is available free or cheaply at all PHCs and most pharmacies; give by spoon or cup in small sips if the child is reluctant
- Weight loss, poor growth, and frequent illness can be signs of underlying malnutrition — take your child for regular growth monitoring at the PHC under-5 clinic
- Mouth thrush (white patches on the tongue and inner cheeks) is a common cause of feeding refusal in infants and is easily treated with antifungal medicine from the nurse
- Teething causes discomfort and temporary feeding reduction — this is normal; it resolves on its own and does not require medicine

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- **Pre-lacteal feeds and mixed feeding:** In many parts of northern Nigeria, newborns are given water, sugar water, cow's milk, or herbal preparations before or alongside breastfeeding. This reduces a baby's interest in breastfeeding, introduces infection, and fills the stomach with non-nutritious fluid. It is a significant cause of poor feeding in newborns. Breast milk alone — including colostrum — is all a newborn needs.
- **Appetite stimulant herbs:** Many families use local herbs or patent medicine "tonics" to stimulate a child's appetite. Some of these contain alcohol, steroids, or substances harmful to the liver. They do not address the true cause of poor feeding. Please bring your child to the nurse rather than trying appetite herbs.
- **Malnutrition risk:** Northern Nigeria has high rates of childhood malnutrition. A child who is feeding poorly for more than a few days is at risk of kwashiorkor or marasmus. PHCs provide free nutritional assessment and therapeutic feeding support — go early, not late.
- **Community health workers:** CHEWS and community health workers in Kano can visit homes and assist with breastfeeding support. If you are struggling with breastfeeding, ask at your PHC for a referral to a breastfeeding support visit.
- **Ramadan and breastfeeding:** Breastfeeding mothers who fast during Ramadan should know that fasting can reduce breast milk volume. If your baby is feeding poorly during this time, increase fluid intake when breaking fast and before Suhoor. Seek nurse advice if baby's feeding does not improve.
- **Heat and appetite:** In Kano's high heat, children's appetites naturally reduce. However, fluid needs increase. Prioritise fluids — ORS, water, breast milk — over solid food if the child is not eating in hot weather.
