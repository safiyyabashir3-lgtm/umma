# UMMA AI — Symptom Care File
# Symptom: VOMITING IN A CHILD (under 5 years)
# Context: Nigeria / Northern Nigeria (Kano)

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- Child is vomiting and has a severe headache and stiff neck (possible meningitis)
- Vomit is green (bile-coloured) — especially in a young baby — could be intestinal blockage
- Vomit contains blood or material that looks like dark coffee grounds
- Child is unconscious, very drowsy, or cannot be woken
- Child is showing signs of severe dehydration — very dry mouth, sunken eyes, no urine, skin stays pinched up
- Vomiting after a head injury or fall
- Vomiting with a bulging fontanelle (the soft spot on a baby's head)
- Child under 2 months with repeated vomiting
- Vomiting with seizures
- Child's belly is very swollen and hard with vomiting
- Vomiting so forceful it shoots across the room (projectile vomiting) in a baby under 3 months

**Plain language:**
"This is a serious situation and your child needs hospital care immediately. Do not wait. On the way to hospital, do not force the child to eat or drink if they are vomiting continuously. Keep them on their side if they are lying down, to prevent choking on vomit."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Child has been vomiting repeatedly for more than 12 hours
- Child cannot keep any fluids down for more than 6 hours
- Vomiting with high fever
- Vomiting with diarrhoea — risk of combined fluid loss
- Signs of mild dehydration — dry mouth, fewer tears, fewer wet nappies, restless
- Child under 6 months with repeated vomiting (more than 3 times)
- Vomiting with ear pain, severe cough, or belly pain
- Child has not passed urine in 6 hours

**Plain language:**
"Your child needs to be seen today. Please go to your nearest clinic or Primary Health Centre. Try to give very small amounts of ORS — a teaspoon every few minutes — on the way. Do not give large amounts at once as it may cause more vomiting."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
**Conditions for home care:**
- Child is over 6 months old
- Vomiting is mild — fewer than 3 episodes in the last few hours
- Child is alert and responsive
- Child is still passing urine
- No blood, no green colour in vomit
- No severe belly pain, no headache with stiff neck
- No fever, or mild fever only
- Child vomited after a coughing fit (common in whooping cough or chest infection) rather than repeatedly out of nowhere

**Home care instructions:**
1. **Rest the stomach** — do not offer food or milk for 30–60 minutes after vomiting. Let the stomach settle.
2. **Reintroduce fluids very slowly** — start with ORS. Give a teaspoon every 2–3 minutes. If this stays down for 20 minutes, slowly increase the amount. Do not rush.
3. **Breastfed babies** — offer short, frequent breastfeeds rather than one long feed. The breast can be offered every 30 minutes in small amounts.
4. **Older children** — once fluids are tolerated, introduce small amounts of plain food: rice, yam, plain porridge. Avoid rich, fatty, or spicy food.
5. **Do not give anti-vomiting medicines** without medical advice — some are not safe for young children.
6. **Keep the child resting** — lying quietly helps nausea.
7. **Position carefully** — if the child is sleepy and vomiting, keep them on their side so vomit does not block the airway.

**Plain language:**
"Your child's vomiting seems mild right now. Rest their stomach, then give very small sips of ORS every few minutes. Do not rush to give food. Watch them closely. If vomiting gets worse or they cannot keep any fluids down, go to the clinic."

---

### MONITOR — With clear safety net
**Conditions:**
- Mild vomiting, improving or not worsening
- Child had one or two episodes and now seems better
- Alert, interested in surroundings
- Able to accept small amounts of fluids

**What to watch:**
- Vomiting increasing in frequency
- Child becoming drowsy or limp
- Signs of dehydration (dry mouth, no tears, no urine)
- Blood or green colour in vomit
- Severe belly pain developing
- Headache with stiff neck
- Fever rising

**Plain language:**
"Your child seems okay right now but watch them closely for the next few hours. Offer fluids slowly and check on them often. Do not leave them alone if they are very young. If anything from the safety net list happens, go to hospital immediately."

---

### UNCERTAIN — When UMMA cannot determine severity
**Plain language:**
"I am not sure how serious your child's vomiting is from what you have told me. To be safe, please take them to the nearest health facility today. In the meantime, offer very small sips of ORS if they can swallow, and keep them lying on their side."

---

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**

- Vomit is green or contains blood
- Your child cannot keep even small sips of water or ORS down
- Your child has not passed urine in 8 hours
- Your child's eyes become sunken or mouth becomes very dry
- Your child becomes very drowsy or limp
- Your child has a very stiff neck with the vomiting
- Vomiting began after a head injury or fall
- Your child's belly becomes swollen and hard
- Your child has a seizure
- You feel something is seriously wrong

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE

- **ORS for fluid replacement** — ORS is the safest way to replace fluids in a vomiting child. Give it slowly — a teaspoon at a time. Large amounts at once will trigger more vomiting.
- **Do not give anti-vomiting medications** to young children without medical advice. Promethazine (Phenergan) in particular is not recommended in young children as it can cause serious side effects.
- **Do not give solid food when vomiting actively** — wait until vomiting has stopped for at least 30–60 minutes before offering food.
- **Safe position** — if a young child is vomiting and sleepy, lay them on their side (recovery position) to prevent choking.
- **Dehydration risk** — vomiting combined with diarrhoea causes very rapid fluid loss. Always start ORS early.

---

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE

**Patent medicine vendors (PMVs):**
PMVs may offer injection-based anti-emetics (anti-vomiting drugs) for children. These are not recommended without proper medical assessment. ORS sachets from the PMV are appropriate. For anything beyond simple rehydration, go to the PHC.

**Herbal remedies:**
Some families give herbal teas or stomach preparations to children who are vomiting. Some of these can worsen vomiting, irritate the stomach, or be toxic to young children. UMMA respectfully advises against this. If any herbal preparation has been given, please tell the health worker at the clinic.

**Heat:**
In northern Nigeria's heat, a vomiting child loses fluids faster than in cooler climates. Start ORS early and do not wait to see if the child "gets better on their own."

**Transport:**
If the child is actively vomiting during transport, keep them sitting up or slightly forward, or on their side. Do not lay them flat on their back during vomiting.

**Food beliefs:**
Some families believe giving fluids to a vomiting child makes vomiting worse. The opposite is true — withholding fluids quickly leads to dehydration, which is dangerous. Small, slow sips of ORS are safe and important.

**Nearest PHC:**
PHC workers trained in IMCI can assess vomiting in a child and identify danger signs that require referral. If in doubt, always go.
