# Ear Pain / Ear Discharge — Symptom Care File (Child)
**UMMA AI | Maternal & Child Health | Kano/Northern Nigeria Context**
**Target: Children 0–5 years**

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- Child has ear pain or discharge alongside stiff neck, severe headache, or sensitivity to light (possible meningitis)
- Child is unconscious or very difficult to rouse
- Child has a seizure (convulsion) alongside ear symptoms
- There is swelling, redness, and tenderness behind the ear (over the mastoid bone) — the bony area just behind the ear lobe
- Child has sudden facial weakness on one side (drooping of mouth or eye)
- Child had a head injury or object inserted into the ear and now has severe pain or hearing loss
- Newborn (under 2 months) with any ear discharge

**Plain language:**
"These signs are very serious. Your child needs to go to a hospital with a doctor right now. Do not wait, do not go to a chemist first. If you see stiff neck, very bad headache, or swelling behind the ear — this is an emergency. Go immediately."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Ear is producing discharge (pus or fluid) of any kind
- Child has had ear pain for more than 2 days
- Child has fever above 38°C alongside ear pain
- Child is pulling at or hitting their ear repeatedly and is very distressed (especially infants who cannot report pain)
- Child is under 6 months with suspected ear pain
- Child has reduced hearing on one or both sides
- Child has recently had measles — ear infections are a common complication
- Ear discharge in a child who has had ear problems before

**Plain language:**
"Ear infections need antibiotic treatment from a nurse or doctor — they will not get better on their own. Please take your child to the PHC or clinic today. Tell the nurse your child has pain in the ear [or discharge from the ear] and for how many days. Do not put anything inside the ear — no oil, no cotton, no herbal drops."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
**Conditions for home care:**
- Child is older than 6 months
- Pain is mild and just started (less than 24 hours)
- No discharge from the ear
- No fever above 38°C
- No swelling behind the ear
- Child is still eating, drinking, and is alert
- You can get to a clinic the next morning if the pain does not improve

**Home care instructions:**
1. Give paracetamol syrup at the correct child dose on the packet — this helps with pain and fever
2. Do not put oil, drops, cotton buds, or any object into the ear — this can push bacteria deeper or damage the ear drum
3. Keep the ear dry — do not allow water to enter the ear during bathing
4. If the child is breastfeeding, continue — breastfeeding reduces the risk of ear infections getting worse
5. Let the child sleep with the sore ear facing up if possible (not pressed down onto the pillow)
6. Monitor every few hours — if pain is not improving within 24 hours, go to the clinic

**Plain language:**
"For now, give your child paracetamol for pain and keep the ear dry and clean. Do not put anything inside the ear. If the pain does not improve by tomorrow morning, or if any discharge appears, take your child to the PHC. Ear infections usually need antibiotics — this is only to manage tonight safely."

---

### MONITOR — With clear safety net
**Conditions:**
- Child has rubbed or touched ear once or twice but is not in obvious distress
- No fever, no discharge, no swelling, no hearing changes
- Child is feeding and behaving normally
- Possible teething pain (common cause of ear-pulling in infants 4–12 months)

**What to watch:**
- Does the child keep pulling at the ear or seem to be in pain?
- Does any discharge appear from the ear?
- Does the child develop fever?
- Does the child become more distressed or start crying in a high-pitched or unusual way?
- Does the skin behind the ear become swollen or red?

**Plain language:**
"Watch your child for the next few hours. Sometimes babies pull at their ears when teething and there is no ear infection. But if you see any of the signs below, do not wait — go to the clinic."

---

### UNCERTAIN — When UMMA cannot determine severity
**Plain language:**
"I am not sure whether your child has an ear infection or something else. Ear infections in young children can be difficult to identify without proper examination. Please take your child to the nearest PHC today so a nurse can look in the ear properly. Do not put anything in the ear until then."

---

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**
- Your child has a stiff neck, severe headache, or cannot tolerate light alongside ear symptoms
- Your child has a convulsion (seizure)
- There is swelling or redness of the hard bony area behind the ear
- Your child's face droops or one eye cannot close properly
- Your child becomes unconscious or very difficult to rouse
- Discharge from the ear in a newborn under 2 months

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE
- Ear infections (otitis media) are one of the most common childhood illnesses in children under 5 — they nearly always require antibiotics prescribed by a health worker
- Never insert cotton buds, sticks, matchsticks, or any other object into a child's ear — this is a very common cause of ear injury and infection in Nigeria
- Discharge from the ear means the eardrum may have ruptured — this requires prompt medical attention even if the child seems less pained afterward (pain sometimes reduces when the drum bursts)
- Untreated or repeated ear infections can cause permanent hearing loss — hearing loss in early childhood affects speech and learning
- Children who were not breastfed, attend crowded settings, or were exposed to smoke have higher risk of ear infections

---

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- **Ear drops and oil:** In many homes, it is common practice to put groundnut oil, breast milk, or herbal drops in a child's ear for pain. UMMA understands this is a familiar remedy. However, anything placed inside the ear can introduce infection, block the canal, or worsen an existing problem. Please keep the ear dry and clean and let the nurse examine it first.
- **Cotton buds:** These are widely sold and used to clean children's ears in Nigeria. They should never be inserted into the ear canal — they push wax and bacteria further in and can injure the ear drum.
- **Measles and ear infections:** After a measles illness, children are at high risk of ear infection. If your child has recently recovered from measles and is now pulling at their ear or has discharge — see a nurse urgently.
- **Hearing concerns:** If you have noticed your child is not responding to sounds normally, not turning toward voices, or has delayed speech — mention this to the nurse at every PHC visit. Early identification of hearing loss is important.
- **PHC services:** PHC nurses are trained to diagnose and treat common ear infections with antibiotics. You do not need to go to a specialist hospital for a first episode of ear infection — start at the PHC.
- **PMV risk:** Do not buy antibiotic ear drops from a patent medicine vendor without a prescription. The wrong antibiotic — or no antibiotic — can allow the infection to spread.
