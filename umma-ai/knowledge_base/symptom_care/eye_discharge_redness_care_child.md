# Eye Discharge / Eye Redness — Symptom Care File (Child)
**UMMA AI | Maternal & Child Health | Kano/Northern Nigeria Context**
**Target: Children 0–5 years**

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- Newborn (0–28 days) with any eye discharge — this must always be treated as an emergency
- Child has eye symptoms alongside stiff neck, severe headache, or high fever (possible meningitis)
- Eye is protruding or bulging forward
- Child has sudden loss of vision or reports they cannot see
- Chemical or substance has entered the eye — wash immediately with clean water for 10–15 minutes, then go to hospital
- Deep cut or penetrating injury to the eye or eyelid
- Child's eye is extremely painful, very swollen shut, or the cornea (clear part of the eye) appears cloudy or white
- Child had measles recently and now has severe eye discharge or cannot open the eye (risk of corneal scarring and blindness)

**Plain language:**
"Eye problems in newborns and serious eye injuries are emergencies. Please go to the hospital right now. A newborn with discharge from the eye needs antibiotic eye treatment immediately — do not wait even one day. If your child has recently had measles, eye problems must be seen urgently today."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Child has yellow or green discharge from one or both eyes
- Eye discharge is present in a child under 2 months
- Child's eyelid is very swollen or red (not just the white of the eye — the eyelid itself)
- Child has had eye redness and discharge for more than 3 days without improving
- Child has a fever above 38°C alongside eye symptoms
- Child is in significant pain or light sensitivity
- White dot or spot visible on the cornea (clear part of the eye)
- Child has had measles in the past 4 weeks and now has eye redness or discharge
- Discharge is continuous and heavy — not just mild crusting in the morning

**Plain language:**
"Your child's eye needs to be looked at by a nurse or doctor today. Eye infections can spread quickly and, if untreated, can damage vision permanently. Go to the PHC or clinic this morning or afternoon. Do not put breast milk, groundnut oil, or any herbal substance in the eye — these can introduce more infection."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
**Conditions for home care:**
- Child is older than 2 months
- Discharge is mild — only slight crusting at the inner corner of the eye, especially in the morning
- White of the eye is only mildly pink, not deeply red
- No fever, no eyelid swelling, no pain
- No measles history in the past month
- Child can open the eye normally
- No change in vision (as far as you can tell)

**Home care instructions:**
1. Clean the eye gently with a clean cloth or clean cotton wool dampened with clean, previously boiled and cooled water
2. Wipe from the inner corner (near the nose) outward — never inward
3. Use a fresh piece of cloth or cotton for each wipe — do not reuse the same piece
4. Use separate cloths for each eye if both eyes are affected
5. Wash your hands before and after touching the child's eye
6. Do not share towels or cloths used for the child's face with other family members
7. Keep the child away from smoke and dust
8. If the discharge is not improving within 24–48 hours or becomes worse — go to the clinic
9. Do not apply kohl (tiro/tiro-laushi), breast milk, oil, or any drops to the eye

**Plain language:**
"You can clean the eye gently at home with clean water and a fresh cloth. Wipe from the nose outward. Wash your hands well before and after. Watch the eye closely — if it is not getting better by tomorrow, or gets worse at any time, go to the clinic. Do not put anything in the eye."

---

### MONITOR — With clear safety net
**Conditions:**
- Very mild eye crusting only in the morning that clears during the day
- No redness, no discharge during the day, no swelling
- Child older than 2 months, otherwise well
- Possibly a blocked tear duct in infant (common in infants under 6 months — causes watery or mild crusty eye without infection)

**What to watch:**
- Is the discharge becoming yellow or green?
- Is the white of the eye becoming more red?
- Is the eyelid swelling or becoming red?
- Is the child rubbing the eye more, or avoiding light?
- Is there any fever?

**Plain language:**
"This sounds like it may be mild. Watch the eye for the next 24 hours. If the discharge stays clear and only mild, and your child is well — continue cleaning gently. But if discharge becomes yellow or green, or anything gets worse — go to the clinic the same day."

---

### UNCERTAIN — When UMMA cannot determine severity
**Plain language:**
"I am not sure how serious your child's eye problem is from what you have described. Eye problems can worsen quickly and affect vision permanently if untreated. Please take your child to the PHC or clinic today so a nurse can look at the eye properly."

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**
- Your child is a newborn (0–28 days) — any eye discharge is an emergency
- The eye becomes very painful, very swollen, or cannot open
- The clear part of the eye (cornea) becomes cloudy or has a white spot
- Your child cannot see clearly or tells you their vision has changed
- The eye begins to bulge forward
- Chemical or object enters the eye
- Your child recently had measles and now has eye discharge or cannot open the eye

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE
- Newborn eye infection (ophthalmia neonatorum) can be caused by gonorrhoea or chlamydia passed during delivery — it can cause permanent blindness if not treated within hours to days. This is why every newborn eye discharge is an emergency.
- Conjunctivitis (pink eye) is common in children and is usually treated with antibiotic eye drops prescribed by a nurse — do not use adult eye drops or unapproved preparations
- Eye infections spread easily — wash hands frequently, do not share face cloths or towels
- Vitamin A deficiency causes eye problems and blindness in children — ensure your child receives the free Vitamin A supplements given at PHCs every 6 months for children 6 months to 5 years
- Measles causes severe eye damage — if your child is not vaccinated, go to the PHC for measles vaccine today

---

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- **Kohl (tiro/surma):** Applying kohl (tiro) to newborn and infant eyes is a deeply rooted tradition in Kano and northern Nigeria, practised as a blessing and protection. UMMA respects this tradition and the love behind it. However, kohl has been found to contain high levels of lead, which enters the bloodstream through the eye and can cause brain damage and poisoning in young children. Many health authorities strongly advise against applying kohl to infants and young children. If you wish to follow this tradition, please discuss it with your nurse or doctor.
- **Breast milk in the eye:** Putting breast milk in a child's eye is a common home remedy. While breast milk has some immune properties, putting it in an infected eye can introduce bacteria and worsen the infection. Please do not use breast milk as eye drops.
- **Harmattan and dust:** During harmattan season, dusty air causes eye irritation and redness that is not infection. Keep children's faces covered in dusty conditions and rinse eyes with clean water if irritated.
- **Measles and vision:** Vitamin A deficiency combined with measles is a leading cause of childhood blindness in northern Nigeria. If your child has measles, seek care immediately and ensure they receive Vitamin A supplementation as part of their treatment — ask for it at the PHC.
- **Patent medicine vendors:** Do not buy eye drops from a PMV for a child without a nurse's prescription. Many adult eye drops are too strong for children and some contain steroids that damage the eye if used for infection.
- **PHC eye care:** PHCs can diagnose and treat common childhood eye infections. Referral to an eye specialist is only needed for serious or complicated cases.
