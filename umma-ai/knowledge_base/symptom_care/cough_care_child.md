# Cough — Symptom Care File (Child)
**UMMA AI | Maternal & Child Health | Kano/Northern Nigeria Context**
**Target: Children 0–5 years**

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- Child is breathing very fast (more than 60 breaths/minute in infant under 2 months; more than 50 in child 2–12 months; more than 40 in child 1–5 years)
- Child's chest is caving in with each breath (chest indrawing — the skin between or below the ribs pulls inward)
- Child has blue or grey lips, tongue, or fingertips (cyanosis)
- Child is making a high-pitched crowing noise when breathing in (stridor)
- Child is unconscious, floppy, or cannot be roused
- Child is choking or suddenly cannot breathe after possible swallowing of object
- Child has whooping cough — long coughing fits ending with a "whoop" or vomiting, especially in unvaccinated child
- Newborn (0–2 months) with any cough

**Plain language:**
"This is an emergency. Your child is struggling to breathe and needs hospital care right now. Do not wait — go immediately to the nearest hospital with a children's ward. If you have nothing else, go to the nearest PHC and tell them your child cannot breathe properly — they will help you get further care. Do not give any cough medicine before you go."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Cough has lasted more than 14 days (possible TB, whooping cough, or other serious cause)
- Child has fever above 38.5°C with cough
- Child is breathing faster than normal but not in emergency range
- Child is not drinking or feeding well due to cough
- Child is under 6 months with cough of any kind
- Cough is productive with green, yellow, or blood-streaked mucus
- Child has had measles in the last 4 weeks and now has cough (risk of measles pneumonia)
- Child is wheezing (whistling sound when breathing out)
- Child appears very tired, not playing, or is refusing feeds

**Plain language:**
"Your child needs to be seen today. This type of cough needs a proper check by a nurse or doctor — they will listen to the chest and decide if treatment is needed. Go to your nearest PHC or clinic today and explain how long the cough has lasted and if there is fever. Do not give cough syrup from a chemist before seeing a nurse — some cough medicines are not safe for young children."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
**Conditions for home care:**
- Child is older than 6 months
- Cough is mild and child is still feeding, playing, and alert
- No fast breathing, no chest indrawing, no blue lips
- No fever, or low fever under 38°C
- Cough has lasted fewer than 7 days
- Child is passing urine normally (not dehydrated)
- Child has been vaccinated (including measles vaccine)

**Home care instructions:**
1. Keep the child in a warm, well-ventilated room — avoid dusty or smoky air (including cooking fire smoke)
2. If breastfeeding, feed more often — breast milk protects the airway and keeps the child hydrated
3. For children over 1 year: warm water, warm soup, or warm diluted juice can soothe the throat
4. Do not give honey to children under 1 year — it is dangerous for infants
5. Keep the child's head slightly elevated during sleep to ease breathing
6. Do not give adult cough medicines or syrups — they are not safe for young children
7. Do not give antihistamines (like "phenergan") without a prescription
8. Saline (salt water) nose drops can help clear a blocked nose if available — one small pinch of clean salt dissolved in half a cup of clean boiled water; use a clean dropper
9. Paracetamol syrup (correct child dose on the packet) if child also has fever

**Plain language:**
"You can care for your child at home for now. Keep them warm, away from smoke and dust, and give them fluids often — breast milk if you are still feeding. Do not give cough syrup from the chemist without seeing a nurse first. Check on your child every few hours and watch for any of the danger signs below."

---

### MONITOR — With clear safety net
**Conditions:**
- Very mild dry cough, just started today
- Child is completely well otherwise — playing, feeding, alert
- No fever, no fast breathing, no other symptoms

**What to watch:**
- Is the breathing getting faster?
- Is the chest pulling in with each breath?
- Are the lips or fingers becoming bluish?
- Is the child refusing to feed or drink?
- Is the cough getting worse or more frequent?
- Does the child develop fever?

**Plain language:**
"Your child's cough sounds mild right now. Watch them closely over the next 24 hours. If anything changes — faster breathing, high fever, trouble feeding, or the signs below — go to the clinic without delay."

---

### UNCERTAIN — When UMMA cannot determine severity
**Plain language:**
"I am not able to tell from what you have described whether your child's cough is mild or serious. Coughs in young children can become serious quickly, especially in babies under 1 year. Please take your child to the nearest PHC or clinic today so a nurse can listen to their chest and advise you properly."

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**
- Your child's chest is caving in or pulling between the ribs when they breathe
- Your child's lips or fingertips turn blue or grey
- Your child is breathing very fast or making a crowing/grunting noise
- Your child becomes floppy, unconscious, or very difficult to wake
- Your child is choking or cannot breathe suddenly
- A newborn (0–2 months) develops any cough
- The cough has lasted more than 14 days

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE
- Fast breathing and chest indrawing are the most important warning signs of pneumonia in a child — learn to count breaths and look at the chest
- Measles vaccination and pneumococcal vaccine (PCV) protect children against the most serious cough illnesses — ensure your child's vaccines are up to date at the PHC
- Never give adult cough mixtures to children under 5 — many contain codeine, antihistamines, or alcohol that can be harmful
- Keep children away from cooking fire smoke, burning rubbish, and dusty environments — these worsen coughs
- ORS should be given if cough is accompanied by vomiting or diarrhea and dehydration is a concern

---

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- **Cooking fire smoke:** In many homes in northern Nigeria, cooking is done indoors or in enclosed spaces. Smoke is a major trigger and worsener of childhood cough and lung infections. Where possible, cook outside or increase ventilation when cooking.
- **Harmattan season:** The dry, dusty harmattan winds (November–February) are a major cause of childhood respiratory illness in Kano. During harmattan, keep children indoors in the early morning, cover their nose and mouth when going out, and increase fluids.
- **Herbal remedies:** Some families use steam inhalation with local herbs or give herbal teas for cough. UMMA understands this tradition. However, herbal preparations can cause burns (from hot steam), respiratory irritation, or toxicity in young children. Please avoid these and use the safe home measures described above instead.
- **PMVs and chemist shops:** Do not buy cough syrups from a patent medicine vendor without first seeing a nurse. If you must go to a PMV, ask specifically for paracetamol syrup for fever only — not cough mixture.
- **TB awareness:** A cough lasting more than 2 weeks in a child who has been in contact with a person with TB (or who coughs blood) must be investigated at a government health facility — TB care is free in Nigeria.
- **PHC access:** All PHCs in Kano state provide free or low-cost treatment for childhood chest infections. Ask specifically for the "under-5 clinic" or "child welfare clinic."
