# Breathing Difficulty — Symptom Care File (Pregnancy)
**UMMA AI | Maternal & Child Health | Kano/Northern Nigeria Context**
**Target: Pregnant women, all trimesters**

---

## CRITICAL NOTE FOR UMMA
Breathing difficulty in pregnancy has a broad differential — from normal physiological breathlessness of late pregnancy to life-threatening emergencies including pulmonary embolism, severe pre-eclampsia, severe anaemia, pneumonia, peripartum cardiomyopathy, and severe malaria. UMMA must treat any significant or sudden breathing difficulty as potentially serious. The threshold for escalation is low.

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- Sudden onset of severe breathlessness — the woman cannot complete a sentence, cannot lie flat, or is gasping
- Breathlessness with chest pain — especially sharp, one-sided chest pain that is worse with breathing in
- Breathlessness with rapid heartbeat (heart racing or pounding) that will not settle
- Breathlessness with coughing up pink, frothy, or blood-streaked sputum
- Breathlessness with blue or grey lips or fingertips (cyanosis)
- Breathlessness with loss of consciousness or near-fainting
- Breathlessness with high fever and shaking chills (possible severe malaria, pneumonia, or sepsis)
- Breathlessness alongside severe headache, visual disturbance, or face and hand swelling (possible severe pre-eclampsia)
- Breathlessness with swelling or pain in one leg that appeared recently (possible deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism — a blood clot)
- Breathlessness at rest in any trimester where it was not present before
- Breathlessness in a woman known to have heart disease, severe anaemia, or multiple pregnancy
- Any breathing difficulty in the postpartum period (within 6 weeks of delivery)
- Woman is unable to breathe lying down and can only breathe sitting upright

**Plain language:**
"Breathing problems in pregnancy can be very serious. If you are struggling to breathe, have chest pain, your lips are going blue, or you feel like you are going to faint — this is an emergency. Go to the hospital immediately. Do not rest and wait to see if it passes. Ask someone to take you now. If you have a fast heartbeat and one leg is swollen — go immediately, this can be a blood clot in the lung."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Breathlessness that has been gradually worsening over the past week and is now affecting daily activities
- Breathlessness with persistent cough, fever, or feeling very unwell (possible pneumonia or severe malaria)
- Breathlessness in a woman with known anaemia or who has been told her blood is "low"
- Breathlessness with swollen ankles that came on suddenly and is much worse than before
- Woman is waking at night because she cannot breathe (orthopnoea) — a new symptom
- Breathlessness after any physical exertion that previously did not cause this problem
- Breathlessness in early pregnancy (first trimester) — breathlessness is not expected this early and needs investigation
- Woman with known asthma whose symptoms are worsening in pregnancy

**Plain language:**
"This breathing problem needs to be checked today — it should not be left until your next ANC appointment. Go to the PHC or hospital today. Tell the nurse you are pregnant and that you are having difficulty breathing. Bring your antenatal card. If you have asthma, bring your inhaler if you have one."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
**Conditions for home care:**
- Breathlessness is mild and has been present for several weeks in a known pattern
- Breathlessness only occurs with significant exertion — walking long distances, climbing stairs — and not at rest
- No chest pain, no rapid heartbeat, no coughing blood, no blue lips
- No fever
- No face, hand, or sudden leg swelling
- No headache or visual changes
- Blood pressure was normal at the last ANC visit
- Woman is in the third trimester and breathlessness is consistent with the baby pressing up under the diaphragm
- Woman has discussed this at ANC and nurse confirmed it as physiological

**Home care instructions:**
1. Slow down — reduce the pace of activities; this is not the time to push through breathlessness
2. Sleep propped up on two or three pillows or on your left side — lying flat makes breathing harder in late pregnancy as the uterus presses the diaphragm
3. Take rest breaks frequently during any activity
4. In hot weather or during harmattan, stay inside during the hottest parts of the day — heat worsens breathlessness
5. Keep the room ventilated — open windows when possible; avoid cooking fire smoke indoors
6. Do not use any herbal inhalations or steam treatments — these can irritate the airways
7. Paracetamol is safe if needed for any concurrent discomfort — do not use any other medicine without a nurse's advice
8. If you have asthma, use your prescribed inhaler as directed — do not stop asthma medicine in pregnancy without discussing with your doctor
9. Do not take any cough mixture or breathing medicine from a patent medicine vendor without a prescription

**Plain language:**
"Mild breathlessness in late pregnancy from the baby pressing up on your lungs is common. Rest more, sleep propped up, and slow down. But watch carefully — if you become more breathless, cannot breathe at rest, or any of the warning signs below appear — go to the hospital. Do not wait."

---

### MONITOR — With clear safety net
**Conditions:**
- Very mild breathlessness that the woman has had for several weeks with no change
- Only on significant exertion, not at rest or lying down
- Third trimester, previously assessed at ANC as physiological
- No other symptoms

**What to watch:**
- Is the breathlessness getting worse — happening with less and less activity?
- Is it now happening at rest or while lying down?
- Has any chest pain appeared?
- Is the heart racing or pounding?
- Has any fever developed?
- Are the lips or fingertips looking blue or grey?
- Has swelling in the face, hands, or one leg appeared suddenly?

**Plain language:**
"Watch your breathing every day. Note if it is getting worse or happening at rest. If anything changes from your normal pattern — go to the clinic the same day. Do not ignore worsening breathlessness in pregnancy."

---

### UNCERTAIN — When UMMA cannot determine severity
**Plain language:**
"I am not certain whether your breathing difficulty is the normal breathlessness of late pregnancy or something more serious. Breathing problems in pregnancy can become dangerous quickly. Please go to the nearest PHC or hospital today to be assessed. Do not wait. Bring your antenatal card and tell the nurse exactly when the breathlessness started and what makes it better or worse."

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**
- You cannot complete a sentence without stopping to breathe
- You have pain in the chest — especially sharp pain that is worse when breathing in
- Your lips or fingertips go blue or grey
- You are coughing up pink, frothy, or bloody sputum
- You feel your heart racing or pounding very fast
- You feel faint, dizzy, or lose consciousness
- You develop a high fever with shaking chills
- You have swelling in one leg and new breathlessness — together these can mean a blood clot
- You can only breathe sitting upright and not lying down at all
- The breathlessness came on suddenly rather than gradually

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE
- Mild breathlessness in the third trimester is physiological — the growing uterus pushes up against the diaphragm, reducing lung space. This is common and expected.
- Severe anaemia (low blood) is one of the most common causes of breathlessness in pregnant Nigerian women — it is caused by malaria, nutritional deficiency (iron and folate), and hookworm; it is detected by a blood test at ANC and treated with iron tablets and sometimes blood transfusion; attend ANC regularly to have your blood checked
- Blood clots in the lungs (pulmonary embolism) are a rare but life-threatening cause of sudden breathlessness in pregnancy and the postpartum period; sudden breathlessness + chest pain + swollen leg = emergency
- Peripartum cardiomyopathy — a condition where the heart muscle weakens during or after pregnancy — is more common in Nigerian women than in many other populations; symptoms include breathlessness at rest, breathlessness lying flat, and leg swelling; it can appear after delivery as well as in pregnancy; any new breathlessness in the postpartum period must be taken seriously
- Never take decongestants, antihistamines, or over-the-counter breathing medicines in pregnancy without medical advice — some are unsafe for the baby
- Asthma can worsen in pregnancy — do not stop prescribed inhalers; continue as directed by your doctor

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- **Severe anaemia:** Nigeria has very high rates of anaemia in pregnancy, particularly in the north, due to malaria, poor diet, and frequent pregnancies. Breathlessness due to severe anaemia requires hospital treatment — oral iron tablets are not sufficient for severe anaemia. If you are breathless and have been told your blood is very low, go to the hospital for urgent blood assessment.
- **Malaria in pregnancy:** Malaria in pregnancy causes severe anaemia, high fever, and breathlessness — and can kill both mother and baby quickly. Intermittent preventive treatment for malaria (IPTp with sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine) is given free at ANC visits. Every pregnant woman should receive this. If you develop fever and breathlessness — especially in the second and third trimester — go to hospital urgently for malaria testing.
- **Cooking fire smoke:** Cooking indoors over firewood or charcoal is a significant cause of respiratory irritation and worsened breathlessness in pregnant women. Where possible, cook outside or in well-ventilated spaces. Ask family members to help with cooking if breathlessness is limiting your activity.
- **Harmattan:** During harmattan season in Kano (November–February), dusty, dry air worsens breathing for all women, especially those with asthma or anaemia. Stay indoors during peak dust times, cover nose and mouth when outdoors, and increase fluid intake.
- **Peripartum cardiomyopathy awareness:** This condition is significantly more common in northern Nigeria than in most other regions globally — possibly linked to the practice of "kadediya" (lying on a hot bed postpartum and eating large amounts of salt and kanwa). Women should be aware that breathlessness, swollen legs, and inability to lie flat after delivery are not normal recovery — they require urgent hospital assessment. Do not assume postpartum breathlessness is tiredness.
- **Postpartum period:** Breathing difficulty does not stop being dangerous after delivery. The 6 weeks after birth are a high-risk period for blood clots, heart failure, and infection. Any new or worsening breathlessness after delivery must be assessed at the hospital on the same day.
- **Transport and night emergencies:** Breathing emergencies can happen at any time. Having a prepared transport plan — including at night — is essential for every pregnant woman. Identify your hospital, your transport contact, and your escort before you need them.
