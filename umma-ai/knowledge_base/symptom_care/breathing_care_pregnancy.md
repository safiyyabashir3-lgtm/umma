# SYMPTOM CARE FILE: DIFFICULTY BREATHING IN PREGNANCY
## Nigerian Context — Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
Triggered by any of:
- Sudden severe difficulty breathing
- Lips or fingertips turning blue
- Unable to speak in full sentences due to breathlessness
- Breathing difficulty with chest pain
- Breathing difficulty with collapse or fainting
- Breathing difficulty with no fetal movement
- Breathing difficulty with high fever and confusion
- Breathing difficulty with coughing up blood
- Breathing difficulty at rest with no exertion

Plain language: "You are having serious difficulty breathing. This is an emergency. Please go to hospital immediately — get help to take you now, do not drive yourself."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
Triggered by any of:
- Breathlessness that is new or worsening over days
- Breathlessness with fever
- Breathlessness with cough producing coloured sputum
- Breathlessness with swelling of legs, face, or hands
- Breathlessness with rapid heart rate felt by the woman
- Breathlessness that wakes her from sleep
- Any breathing difficulty in third trimester that is new
- Any uncertainty about cause or severity

Plain language: "Your breathing needs to be checked by a doctor today. Please go to your nearest clinic or hospital — do not wait."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
- Very mild breathlessness on significant exertion only
- Known to be present since earlier in pregnancy and not worsening
- No chest pain
- No fever
- No cough with coloured sputum
- Baby moving normally
- No swelling of face or hands
- Previously assessed at antenatal clinic and told this is expected

Home care instructions:
- Rest and avoid strenuous activity
- Sleep propped up on pillows if lying flat worsens breathing
- Sleep on left side
- Avoid hot, poorly ventilated rooms
- Drink adequate fluids
- Do not use any traditional inhalation remedies
- Return to clinic immediately if breathing worsens

Plain language: "Mild breathlessness from the baby pressing on your lungs is common in late pregnancy. Rest, sleep propped up, and avoid exertion. But please go to the clinic today if it gets worse, you develop fever, or you are worried."

---

### MONITOR — With very clear safety net
- Mild positional breathlessness, well-known since mid-pregnancy, no new features
- Must reassess at every antenatal visit
- Any new symptom or worsening — go to clinic immediately

---

## SAFETY NET — Attach to EVERY response about breathing difficulty in pregnancy
"Please watch carefully. Go to hospital immediately if your breathing becomes very difficult or sudden, if you have chest pain, if your lips or fingers look blue, if your baby stops moving, if you develop a high fever, or if you feel you cannot get enough air even at rest. Do not wait if you are worried — breathing problems in pregnancy must always be checked by a doctor."

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE FOR BREATHING DIFFICULTY IN PREGNANCY
- Mild breathlessness from the growing uterus pressing on the diaphragm is common in late pregnancy — this is expected
- Any sudden or severe breathlessness is never normal and always needs urgent assessment
- Do not take any asthma inhalers, decongestants, or breathing medicines without medical advice in pregnancy
- Do not use steam inhalation with herbal substances in pregnancy
- Sleep on left side and with head elevated to ease breathing
- Avoid smoky environments — second-hand smoke worsens breathing
- Attend all antenatal appointments — breathing and blood pressure must be monitored together

---

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- Anaemia is very common in pregnant Nigerian women and is a significant cause of breathlessness — must be checked at clinic
- Malaria causing severe anaemia can present with breathlessness — fever with breathlessness is always urgent
- Cardiac problems in pregnancy are under-recognised in Nigeria — new breathlessness with rapid heartbeat must not be dismissed
- Cooking smoke from wood or kerosene stoves worsens breathing in pregnant women in northern Nigeria — improve ventilation where possible
- Pre-eclampsia with pulmonary oedema can cause sudden severe breathlessness — this is life threatening and must be treated as emergency
- Primary health centres may not have capacity to manage severe breathing difficulties — go to secondary or tertiary facility if symptoms are severe
