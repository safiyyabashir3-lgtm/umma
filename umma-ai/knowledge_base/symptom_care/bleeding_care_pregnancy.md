# SYMPTOM CARE FILE: VAGINAL BLEEDING IN PREGNANCY
## Nigerian Context — Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
Triggered by any of:
- Any heavy vaginal bleeding at any stage of pregnancy
- Bleeding with severe abdominal pain
- Bleeding with dizziness, fainting, or collapse
- Bleeding with no fetal movement in third trimester
- Bleeding with fever and foul smelling discharge
- Bleeding soaking more than one pad per hour
- Bleeding with signs of shock — pale, cold, sweating, weak
- Any bleeding in third trimester regardless of amount

Plain language: "Vaginal bleeding in pregnancy is always serious. Please go to hospital immediately — do not wait. Do not drive yourself. Get help to take you now."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
Triggered by any of:
- Any bleeding in second trimester
- Light bleeding in first trimester with abdominal pain or cramping
- Bleeding that has stopped but was present
- Spotting with fever
- Any bleeding where cause is unknown
- Previous miscarriage or ectopic pregnancy history with any bleeding

Plain language: "Any bleeding in pregnancy must be checked by a doctor today. Please go to your nearest clinic or hospital now — do not wait to see if it stops on its own."

---

### MONITOR — First trimester spotting only, with strict conditions
Only if ALL of these are true:
- Very light spotting only — a few drops, not filling a pad
- First trimester only
- No pain or cramping
- No fever
- No foul smell
- Has been assessed by a doctor previously for this pregnancy

This is not home management — it is watchful waiting while preparing to go if anything changes.

Plain language: "Very light spotting in early pregnancy sometimes occurs, but it must still be checked. Please go to the clinic today. Watch carefully and go immediately if bleeding increases, pain starts, or you feel unwell."

---

### THERE IS NO HOME CARE TIER FOR VAGINAL BLEEDING IN PREGNANCY
Any vaginal bleeding in pregnancy requires clinical assessment. There is no safe home management for this symptom.

---

## SAFETY NET — Attach to EVERY response about bleeding in pregnancy
"Any bleeding in pregnancy must be taken seriously. Go to hospital immediately if bleeding is heavy or increasing, if you have severe pain, if you feel dizzy or faint, if your baby stops moving, or if you feel very unwell. Do not wait. Do not manage this at home."

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE FOR BLEEDING IN PREGNANCY
- Never ignore vaginal bleeding in pregnancy — there is no safe amount to ignore
- Do not use traditional remedies or herbal medicines to stop bleeding — this is dangerous
- Do not insert anything into the vagina
- Lie down and rest while arranging transport to hospital
- Note how much blood — how many pads used, colour, clots present
- Note any pain, fever, or other symptoms — tell the doctor everything
- If in third trimester and bleeding — this is always an emergency

---

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- Transportation to hospital is a significant barrier in northern Nigeria — begin arranging transport immediately, do not wait to see if bleeding stops
- Placenta praevia and placental abruption are serious causes of bleeding in late pregnancy — both are emergencies requiring hospital
- Obstetric haemorrhage is among the leading causes of maternal death in northern Nigeria
- Do not accept advice from community members or traditional birth attendants to manage bleeding at home — this is life-threatening
- Blood transfusion may be needed — go to a facility that can provide this
- Unsafe abortion complications presenting as bleeding must be recognised — do not withhold history from doctors, they are there to help not to judge
- Primary health centres may not have capacity to manage serious bleeding — aim for secondary or tertiary facility if bleeding is heavy
