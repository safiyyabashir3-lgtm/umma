# SYMPTOM CARE FILE: NAUSEA AND VOMITING IN PREGNANCY
## Nigerian Context — Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
Triggered by any of:
- Vomiting with inability to keep any fluid down for more than 24 hours
- Vomiting with signs of severe dehydration — very dark urine, no urine for 8 hours, confusion, extreme weakness
- Vomiting with severe abdominal pain
- Vomiting blood or material that looks like coffee grounds
- Vomiting with high fever and confusion
- Vomiting with no fetal movement in third trimester
- Vomiting with yellowing of eyes or skin — jaundice
- Vomiting with seizure or collapse

Plain language: "You are very unwell and need hospital care immediately. You may be severely dehydrated or there may be a serious problem. Please go to hospital now — do not wait."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
Triggered by any of:
- Unable to keep fluids down for more than 12 hours
- Vomiting more than 5 times per day and not improving
- Vomiting with weight loss or unable to eat anything for more than 2 days
- Vomiting with dizziness or feeling faint when standing
- Vomiting with dark urine or reduced urine output
- Vomiting beyond 14 weeks of pregnancy that is severe
- Vomiting with fever
- Vomiting in third trimester that is new or severe
- Any uncertainty about severity or cause

Plain language: "You need to be seen by a doctor or nurse today. You may be becoming dehydrated and need treatment. Please go to your nearest clinic — do not wait until tomorrow."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
- Nausea and vomiting in first trimester only
- Able to keep some fluids down
- Passing urine normally — urine pale yellow not dark
- No fever
- No severe abdominal pain
- No blood in vomit
- Baby moving normally if past first trimester
- Not losing significant weight

Home care instructions:
- Eat small amounts very frequently — do not eat large meals
- Eat dry plain foods first thing in morning before getting up — plain bread, crackers, or rice
- Avoid strong smells, spicy foods, and fatty foods
- Ginger is safe in pregnancy and may help nausea — ginger tea in small amounts
- Rest as much as possible
- Drink small sips of clean water or oral rehydration solution frequently — do not try to drink large amounts at once
- Avoid lying flat immediately after eating — sit upright for at least 30 minutes
- Do not take any anti-nausea medicines without medical advice
- Do not take herbal or traditional remedies for vomiting in pregnancy

Plain language: "Nausea and vomiting in early pregnancy is very common. Eat small amounts often, drink small sips frequently, rest, and avoid strong smells. Go to the clinic today if you cannot keep any fluid down or you feel very weak."

---

### MONITOR — With very clear safety net
- Mild nausea with occasional vomiting, first trimester, able to drink, passing urine normally
- Reassess daily
- Any worsening or inability to keep fluids down — go to clinic same day

---

## SAFETY NET — Attach to EVERY response about vomiting in pregnancy
"Please watch carefully. Go to hospital immediately if you cannot keep any fluid down for more than 12 hours, if you have not passed urine for 8 hours or your urine is very dark, if you vomit blood, if you have severe pain in your stomach, if you feel very weak or confused, or if your baby stops moving. Severe vomiting in pregnancy can cause dangerous dehydration — do not wait if you are worried."

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE FOR VOMITING IN PREGNANCY
- Nausea and vomiting in the first trimester is very common and usually not dangerous — but it must be monitored
- Hyperemesis gravidarum — severe vomiting that causes dehydration and weight loss — is a serious condition that needs hospital treatment with fluids
- Do not take anti-nausea medicines without medical advice — some are not safe in pregnancy
- Do not take aspirin or ibuprofen for any reason in pregnancy
- Do not use herbal or traditional remedies for vomiting in pregnancy — safety is unknown
- Ginger tea in small amounts is safe and may help mild nausea
- Small frequent meals are more effective than large meals
- Dehydration from vomiting can harm both mother and baby — fluid intake must be maintained

---

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- Intravenous fluids for severe vomiting are available at primary health centres and hospitals — do not delay going if unable to keep fluids down
- Traditional belief that severe vomiting is normal and must be endured is dangerous — hyperemesis gravidarum requires hospital treatment
- Do not use agbo, local herbs, or roadside medicines for vomiting in pregnancy — these can be harmful to the baby
- In hot northern Nigerian climate — dehydration from vomiting worsens much faster than in cooler climates, act quickly
- Malaria can cause vomiting in pregnancy — fever with vomiting always needs malaria assessment at clinic
- Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) may help nausea and is available at antenatal clinics — ask the nurse or doctor
- Women who cannot attend clinic alone due to cultural restrictions — explain to husband or family member that severe vomiting requires medical attention and is not normal
