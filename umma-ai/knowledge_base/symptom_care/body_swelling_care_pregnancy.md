# Body Swelling (Oedema) — Symptom Care File (Pregnancy)
**UMMA AI | Maternal & Child Health | Kano/Northern Nigeria Context**
**Target: Pregnant women, all trimesters**

---

## CRITICAL NOTE FOR UMMA
Swelling in pregnancy ranges from normal and benign (mild ankle swelling in late pregnancy) to a sign of severe pre-eclampsia, which is one of the leading causes of maternal death in Nigeria. UMMA must distinguish between normal physiological oedema and dangerous pathological swelling. The combination of swelling + headache + visual disturbance + high blood pressure is a pre-eclampsia emergency. Never dismiss swelling in pregnancy without assessing the full picture.

---

## ACTION TIERS

### EMERGENCY — Go to hospital immediately
**Triggered by:**
- Swelling of the face, especially around the eyes, that appeared suddenly or is severe
- Swelling of the hands and fingers — rings that fitted yesterday now cannot be removed
- Swelling accompanied by severe headache — especially at the front or back of the head
- Swelling with visual disturbances — blurred vision, seeing spots or flashing lights, temporary blindness
- Swelling with severe pain in the upper right abdomen or across the upper abdomen (epigastric pain)
- Swelling with confusion, agitation, or difficulty thinking clearly
- Swelling with reduced or no urine output
- Swelling with shortness of breath at rest — the woman cannot breathe comfortably lying flat
- Swelling that appeared suddenly and rapidly all over the body
- Any combination of swelling + headache + visual changes — this is severe pre-eclampsia until proven otherwise
- Swelling in a woman who has been told she has high blood pressure in this pregnancy

**Plain language:**
"Swelling in the face, hands, or with a headache and vision problems is a serious warning sign in pregnancy. This could be pre-eclampsia — a condition that can be fatal if not treated. Please go to the hospital right now. Do not take any medicine at home first. Do not wait until morning. Go immediately and tell the nurses and doctors that you have swelling, headache, and vision problems."

---

### URGENT CLINIC — Go today, same day
**Triggered by:**
- Ankle and foot swelling that is present in the morning when you wake up (not just at the end of the day)
- Swelling that has gotten suddenly worse in the past 24–48 hours
- Swelling above the knee, involving the thighs
- Any new swelling in the hands or face, even if mild
- Blood pressure reading above 140/90 mmHg at home or at another health facility, alongside swelling
- Swelling with headache, even if mild
- Swelling with reduced urine or urine that looks dark or foamy/frothy
- Swelling in the first or early second trimester (before 20 weeks) — swelling this early is not typical and needs investigation
- Woman has not had blood pressure checked in the last 2–4 weeks and has new or worsening swelling

**Plain language:**
"This swelling needs to be checked today. Your nurse needs to check your blood pressure and test your urine. Bring your antenatal card. If the swelling is in your face or hands, or has gotten worse quickly — do not wait, go now. Do not try to manage this at home."

---

### HOME CARE — Only if ALL of these are true
**Conditions for home care:**
- Swelling is only in the ankles and feet
- Swelling is worse in the evening and gone or much improved in the morning
- No face or hand swelling
- No headache
- No visual changes
- Blood pressure was normal at last ANC visit (and was recent)
- Urine has no unusual colour, smell, or frothiness
- Woman is in the second half of pregnancy (after 20 weeks) — this pattern is common and usually physiological
- No other symptoms of pre-eclampsia

**Home care instructions:**
1. Rest with your feet raised above the level of your heart — lie on your side (left side preferred) and prop your feet on a pillow
2. Avoid standing for long periods without breaks — if you must stand, shift your weight and move your feet regularly
3. Avoid sitting with legs hanging down — elevate whenever possible
4. Drink adequate clean water — reducing fluid intake does not help oedema and is harmful
5. Reduce added salt in food — avoid very salty snacks, salted dried fish, and adding extra salt to meals
6. Wear comfortable, loose footwear — tight shoes worsen swelling and cause pain
7. Gentle walking (if you are able) helps circulation
8. Do not take water tablets (diuretics) — these are dangerous in pregnancy unless prescribed by a doctor
9. Check your face and hands every morning — if swelling appears in your face or hands, go to the clinic immediately
10. Do not miss your next ANC appointment — blood pressure monitoring is essential

**Plain language:**
"Mild swelling in your feet and ankles at the end of the day is common in late pregnancy. Rest with your feet raised, lie on your left side, and reduce salt. Drink enough water. But check your face and hands every morning. If swelling appears in your face or hands, or you get a headache or vision problems — go to the hospital immediately. Do not wait."

---

### MONITOR — With clear safety net
**Conditions:**
- Very mild ankle swelling that has been present for several weeks and was assessed at ANC
- Blood pressure confirmed normal at recent ANC visit
- No face or hand involvement, no headache, no visual changes
- Swelling follows a clear pattern — worse after standing, better with rest

**What to watch — CHECK EVERY DAY:**
- Is your face or the area around your eyes puffy in the morning?
- Are your hands or fingers swollen — can you make a fist easily?
- Has the ankle swelling gotten suddenly much worse?
- Do you have headache, especially at the front or back of your head?
- Have you noticed any change in your vision — blurring, spots, flashing lights?
- Is your urine looking dark, foamy, or greatly reduced in amount?

**Plain language:**
"Check yourself every morning. Look at your face and hands. If anything changes — go to the clinic the same day. Do not wait for your next scheduled ANC visit if new symptoms appear. Pre-eclampsia can develop quickly."

### UNCERTAIN — When UMMA cannot determine severity
**Plain language:**
"I cannot tell from what you have described whether your swelling is the normal type in pregnancy or a warning sign. The safest thing to do is go to the nearest PHC or ANC clinic today to have your blood pressure checked and your urine tested. This takes only a few minutes and can identify a serious problem early. Please go today — bring your antenatal card."

## SAFETY NET
**Go to hospital immediately if any of these happen:**
- Your face, especially around your eyes, becomes puffy or swollen
- Your hands or fingers become swollen
- You develop a headache — especially severe, at the front or back of the head
- Your vision changes — blurring, flashing lights, spots, or temporary loss of vision
- You have pain in the upper right side of your abdomen
- You feel confused, agitated, or unlike yourself
- You cannot pass urine, or your urine is very dark or foamy
- You find it hard to breathe while resting
- The swelling in your legs became suddenly much worse overnight

---

## GENERAL SAFE ADVICE
- Pre-eclampsia is a leading cause of maternal death in Nigeria — it is caused by high blood pressure in pregnancy and can damage the kidneys, liver, and brain; it can cause seizures (eclampsia) which are life-threatening
- The only cure for pre-eclampsia is delivery — but treatment at hospital can stabilise the mother and protect both lives; early detection saves lives
- Regular blood pressure checks at ANC are the most important way to detect pre-eclampsia before it becomes dangerous — do not miss ANC appointments
- Do NOT take herbal diuretics or "water-flushing" remedies for swelling in pregnancy — these are unsafe
- Do NOT reduce your water intake to treat swelling — dehydration worsens outcomes in pregnancy
- Foamy or frothy urine (that looks like it has bubbles that persist) can indicate protein in the urine — a sign of pre-eclampsia; report this immediately

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC CARE ADVICE
- **ANC attendance:** In Kano and across northern Nigeria, ANC attendance remains below recommended levels, and late booking is common. Blood pressure is the most critical screening tool for pre-eclampsia — it requires attending ANC. UMMA strongly encourages every pregnant woman to attend ANC as early as possible and to attend all 8 recommended visits.
- **Home blood pressure monitors:** In some urban households in Kano, home blood pressure machines are available. If you have access to one, take your blood pressure regularly after 20 weeks. A reading of 140/90 or above means go to the hospital today.
- **Herbal remedies for swelling:** Some families encourage pregnant women to use herbal remedies to "flush out" swelling or "cleanse the kidneys." UMMA understands that these are given out of care and concern. However, these substances — including diuretic herbs, bark preparations, and teas — can cause blood pressure changes and kidney stress that are dangerous in pre-eclampsia. They can mask serious symptoms. Please go to the hospital instead.
- **Transport planning:** Pre-eclampsia can become an emergency at any time of day or night. Every pregnant woman after 20 weeks should have a transport plan ready — who to call, which hospital to go to, how to pay. The State Emergency Obstetric Care facilities at AKTH and Murtala Muhammad Specialist Hospital Kano operate 24 hours.
- **Magnesium sulphate:** The treatment for severe pre-eclampsia and eclampsia at hospital is magnesium sulphate injection — this is available at government hospitals in Kano and is life-saving. Do not be afraid of the hospital — this medicine saves lives.
- **Decision-making in the household:** In some families, a woman needs permission from her husband or mother-in-law to go to the hospital. UMMA acknowledges this reality. If swelling with headache and visual disturbance is present — this is a life-threatening emergency. Please speak clearly to your family: "The AI health companion told me to go to the hospital immediately — this is dangerous." Your life matters.
