# SYMPTOM FILE: HEADACHE IN PREGNANCY
## For UMMA AI Clinical Reasoning — Nigerian Context
### Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## SECTION 1: SYMPTOM VERIFICATION

### Confirming the symptom
If headache is clearly stated — proceed to triage immediately.
If unclear — ask only: "Where exactly is the pain — is it in your head?"

### Symptoms that may be confused with headache
- Neck pain or stiffness — important to differentiate — stiff neck with fever is emergency
- Eye pain — may indicate visual problem
- Sinus pain — facial pain and pressure

---

## SECTION 2: TRIAGE

### QUESTIONING RULES
- One question at a time only
- Maximum 3 triage questions
- If opening message already contains red flag — escalate immediately
- Acknowledge warmly before each question

### RED FLAGS — Escalate immediately if any present
- Worst ever headache
- Headache with blurry vision or seeing spots or flashing lights
- Headache with swelling of face or hands
- Headache with seizure or convulsion
- Headache with confusion
- Headache after a fall or head injury
- Headache with fever and stiff neck

### Triage question priority order
Priority 1: "Is this the worst headache you have ever had, or is your vision blurry or are you seeing spots?"
Priority 2: "Do you have any swelling of your face or hands along with the headache?"
Priority 3: "Do you have a fever or does your neck feel stiff?"

### IF ANY RED FLAG CONFIRMED — Escalate immediately
### IF NO RED FLAGS — Proceed to Section 3

## SECTION 3: CLINICAL REASONING GUIDE

### QUESTIONING RULES
- One question at a time
- Maximum 4 questions total
- Use what user has already said
- Reason confidently if pattern is clear

### NIGERIAN CONTEXT NOTE
Pre-eclampsia must always be considered first in headache after 20 weeks of pregnancy. Malaria is the second priority. Never dismiss headache in pregnancy as trivial.

---

### DISEASE 1: PRE-ECLAMPSIA
Nigerian priority: HIGHEST after 20 weeks

Key differentiating features:
- Gestational age over 20 weeks
- Headache with swelling of face or hands
- Visual disturbance
- Reduced urine output

Differentiating questions:
- "How many months pregnant are you?"
- "Do you have any swelling of your face or hands?"
- "Has your vision been blurry or have you seen any spots or flashing lights?"
- "Have you noticed any reduction in how much urine you are passing?"

Confirm towards pre-eclampsia if:
- Over 20 weeks + headache + any swelling of face or hands
- Over 20 weeks + headache + any visual symptom
- Over 20 weeks + headache + reduced urine

Uncertainty note:
- If over 20 weeks and headache present — treat as possible pre-eclampsia until proven otherwise
- Low threshold to escalate — this kills Nigerian women

---

### DISEASE 2: MALARIA
Nigerian priority: HIGH — always consider

Key differentiating features:
- Fever with chills and sweating
- Headache prominent feature of malaria
- Vomiting

Differentiating questions:
- "Do you have a fever along with the headache?"
- "Are you feeling cold and shivery at times then very hot?"

Confirm towards malaria if:
- Headache + fever + chills + sweating

Uncertainty note:
- Malaria and pre-eclampsia can coexist
- If over 20 weeks — always consider pre-eclampsia even if malaria is also suspected

---

### DISEASE 3: TENSION HEADACHE
Nigerian priority: MEDIUM — diagnosis of exclusion

Key differentiating features:
- Band-like pressure around head
- Related to stress, poor sleep, heat, dehydration
- No fever, no visual symptoms, no swelling
- Mild to moderate intensity

Differentiating questions:
- "Does the headache feel like a tight band around your head?"
- "Have you been under a lot of stress or sleeping poorly?"
- "Have you been drinking enough water today?"

Confirm towards tension headache if:
- Band-like headache + no fever + no visual symptoms + no swelling + stress or dehydration present
- Must confirm under 20 weeks OR confirm no pre-eclampsia features before reassuring

Uncertainty note:
- Never diagnose tension headache in pregnancy without excluding pre-eclampsia first

---

### DISEASE 4: ANAEMIA
Nigerian priority: MEDIUM-HIGH

Key differentiating features:
- Dizziness and pallor with headache
- Fatigue
- Poor diet

Differentiating questions:
- "Do you feel dizzy or do the inside of your eyelids look pale?"
- "Are you feeling very weak or tired along with the headache?"

Confirm towards anaemia if:
- Headache + dizziness + pallor + fatigue + poor diet history

---

### DISEASE 5: DEHYDRATION
Nigerian priority: MEDIUM

Key differentiating features:
- Poor fluid intake
- Vomiting
- Dark urine
- Dry mouth
- Hot weather exposure

Differentiating questions:
- "Have you been drinking enough water today and is your urine dark?"
- "Have you been vomiting or unable to eat or drink today?"

Confirm towards dehydration if:
- Headache + dark urine + poor fluid intake + dry mouth + no fever + no swelling

---

### GENERAL UNCERTAINTY RULE
- If over 20 weeks and headache — treat as possible pre-eclampsia regardless of other features
- If cause unclear — urgent clinic visit
- Never give reassurance for headache in pregnancy without excluding pre-eclampsia
