# UMMA Symptom File: Difficulty Breathing in Pregnancy

---

## SECTION 1: SYMPTOM VERIFICATION

**Confirm the symptom:**
- Ask whether the woman is struggling to breathe, breathing faster than normal, feeling short of breath at rest or on minimal exertion, or feeling she cannot get enough air
- Ask when it started — sudden onset versus gradual over days or weeks
- Ask gestational age — some breathlessness is normal from the second trimester onward due to diaphragm elevation, but new, sudden, or worsening breathlessness is always a concern
- Ask whether it is present at rest or only on exertion
- Ask whether it is worse lying down

**Commonly confused with:**
- Normal physiological breathlessness of pregnancy — mild breathlessness on exertion from the second trimester, no breathlessness at rest, improves slightly in late third trimester when baby engages; this is a diagnosis of exclusion
- Anxiety or panic — may cause fast breathing and feeling of not getting enough air; but anxiety is a diagnosis of exclusion in pregnancy; dangerous causes must be ruled out first
- Nasal congestion — pregnancy rhinitis causes blocked nose and breathing through the mouth; ask whether the problem is nose-related or a feeling of not getting enough air

**Critical immediate checks:**
- Is she breathless at rest? — always escalate
- Did it start suddenly? — pulmonary embolism or severe asthma attack until excluded
- Is there chest pain? — pulmonary embolism, cardiac cause, or pneumonia
- Is she pale or does she look blue around the lips? — emergency
- Is she unable to speak in full sentences? — severe respiratory compromise, escalate immediately

---

## SECTION 2: TRIAGE — RED FLAGS

Escalate immediately if any of the following are present:

1. Breathlessness at rest — cannot get enough air without exertion
2. Sudden onset difficulty breathing — pulmonary embolism until excluded
3. Breathlessness with chest pain — pulmonary embolism, cardiac emergency, or pneumonia
4. Breathlessness with coughing up blood or blood-stained sputum
5. Breathlessness with fever — pneumonia or severe malaria
6. Breathlessness with inability to speak in full sentences
7. Breathlessness with blue or grey colouring of the lips or fingernails (cyanosis)
8. Breathlessness that is getting rapidly worse over hours
9. Breathlessness with leg swelling and calf pain — pulmonary embolism from DVT
10. Breathlessness with inability to lie flat (orthopnoea) — cardiac failure
11. Breathlessness with wheeze that is not responding to any inhaler the woman uses
12. Breathlessness with dizziness, fainting, or feeling of imminent collapse
13. Breathlessness with reduced or absent fetal movement
14. Breathlessness in a woman with known heart disease, sickle cell disease, or HIV
15. Any new or worsening breathlessness in the third trimester that was not present before

---

## SECTION 3: CLINICAL REASONING GUIDE

---

### DISEASE 1: PULMONARY EMBOLISM
**Nigerian priority level: HIGHEST**

Pulmonary embolism (blood clot in the lung) is a leading cause of maternal death globally and in Nigeria. Pregnancy is a major risk factor. It causes sudden breathlessness and chest pain and can be rapidly fatal. It must be the first consideration for any sudden onset breathlessness in pregnancy.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Sudden onset — was breathing normally and then suddenly could not breathe properly
- Pleuritic chest pain — sharp pain on breathing in, often one-sided
- May follow leg swelling or calf pain (DVT as source of clot)
- Rapid heart rate
- Coughing up blood or blood-stained sputum
- Risk factors: immobility, long travel, previous DVT, caesarean section, obesity, thrombophilia
- No fever necessarily (distinguishes from pneumonia, though low fever can occur)

**Differentiating questions:**
- Did the breathlessness start suddenly — was she well and then suddenly short of breath?
- Does she have chest pain, especially when she breathes in deeply?
- Has she had swelling or pain in one leg recently?
- Has she had a long car or bus journey recently, or has she been lying in bed for a long time?
- Has she had a blood clot before?
- Does she cough up any blood or blood-stained sputum?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Sudden onset breathlessness with pleuritic chest pain
- Preceding leg swelling or calf pain
- Risk factors for thromboembolism
- Rapid heart rate
- Haemoptysis

**Uncertainty note:**
Pulmonary embolism can be rapidly fatal. Any sudden onset breathlessness in pregnancy — even without chest pain — must be escalated as an emergency. There is no safe observation period.

---

### DISEASE 2: PNEUMONIA
**Nigerian priority level: HIGH**

Pneumonia is more severe in pregnancy due to physiological changes and is a significant cause of maternal mortality. Community-acquired bacterial pneumonia, including that following influenza, is the most common type. Women with HIV, sickle cell, or severe anaemia are at higher risk.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Gradual onset over days — cough preceding breathlessness
- Productive cough with sputum — may be yellow, green, or rust-coloured
- Fever present
- Pleuritic chest pain
- Unilateral or bilateral in distribution
- History of contact with someone with respiratory illness
- HIV or other immunocompromised state

**Differentiating questions:**
- Has she had a cough for several days before the breathing difficulty started?
- Is she bringing up sputum — what colour is it?
- Does she have fever alongside the breathlessness?
- Is there sharp chest pain when she breathes in?
- Has she been in contact with someone who was coughing or ill?
- Does she have HIV or sickle cell disease?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Cough preceding breathlessness by days
- Fever with breathlessness
- Productive cough with coloured sputum
- Pleuritic chest pain
- Immunocompromised state

**Uncertainty note:**
Pneumonia in pregnancy can deteriorate rapidly, particularly in women with HIV. Any fever with breathlessness must be escalated urgently. Do not attribute cough and breathlessness to normal pregnancy changes.

---

### DISEASE 3: CARDIAC DISEASE IN PREGNANCY
**Nigerian priority level: HIGH**

Rheumatic heart disease is highly prevalent in northern Nigeria and may be undiagnosed before pregnancy. The dramatically increased cardiac workload of pregnancy can cause decompensation and pulmonary oedema, presenting as breathlessness — especially orthopnoea and paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Cannot lie flat — must sleep propped up on multiple pillows (orthopnoea)
- Woken at night by breathlessness (paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea)
- Progressive breathlessness worsening over weeks of pregnancy
- Palpitations — irregular or fast heartbeat
- Leg and ankle swelling
- History of rheumatic fever, heart murmur, or prior cardiac diagnosis
- Fatigue out of proportion to pregnancy

**Differentiating questions:**
- How many pillows does she use to sleep — can she lie flat?
- Has she been woken up at night feeling she cannot breathe?
- Does she get breathless very quickly doing small tasks?
- Does she feel her heart beating fast or irregularly?
- Has she been told she has a heart problem or murmur?
- Did she have rheumatic fever as a child?
- Is there swelling in her legs?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Orthopnoea and paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea
- Palpitations and leg swelling
- Known or suspected rheumatic heart disease
- Progressive breathlessness worsening through pregnancy

**Uncertainty note:**
Cardiac decompensation in pregnancy progresses rapidly and is life-threatening. Any breathlessness that is worse lying flat, or that wakes a woman from sleep, requires emergency facility assessment.

---

### DISEASE 4: SEVERE MALARIA WITH PULMONARY COMPLICATIONS
**Nigerian priority level: HIGHEST**

Severe Plasmodium falciparum malaria can cause pulmonary oedema (fluid in the lungs), which presents as acute breathlessness in pregnancy. This is a direct life-threatening complication. Pregnant women are at higher risk for severe malaria.

**Key differentiating features:**
- High fever with breathlessness — fever onset usually precedes breathlessness
- Cyclical fever pattern with chills and rigors
- Altered consciousness or confusion
- Severe anaemia signs — pallor of conjunctiva, lips, palms
- No bednet use or missed IPTp
- Can progress rapidly to respiratory failure

**Differentiating questions:**
- Is she having fever alongside the difficulty breathing?
- Is she having chills and sweating?
- Is she confused or drowsy?
- Does she look pale in her eyes and palms?
- Is she sleeping under a mosquito net?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Fever with breathlessness — especially cyclical fever
- Severe anaemia signs
- Confusion or altered consciousness
- No bednet use or missed IPTp

**Uncertainty note:**
Severe malaria with pulmonary involvement can kill within hours. Fever with breathlessness in pregnancy is severe malaria until excluded. Escalate immediately.

---

### DISEASE 5: SEVERE ANAEMIA
**Nigerian priority level: HIGH**

Severe anaemia causes breathlessness through reduced oxygen-carrying capacity and compensatory high-output cardiac state. It is extremely common in pregnancy in northern Nigeria from malaria, nutritional deficiency, and sickle cell disease.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Breathlessness on minimal exertion or at rest in severe cases
- Extreme fatigue and weakness
- Visible pallor — pale conjunctiva, lips, palms, nail beds
- Rapid heart rate (compensatory tachycardia)
- History of sickle cell disease, repeated malaria, or poor nutritional intake
- Gradual onset over weeks

**Differentiating questions:**
- Does she look pale — in her eyes, lips, or palms?
- Is she extremely tired or weak?
- Has she been having repeated malaria?
- Does she have sickle cell disease?
- Has anyone checked her blood level during this pregnancy?
- Is the breathlessness getting worse gradually over weeks?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Pallor of conjunctiva and mucous membranes
- Extreme fatigue with breathlessness
- History consistent with severe anaemia
- Gradual onset over weeks

**Uncertainty note:**
Severe anaemia causing breathlessness requires urgent facility assessment and may require blood transfusion. Do not observe at home.

---

### DISEASE 6: ASTHMA EXACERBATION
**Nigerian priority level: HIGH**

Asthma affects a significant proportion of women of reproductive age in Nigeria. Pregnancy can worsen asthma in some women, particularly in the second trimester. A severe exacerbation can be rapidly life-threatening.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Prior history of asthma — most women will know their diagnosis
- Wheeze — whistling sound on breathing out
- Breathlessness with tightness in the chest
- Triggered by allergens, smoke, dust, cold air, or infection
- Not responding to usual inhaler or no inhaler available
- No fever in pure asthma (fever suggests infection as trigger)

**Differentiating questions:**
- Has she been diagnosed with asthma before?
- Is she hearing a whistling sound when she breathes?
- Does she feel tightness in her chest?
- Has she used her inhaler — did it help?
- Was there a trigger — dust, smoke, cold air?
- Does she have fever?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Known asthma history
- Wheeze present
- Chest tightness
- Recognised trigger
- Not responding to inhaler

**Uncertainty note:**
Severe asthma not responding to inhaler is a respiratory emergency. Any asthma attack in pregnancy that is not resolving with usual management must be escalated immediately.

---

### DISEASE 7: PHYSIOLOGICAL BREATHLESSNESS OF PREGNANCY
**Nigerian priority level: MEDIUM**

Mild breathlessness on exertion is common and normal in pregnancy, caused by diaphragm elevation from the growing uterus and increased respiratory drive from progesterone. It is a diagnosis of exclusion.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Mild breathlessness on exertion only — not at rest
- Gradual onset in second trimester
- Not worsening rapidly
- Can lie flat comfortably
- No fever, no chest pain, no cough, no palpitations
- No pallor signs
- Normal blood pressure

**Differentiating questions:**
- Does she only feel breathless when she walks or exerts herself, or also when sitting or resting?
- Can she lie flat comfortably?
- Is the breathlessness mild and stable, or is it getting worse?
- Are there any other symptoms alongside the breathlessness?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Mild exertional breathlessness only
- Stable, not worsening
- Able to lie flat
- No associated symptoms
- No risk factors for serious cause

**Uncertainty note:**
Physiological breathlessness of pregnancy cannot be diagnosed remotely. Any woman with breathlessness that is at rest, worsening, or associated with any other symptom must be escalated. Do not apply this diagnosis without facility assessment excluding serious causes.

---

### GENERAL UNCERTAINTY RULE — DIFFICULTY BREATHING IN PREGNANCY

Any breathlessness at rest, sudden onset breathlessness, breathlessness with chest pain or fever, and any breathlessness that is worsening must be escalated immediately. Physiological breathlessness of pregnancy is a diagnosis of exclusion that cannot be made remotely. When the cause of breathlessness in a pregnant woman cannot be clearly and safely identified, escalate without delay.
