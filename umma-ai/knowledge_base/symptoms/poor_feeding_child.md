## SECTION 1: SYMPTOM VERIFICATION

### Confirming the symptom
Poor feeding means the child is drinking, breastfeeding, or eating significantly
less than usual — or refusing entirely.

If not clearly stated, ask ONE of:
- "Is the child refusing to feed or drink — or taking much less than usual?"
- "Has the child stopped breastfeeding or drinking when they normally would?"

Establish age immediately if not stated: "How old is the child?"
- In a child under 2 months — poor feeding is always a red flag. Escalate urgently.

### Distinguish between:
- Refusing completely — unable to drink or breastfeed at all — always red flag
- Drinking less than usual — concerning, needs assessment
- Eating less solid food but drinking fluids normally — less urgent but still
  needs evaluation

## SECTION 2: TRIAGE

### QUESTIONING RULES FOR TRIAGE
- Ask ONE question at a time only
- Maximum 3 triage questions before moving to disease reasoning
- If red flag already stated — escalate immediately
- Acknowledge caregiver warmly before each question

### RED FLAGS — Escalate immediately if any present
- Child under 2 months refusing to breastfeed or drink at all
- Child completely unable to swallow — drooling, choking on feeds
- Child unconscious or very difficult to rouse
- Poor feeding with very fast or difficult breathing
- Poor feeding with high fever and child looking severely unwell
- Poor feeding with seizure
- Poor feeding with sunken eyes, dry mouth, no tears — severe dehydration
- Newborn who has never fed well since birth — within first week

### Triage question priority order
Ask only what is not already known.

Priority 1: "Is the child able to swallow anything at all — even a small
             amount of water or breast milk?"
Priority 2: "Does the child have a fever or is the child breathing fast
             or with difficulty?"
Priority 3: "How old is the child and how long has the poor feeding
             been going on?"

### IF ANY RED FLAG CONFIRMED — Escalate immediately. Do not proceed.
### IF NO RED FLAGS AFTER MAXIMUM 3 QUESTIONS — Proceed to Section 3.

## SECTION 3: CLINICAL REASONING GUIDE

### QUESTIONING RULES FOR DISEASE REASONING
- Ask ONE question at a time only
- Maximum 4 questions total across all disease reasoning
- Use answers already given — do not re-ask
- If pattern already clear — reason confidently
- If unclear after 4 questions — apply uncertainty rule

### NIGERIAN / KANO CONTEXT NOTE
Poor feeding in a child under 5 in Kano is almost never trivial. It is a
common final pathway for many serious illnesses — malaria, pneumonia, sepsis,
meningitis, severe malnutrition, and neonatal sepsis all cause poor feeding.
Malnutrition affects 62% of under-5 children in Kano, making them more
vulnerable to deterioration from any illness that reduces feeding. In neonates
and young infants, poor feeding is one of the most important danger signs of
serious infection.

---

### DISEASE 1: SYSTEMIC ILLNESS — MALARIA, SEPSIS, PNEUMONIA
Nigerian priority: HIGHEST — poor feeding with fever in any child
is serious until proven otherwise

Key differentiating features:
- Fever present
- Child looks unwell — not just fussy but genuinely ill
- Reduced activity and alertness alongside poor feeding
- May have other symptoms — cough, vomiting, diarrhoea

Differentiating questions:
- "Does the child have a fever along with the poor feeding?"
- "Is the child looking unwell — unusually quiet, weak, or not interested
   in surroundings?"
- "Are there any other symptoms — cough, diarrhoea, vomiting, or fast
   breathing?"

Confirm towards systemic illness if:
- Poor feeding + fever + reduced alertness or activity
- Poor feeding + any other illness symptom in a child under 5

Uncertainty note:
- Poor feeding + fever in any child — treat as serious and escalate
- Do not wait to identify the specific illness before advising clinic visit

---

### DISEASE 2: NEONATAL SEPSIS — IN CHILD UNDER 28 DAYS
Nigerian priority: HIGHEST in newborns — leading cause of neonatal death

Key differentiating features:
- Age under 28 days
- Feeding well at birth then gradually or suddenly stopping
- Baby looks yellow (jaundice)
- Baby feels cold to touch or has fever
- Umbilical cord area red, swollen, or discharging
- Baby very sleepy — difficult to wake for feeds
- Baby's cry has changed — high-pitched or very weak
- Breathing fast or with difficulty

Differentiating questions:
- "How old is the baby — and was the baby feeding well before this started?"
- "Is the baby very sleepy — hard to wake up even for feeds?"
- "Is there any redness or discharge around the belly button?"

Confirm towards neonatal sepsis if:
- Age under 28 days + stopped feeding well + lethargic or yellow or
  abnormal breathing

Uncertainty note:
- Any newborn under 28 days who is feeding poorly — escalate immediately
- Neonatal sepsis kills within hours — never advise home management
- Signs may be subtle — trust caregiver's instinct that baby is not right

---

### DISEASE 3: SEVERE MALNUTRITION
Nigerian priority: HIGH in Kano — 62% prevalence of malnutrition under 5

Key differentiating features:
- Gradual onset — feeding has been poor for weeks or months
- Visible wasting — ribs showing, very thin limbs, thin face
- Swelling of feet, legs, or face — kwashiorkor
- Hair changes — thin, reddish, or falling out
- Child apathetic — little interest in surroundings
- No fever unless concurrent infection

Differentiating questions:
- "Has the poor feeding been going on for many weeks — not just a few days?"
- "Does the child look very thin — can you see the ribs or have the feet
   or face become swollen?"

Confirm towards severe malnutrition if:
- Weeks of poor feeding + visible wasting or oedema of feet

Uncertainty note:
- Severely malnourished child with any illness — escalate urgently
- Malnutrition and infection coexist very commonly in Kano
- Any child with visible severe wasting needs clinic assessment even
  without other symptoms

---

### DISEASE 4: LOCAL CAUSE — MOUTH SORE OR THROAT PAIN
Nigerian priority: MEDIUM

Key differentiating features:
- Child tries to feed but cries or pulls away
- Drooling more than usual
- Visible sores or white patches inside mouth
- No fever or low-grade fever only
- Child otherwise alert and active

Differentiating questions:
- "Does the child try to feed but then cry or pull away as if it is painful?"
- "Can you see any sores or white patches inside the child's mouth?"

Confirm towards mouth problem if:
- Trying to feed + crying with swallowing + visible mouth sores
- White patches inside mouth in infant — possible oral thrush

Uncertainty note:
- Oral thrush in an infant — usually manageable but needs clinic confirmation
- If child cannot swallow at all due to throat pain — escalate urgently
- Throat swelling with difficulty swallowing — always escalate

---

### GENERAL UNCERTAINTY RULE
- Poor feeding in a child under 2 months — always escalate urgently
- Poor feeding with fever — always escalate same day
- Poor feeding with any other danger sign — emergency
- If cause unclear after maximum questions — advise urgent clinic visit
- Never advise home management for poor feeding in a newborn
- When in doubt — escalate
