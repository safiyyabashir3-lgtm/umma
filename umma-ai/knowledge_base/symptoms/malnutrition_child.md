## For UMMA AI Clinical Reasoning — Nigerian Context (Kano / Northwest Priority)

---

## SECTION 1: SYMPTOM VERIFICATION

### Confirming the symptom
Caregivers may not use the word malnutrition. They may describe the child as
"not growing," "too thin," "swollen," "weak," or "not eating well for a long time."

If not clearly stated, ask ONE of:
- "Has the child been losing weight or not growing as expected for a long time?"
- "Does the child look very thin, or have the feet or face become swollen?"

Establish age: "How old is the child?"
Establish duration: "How long has the child been like this?"

### Symptoms that may be confused with malnutrition
- Short-term illness causing weight loss — resolves with treatment
- Constitutional small child — small but proportionate, active, feeding well
- Swelling from other causes — kidney disease, heart disease

## SECTION 2: TRIAGE

### QUESTIONING RULES FOR TRIAGE
- Ask ONE question at a time only
- Maximum 3 triage questions before moving to disease reasoning
- If red flag already stated — escalate immediately
- Acknowledge caregiver warmly before each question

### RED FLAGS — Escalate immediately if any present
- Child unable to eat or drink anything at all
- Child unconscious or very difficult to rouse
- Severe swelling of both feet AND face together
- Child breathing very fast or with difficulty
- Child with visible severe wasting AND fever
- Child with severe wasting AND diarrhoea
- Severe wasting in a child under 6 months

### Triage question priority order
Ask only what is not already known.

Priority 1: "Is the child able to eat or drink anything — even small amounts?"
Priority 2: "Does the child have a fever or fast breathing along with the
             thinness or swelling?"
Priority 3: "Is there swelling of both feet?"

### IF ANY RED FLAG CONFIRMED — Escalate immediately. Do not proceed.
### IF NO RED FLAGS AFTER MAXIMUM 3 QUESTIONS — Proceed to Section 3.

## SECTION 3: CLINICAL REASONING GUIDE

### QUESTIONING RULES FOR DISEASE REASONING
- Ask ONE question at a time only
- Maximum 4 questions total across all disease reasoning
- Use answers already given — do not re-ask
- If pattern already clear — reason confidently
- If unclear after 4 questions — apply uncertainty rule

### NIGERIAN / KANO CONTEXT NOTE
Malnutrition affects 62% of under-5 children in Kano — the highest burden
in Nigeria. It is both a disease and a major amplifier of every other illness.
Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) is life-threatening and requires hospital or
therapeutic feeding centre treatment. Kano has therapeutic feeding centres
that caregivers may not be aware of. Early identification is critical.
Common contributing factors in Kano include: early cessation of breastfeeding,
poor weaning diet, repeated infections, poverty, and large family size.

### DISEASE 1: SEVERE ACUTE MALNUTRITION — MARASMUS TYPE (SEVERE WASTING)
Nigerian priority: HIGHEST

Key differentiating features:
- Severe wasting — child looks like skin on bones
- Ribs, shoulder blades, and hip bones very prominent
- Loose skin — especially on the buttocks, described as baggy trousers
- Face looks old — sunken cheeks, prominent cheekbones
- Arms and legs very thin
- Child may be alert but weak, or apathetic
- No oedema (swelling)
- Weight very low for age

Differentiating questions:
- "Can you clearly see the child's ribs and are the buttocks very flat
   and loose-skinned?"
- "Has the child's weight been dropping or has the child always been
   this thin?"
- "Is the child eating anything at all — or refusing food completely?"
- "Are there any other children at home also looking thin?"

Confirm towards severe wasting if:
- Visible severe wasting with loose skin + very low weight for age +
  poor feeding

Uncertainty note:
- Severe wasting with any illness — always escalate urgently
- Severely wasted child is immunocompromised — any fever must be taken
  very seriously
- Therapeutic feeding is required — cannot be managed at home alone

---

### DISEASE 2: SEVERE ACUTE MALNUTRITION — KWASHIORKOR TYPE (OEDEMATOUS)
Nigerian priority: HIGH

Key differentiating features:
- Swelling of both feet — symmetrical, pitting oedema
- Swelling may extend to legs, hands, and face in severe cases
- Child may not look very thin — oedema masks wasting
- Hair changes — thin, reddish or light-coloured, falls out easily
- Skin changes — peeling, dark patches, cracked skin
- Apathetic — very quiet, unresponsive to play
- Miserable expression — child looks sad and withdrawn
- Poor appetite

Differentiating questions:
- "Are both feet swollen — and does the swelling remain when you press
   the foot for a few seconds?"
- "Has the child's hair changed — become lighter, thinner, or started
   falling out?"
- "Is the child very quiet and sad — not interested in playing or
   in surroundings?"

Confirm towards kwashiorkor if:
- Bilateral pitting foot oedema + hair changes + apathy + skin changes

Uncertainty note:
- Kwashiorkor with any infection — always escalate urgently
- Oedema may hide true weight — child may not look as ill as they are
- Kwashiorkor carries high mortality if not treated promptly

---

### DISEASE 3: MODERATE ACUTE MALNUTRITION
Nigerian priority: HIGH given Kano prevalence

Key differentiating features:
- Child visibly thin but not severely wasted
- Mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) in yellow or red zone if measured
- Feeding poorly but still taking some food
- Repeated infections — frequent malaria, diarrhoea, chest infections
- Not gaining weight or growing
- No bilateral foot oedema

Differentiating questions:
- "Has the child been having repeated illnesses — fever, diarrhoea, or
   chest problems — over the past few months?"
- "Is the child eating — but still not gaining weight or growing?"

Confirm towards moderate malnutrition if:
- Visible thinness + repeated infections + poor growth + no oedema

Uncertainty note:
- Moderate malnutrition with any acute illness — needs same-day clinic
  assessment
- Transition to severe malnutrition can happen quickly with any illness

---

### GENERAL UNCERTAINTY RULE
- Any visible severe wasting or bilateral foot swelling — escalate urgently
- Any malnourished child with fever, diarrhoea, or fast breathing —
  escalate as emergency
- Never advise home management alone for severe acute malnutrition
- Kano has therapeutic feeding centres — refer when appropriate
- When in doubt about severity — escalate. The consequences of delayed
  treatment are irreversible growth and developmental harm.
