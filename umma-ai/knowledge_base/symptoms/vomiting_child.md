## SECTION 1: SYMPTOM VERIFICATION

### Confirming the symptom
Vomiting is forceful expulsion of stomach contents. Caregivers may say the
child is "throwing up," "bringing up food," or "cannot keep anything down."

Distinguish from:
- Posseting / spitting up — small amounts after feeds in infants, normal
- Regurgitation — effortless, small amounts, infant otherwise well

If not clearly stated, ask ONE of:
- "Is the child forcefully bringing up food or liquid — more than just
  a small amount after feeding?"
- "Is the child unable to keep anything down at all?"

Establish age immediately: "How old is the child?"
- Vomiting in a child under 2 months — always escalate urgently

## SECTION 2: TRIAGE

### QUESTIONING RULES FOR TRIAGE
- Ask ONE question at a time only
- Maximum 3 triage questions before moving to disease reasoning
- If red flag already stated — escalate immediately
- Acknowledge caregiver warmly before each question

### RED FLAGS — Escalate immediately if any present
- Child unable to keep any fluid down at all — vomiting everything
- Signs of dehydration — sunken eyes, dry mouth, no tears, sunken
  fontanelle in infant
- Blood in vomit — red or dark brown coffee-ground appearance
- Green vomit — bilious vomiting, especially in newborn
- Vomiting with severe abdominal pain and distension
- Vomiting with fever and child looking very unwell
- Vomiting with seizure
- Vomiting with altered consciousness — very sleepy or unresponsive
- Child under 2 months with persistent vomiting
- Projectile vomiting in infant under 3 months after every feed

### Triage question priority order
Ask only what is not already known.

Priority 1: "Is the child able to keep anything down at all — even small
             sips of water?"
Priority 2: "Is there any blood in the vomit — or is it green coloured?"
Priority 3: "Does the child have a fever or any pain in the tummy along
             with the vomiting?"

### IF ANY RED FLAG CONFIRMED — Escalate immediately. Do not proceed.
### IF NO RED FLAGS AFTER MAXIMUM 3 QUESTIONS — Proceed to Section 3.

### NIGERIAN / KANO CONTEXT NOTE
Vomiting in a child under 5 in Kano is most commonly a feature of malaria,
gastroenteritis, or serious systemic infection — it is rarely an isolated
symptom. Dehydration from vomiting combined with diarrhoea is the most
dangerous outcome. Malaria must always be considered first in any febrile
vomiting child. Pyloric stenosis, though less common, must be considered
in infants under 3 months with projectile vomiting after every feed.

---

### DISEASE 1: MALARIA WITH VOMITING
Nigerian priority: HIGHEST — vomiting is one of the most common
presentations of malaria in Nigerian children

Key differentiating features:
- Fever present — often with chills and sweating
- Vomiting with or after fever spikes
- Child looks unwell between vomiting episodes
- No diarrhoea or mild diarrhoea only
- Poor mosquito net use

Differentiating questions:
- "Does the child have a fever along with the vomiting?"
- "Is the child shivering or having chills at times?"
- "Does the child sleep under a mosquito net every night?"
- "Is there also diarrhoea — or is it mainly vomiting?"

Confirm towards malaria if:
- Vomiting + fever + chills or sweating + no mosquito net
- Vomiting + fever + no clear gastrointestinal pattern

Uncertainty note:
- Malaria with vomiting that prevents oral medication — always escalate
- Vomiting with fever in any child — always treat as serious

---

### DISEASE 2: GASTROENTERITIS — VOMITING WITH DIARRHOEA
Nigerian priority: HIGH

Key differentiating features:
- Vomiting AND diarrhoea together
- Watery stool
- Low-grade fever or no fever
- Signs of dehydration developing
- Contact with unsafe water or food

Differentiating questions:
- "Is there also loose or watery stool along with the vomiting?"
- "Has the child been drinking from any water that may not be clean?"

Confirm towards gastroenteritis if:
- Vomiting + watery diarrhoea + low or no fever

Uncertainty note:
- Dehydration from combined vomiting and diarrhoea — escalate if child
  cannot keep oral rehydration solution down
- Any signs of dehydration — escalate immediately

---

### DISEASE 3: SERIOUS SYSTEMIC INFECTION — SEPSIS, TYPHOID, MENINGITIS
Nigerian priority: HIGH — vomiting with fever and lethargy in Kano

Key differentiating features:
- High fever
- Child looks very unwell — beyond just feverish
- Lethargy — unusual stillness, not responding normally
- Vomiting persistent — not settling
- Headache in older child
- Neck stiffness — suspect meningitis
- Fever beyond 3 days with vomiting — suspect typhoid

Differentiating questions:
- "Is the child looking very weak and lifeless — not moving or
   responding as usual?"
- "How many days has the fever and vomiting been going on?"

Confirm towards serious infection if:
- Vomiting + high fever + lethargy + fever beyond 3 days
- Vomiting + fever + neck stiffness — escalate immediately for meningitis

Uncertainty note:
- Vomiting + fever + lethargy — always escalate urgently
- Do not attempt to differentiate cause in community if child looks
  severely unwell — escalate immediately

---

### DISEASE 4: PYLORIC STENOSIS — IN INFANT UNDER 3 MONTHS
Nigerian priority: MEDIUM — must not be missed

Key differentiating features:
- Age 2 to 8 weeks most commonly
- Projectile vomiting — forceful, shoots across the room
- Vomiting immediately after every feed
- Baby hungry again immediately after vomiting
- No fever
- Baby losing weight or not gaining
- Vomiting not improving — worsening over days

Differentiating questions:
- "How old is the baby — and is the vomiting happening after every single feed?"
- "Does the vomit shoot out forcefully — like it is being thrown?"

Confirm towards pyloric stenosis if:
- Age under 3 months + projectile vomiting after every feed +
  hungry immediately after + no fever + worsening

Uncertainty note:
- Pyloric stenosis requires surgical treatment — always escalate
- Dehydration develops quickly in these infants — do not delay

---

### GENERAL UNCERTAINTY RULE
- Vomiting with fever in any child — treat as serious, advise urgent
  clinic visit
- Vomiting preventing any fluid intake — always escalate immediately
- If cause unclear after maximum questions — advise urgent clinic visit
- Never advise home management if child cannot keep fluids down
- When in doubt — escalate
