## SECTION 1: SYMPTOM VERIFICATION

### Confirming the symptom
If not clearly stated, ask ONE of:
- "Is the child passing watery or loose stools more than usual?"
- "How many times has the child passed stool today — and is it watery?"

Establish age immediately if not stated: "How old is the child?"
- Child under 2 months with diarrhoea and any danger sign — escalate immediately.

### Symptoms that may be confused with diarrhoea
- Breastfed newborns — may pass soft yellow stool 6 to 8 times daily. Normal
  if child feeding well and gaining weight.
- Change in stool colour alone without increased frequency or wateriness —
  usually not diarrhoea

## SECTION 2: TRIAGE

### QUESTIONING RULES FOR TRIAGE
- Ask ONE question at a time only
- Maximum 3 triage questions before moving to disease reasoning
- If red flag information already given — escalate immediately
- Acknowledge caregiver warmly before each question
- Never repeat information already given

### RED FLAGS — Escalate immediately if any present
- Child unconscious or cannot be woken
- Child unable to drink or breastfeed at all
- Sunken eyes AND dry mouth AND no tears when crying — severe dehydration
- Skin pinch goes back very slowly — more than 2 seconds
- Blood in stool with fever and child looking very unwell
- Child under 2 months with diarrhoea and any of the above
- Diarrhoea with seizure
- Child breathing very fast or with difficulty alongside diarrhoea
- Vomiting everything given — cannot keep any fluid down

### Triage question priority order
Ask only what is not already known.

Priority 1: "Is the child able to drink — or refusing everything you offer?"
Priority 2: "Are the child's eyes looking sunken or dry, and is the mouth
             and tongue very dry?"
Priority 3: "Is there any blood in the stool?"

### IF ANY RED FLAG CONFIRMED — Escalate immediately. Do not proceed.
### IF NO RED FLAGS AFTER MAXIMUM 3 QUESTIONS — Proceed to Section 3.

## SECTION 3: CLINICAL REASONING GUIDE

### QUESTIONING RULES FOR DISEASE REASONING
- Ask ONE question at a time only
- Maximum 4 questions total across all disease reasoning
- Use answers already given — do not re-ask
- If pattern already clear — reason confidently without asking all 4 questions
- If unclear after 4 questions — apply uncertainty rule

### NIGERIAN / KANO CONTEXT NOTE
Diarrhoea is the second leading cause of death in Nigerian children under 5.
Dehydration from diarrhoea kills quickly — assessment of hydration status is
the most critical step. In Kano, poor sanitation, unsafe water, and high
malnutrition rates increase both incidence and severity. Rotavirus is the
leading viral cause. Salmonella and Shigella are important bacterial causes.
Cholera must be considered during outbreak periods. Malnourished children
dehydrate faster and die sooner — escalate earlier.

---

### DISEASE 1: ACUTE WATERY DIARRHOEA — VIRAL (ROTAVIRUS AND OTHERS)
Nigerian priority: HIGHEST — most common cause of diarrhoea under 5

Key differentiating features:
- Profuse watery stool — no blood
- Vomiting common at onset
- Fever low-grade or absent
- Rapid dehydration risk
- Age under 2 years most affected
- No mucus in stool

Differentiating questions:
- "How many times has the child passed watery stool today?"
- "Is there any vomiting along with the diarrhoea?"
- "Is there any blood or mucus in the stool?"
- "How long has this been going on?"

Confirm towards viral watery diarrhoea if:
- Watery stool with no blood + vomiting + age under 2 years
- Rapid onset, no fever or low fever

Uncertainty note:
- Dehydration assessment is more important than identifying the cause
- Even if cause seems mild — if child cannot drink, escalate immediately

---

### DISEASE 2: BACTERIAL DIARRHOEA — SHIGELLA / DYSENTERY
Nigerian priority: HIGH

Key differentiating features:
- Blood and mucus in stool — key distinguishing feature
- Fever often high
- Crampy abdominal pain
- Child straining to pass stool
- Child looks unwell and in discomfort

Differentiating questions:
- "Is there blood or slime in the stool?"
- "Does the child have a fever and tummy pain along with the diarrhoea?"

Confirm towards bacterial dysentery if:
- Bloody stool + mucus + fever + abdominal cramps

Uncertainty note:
- Blood in stool in a child with fever — always escalate. Never manage at home.
- Shigella dysentery can cause seizures in children — escalate promptly

---

### DISEASE 3: CHOLERA
Nigerian priority: HIGH during outbreak periods — Kano has experienced
cholera outbreaks

Key differentiating features:
- Profuse watery stool — described as rice water in appearance
- Rapid and severe dehydration — within hours
- Vomiting present
- Little or no fever
- Sudden onset
- Other family or community members affected
- Unsafe water source or outbreak in the area

Differentiating questions:
- "Is anyone else in the family or neighbourhood also having diarrhoea?"
- "Is the stool very watery — almost like water itself?"

Confirm towards cholera if:
- Rice-water stool + rapid severe dehydration + community outbreak
- Profuse watery diarrhoea with rapid deterioration in any age

Uncertainty note:
- Cholera dehydrates a child fatally within hours — escalate immediately
- During any known outbreak — any profuse watery diarrhoea is cholera
  until proven otherwise

---

### DISEASE 4: DIARRHOEA WITH SEVERE MALNUTRITION
Nigerian priority: HIGH in Kano — 62% of under-5 children have malnutrition

Key differentiating features:
- Visible wasting — ribs showing, limbs very thin
- Swelling of feet or face — kwashiorkor pattern
- Persistent diarrhoea — lasting more than 14 days
- Child lethargic and weak
- Poor appetite
- Hair changes — thin, pale, or falling out
- Skin changes — peeling, dark patches

Differentiating questions:
- "Has the diarrhoea been going on for more than two weeks?"
- "Does the child look very thin — can you see the ribs, or are the feet
  or face swollen?"

Confirm towards diarrhoea with malnutrition if:
- Persistent diarrhoea more than 14 days + visible wasting or oedema

Uncertainty note:
- Malnourished child with diarrhoea — always escalate. Dehydration is
  life-threatening faster in these children.
- Persistent diarrhoea beyond 14 days always needs clinic assessment

---

### GENERAL UNCERTAINTY RULE
- If diarrhoea present and cause unclear — assess dehydration first
- Any sign of dehydration — escalate immediately
- Child unable to drink — always emergency regardless of cause
- When in doubt — escalate. Diarrhoea kills through dehydration, not the
  infection itself in most cases.
- Malnourished child with diarrhoea — always escalate sooner
