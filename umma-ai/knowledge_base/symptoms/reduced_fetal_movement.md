# UMMA Symptom File: Reduced or Absent Fetal Movement

---

## SECTION 1: SYMPTOM VERIFICATION

**Confirm the symptom:**
- Ask the woman when she last felt the baby move and whether the movements feel different from usual
- Confirm gestational age — fetal movement is typically felt from 18–20 weeks in first-time mothers and from 16–18 weeks in women who have been pregnant before
- Ask what is normal for this baby — every baby has its own movement pattern; a significant reduction from the baby's usual pattern is what matters
- Ask whether she has tried to count movements or has done a kick count
- Ask whether she has been very active or busy today — women sometimes miss movements when distracted

**Commonly confused with:**
- Baby in a quiet sleep cycle — fetuses have sleep cycles of 20–40 minutes; one quiet period is not reduced fetal movement
- Mother too busy to notice — but this must not be used to reassure; the symptom should always be taken seriously
- Normal reduction in movement at term — there is no normal reduction in movement at any point; movement should be maintained until labour

**Critical immediate checks:**
- Ask immediately: when did she last feel movement — was it today?
- Ask: has movement completely stopped, or just reduced?
- Ask: has she had any abdominal pain, vaginal bleeding, or fever alongside this?
- Completely absent movement for more than 12 hours is an emergency regardless of any other factor

---

## SECTION 2: TRIAGE — RED FLAGS

Escalate immediately if any of the following are present:

1. No fetal movement felt for 12 hours or more
2. Complete absence of fetal movement at any stage after 24 weeks
3. Reduced fetal movement with abdominal pain — placental abruption
4. Reduced fetal movement with vaginal bleeding
5. Reduced fetal movement with fever
6. Reduced fetal movement in a woman with diabetes, hypertension, or pre-eclampsia — very high risk for fetal compromise
7. Reduced fetal movement in a woman with known intrauterine growth restriction (small baby on scan)
8. Reduced fetal movement in a post-dates pregnancy (beyond 40 weeks)
9. Reduced fetal movement with decreased or absent liquor (woman reports leaking fluid)
10. Reduced fetal movement in a woman who had normal movements until very recently — sudden change
11. Baby's movements have changed character — previously strong, now weak or flutter-only
12. Woman has a strong feeling that something is wrong — maternal instinct is a valid clinical indicator in Nigerian and WHO guidelines
13. Second or third episode of reduced fetal movement in the same pregnancy

---

## SECTION 3: CLINICAL REASONING GUIDE

---

### DISEASE 1: FETAL DISTRESS / ACUTE UTEROPLACENTAL INSUFFICIENCY
**Nigerian priority level: HIGHEST**

Acute fetal distress from sudden reduction in placental blood flow or oxygen supply is the most dangerous cause of reduced fetal movement. It requires immediate delivery to prevent stillbirth. Causes include abruption, cord accident, severe maternal illness, and acute hypertensive crisis.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Sudden onset — baby was moving normally and movements abruptly stopped or dramatically decreased
- Associated with maternal illness: fever, hypertension crisis, severe malaria, dehydration
- Associated with abdominal pain or bleeding
- Baby's movements have changed in character — previously vigorous, now absent or very weak
- Post-dates pregnancy (beyond 40 weeks)

**Differentiating questions:**
- Was the baby moving normally yesterday or this morning and then stopped suddenly?
- Is she having any abdominal pain or bleeding alongside this?
- Has she been unwell — fever, feeling very ill, or dizziness?
- Is she past her due date?
- Has her blood pressure been checked recently?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Sudden cessation of movement after period of normal activity
- Associated maternal illness or pain
- Post-dates pregnancy
- Previously vigorous movements now absent

**Uncertainty note:**
Any sudden or significant reduction in fetal movement is fetal distress until proven otherwise at a facility with cardiotocography or Doppler assessment. Never reassure based on history alone.

---

### DISEASE 2: CHRONIC UTEROPLACENTAL INSUFFICIENCY / INTRAUTERINE GROWTH RESTRICTION
**Nigerian priority level: HIGH**

Chronic placental insufficiency causes progressive fetal compromise and growth restriction. The baby gradually becomes less active as it conserves oxygen. It is associated with hypertension, pre-eclampsia, maternal malnutrition, anaemia, and malaria.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Gradual reduction in movement over days to weeks rather than sudden cessation
- Baby has been moving less for several days
- Woman may report baby feels smaller or that her abdomen does not seem to be growing
- Risk factors: hypertension, pre-eclampsia, chronic anaemia, malaria, diabetes, poor nutritional status
- Previous pregnancy with a small baby
- Fundal height smaller than expected for dates

**Differentiating questions:**
- Has the baby been moving less for several days, or did it stop suddenly?
- Has she been told the baby is small at any scan?
- Does she have high blood pressure, diabetes, or anaemia?
- Has she had malaria or been unwell recently?
- Was her previous baby small?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Gradual reduction in movement over days
- Known risk factors for IUGR
- Previous small baby
- Signs of maternal illness contributing to poor placental function

**Uncertainty note:**
Chronic IUGR with reduced movement represents a baby in danger. Gradual reduction in movement is not reassuring — it requires urgent facility assessment for growth and wellbeing monitoring.

---

### DISEASE 3: STILLBIRTH (FETAL DEATH IN UTERO)
**Nigerian priority level: HIGHEST**

Nigeria has one of the highest stillbirth rates globally. Fetal death in utero presents with complete cessation of fetal movement. Early recognition and appropriate management are essential to prevent maternal complications including infection and coagulopathy.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Complete absence of fetal movement for an extended period
- Woman may report the abdomen feels different — softer, or the baby has not moved at all today or yesterday
- No fetal heartbeat detectable if Doppler available
- May follow a period of reduced movement over several days
- Risk factors: prolonged pregnancy, severe malaria, untreated pre-eclampsia, diabetes, prior stillbirth, recurrent anaemia

**Differentiating questions:**
- When exactly did she last feel the baby move?
- Has she felt any movement at all today?
- Has she noticed anything different about how her abdomen feels?
- Has she had a previous stillbirth?
- Has she been very unwell or untreated for any condition recently?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Complete absence of movement for 12 hours or more
- No fetal heartbeat detected
- History of preceding period of reduced movement
- High-risk pregnancy factors present

**Uncertainty note:**
UMMA cannot confirm fetal death. Any complete absence of fetal movement requires emergency facility attendance for fetal heart auscultation and assessment. Do not delay.

---

### DISEASE 4: CORD ACCIDENT (UMBILICAL CORD COMPRESSION OR PROLAPSE)
**Nigerian priority level: HIGHEST**

Umbilical cord accidents — including cord prolapse, cord entanglement, or true knot — can cause sudden acute fetal compromise. Cord prolapse (cord descending through the cervix) is an obstetric emergency that must be managed within minutes.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Sudden cessation of fetal movement
- May follow rupture of membranes (cord prolapse most common when membranes break)
- No maternal systemic illness — baby stops moving in an otherwise well mother
- Cord prolapse: woman may feel or see the cord at the vaginal opening

**Differentiating questions:**
- Did her waters break before the movement stopped?
- Has she felt or seen anything unusual at the vaginal opening?
- Was the baby moving well and then stopped suddenly without any other symptom?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Sudden movement cessation following membrane rupture
- Nothing felt or seen at vaginal opening
- Otherwise well mother with acute fetal compromise

**Uncertainty note:**
Cord prolapse requires delivery within minutes to save the fetal life. Any sudden movement cessation after membrane rupture is cord prolapse until excluded. Escalate as extreme emergency.

---

### DISEASE 5: SEVERE MATERNAL ILLNESS AFFECTING FETAL OXYGENATION
**Nigerian priority level: HIGHEST**

Severe maternal illness including severe malaria, sepsis, severe anaemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, and eclampsia reduces fetal oxygen delivery and causes acute reduction in fetal movement. The fetal compromise is secondary to maternal collapse.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Reduced fetal movement occurring alongside maternal illness
- Mother unwell: high fever, convulsions, severe weakness, or collapse
- Known diabetes, severe anaemia, or pre-eclampsia
- Severe malaria: reduced consciousness, very high fever, rigors

**Differentiating questions:**
- Is the mother herself unwell alongside the reduced movement?
- Does she have fever, convulsions, or severe weakness?
- Does she have diabetes or severe anaemia?
- Has she had malaria recently or is she having symptoms of malaria now?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Reduced movement concurrent with maternal illness
- Severe malaria, sepsis, or hypertensive crisis
- Diabetes or severe anaemia with poor control

**Uncertainty note:**
When the mother is severely ill and fetal movement is reduced, both maternal and fetal emergencies coexist. Escalate both simultaneously.

---

### DISEASE 6: FETAL SLEEP CYCLE / NORMAL QUIET PERIOD
**Nigerian priority level: MEDIUM**

Fetuses have sleep cycles lasting 20–40 minutes during which movement is minimal or absent. This is a normal phenomenon. However, it is a diagnosis of exclusion and must only be applied with caution.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Movement absent for less than one hour
- Baby moved well earlier in the day
- No associated maternal symptoms
- Movement resumes after the woman drinks cold water, eats, or focuses attention on the baby
- This diagnosis is only appropriate if the quiet period is brief and movement returns promptly

**Differentiating questions:**
- Has the baby moved at all today — even once?
- Did the baby move well this morning?
- Has she tried drinking cold water or lying on her left side to check for movement?
- Has she counted kicks and reached 10 in 2 hours?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Quiet period lasting less than one hour
- Clear prior movement today
- Movement returns after stimulation (cold drink, lying on left side)
- No other symptoms

**Uncertainty note:**
A sleep cycle lasting more than one hour, or any quiet period in a high-risk pregnancy, should never be attributed to sleep without facility assessment. If movement does not return within one hour of the woman focusing on kick counting, escalate.

---

### GENERAL UNCERTAINTY RULE — REDUCED FETAL MOVEMENT

Reduced or absent fetal movement is a potential sign of fetal compromise or death and must always be taken seriously. There is no safe threshold of reduced movement that can be reassured over remotely. Any woman who reports her baby is moving less than usual, or who is worried about her baby's movements, must be escalated to a facility for fetal heart auscultation and assessment. Maternal concern alone is sufficient reason to escalate. When in doubt, the baby must be assessed.
