**File ID:** symptom_urinary_complaints_pregnancy
**Version:** 1.0

## SECTION 1: SYMPTOM VERIFICATION

### What counts as a urinary complaint in pregnancy?
Urinary complaints include any of the following reported by a pregnant woman:
- Pain, burning, or stinging when passing urine (dysuria)
- Passing urine very frequently (more often than usual)
- Urgency — a sudden strong need to urinate that is hard to control
- Urine that looks cloudy, dark, smelly, or blood-stained
- Feeling that the bladder is not fully empty after urinating
- Reduced urine output or difficulty passing urine
- Lower abdominal or pelvic pain or pressure associated with urination

### Commonly confused symptoms — verify carefully
- **Vaginal discharge onto the perineum** may be mistaken for urinary leakage or dysuria — confirm whether discomfort is felt inside the urethra or externally
- **Amniotic fluid leakage** may be mistaken for urinary incontinence — ask whether the fluid is continuous or trickling and whether it has an odour
- **Normal pregnancy urinary frequency** (especially first and third trimester) — this alone without dysuria or other features is not pathological, but must be distinguished from symptomatic UTI
- **Vaginal pain or vulval irritation** may be reported as "pain when passing urine" — clarify whether pain is felt during the stream or only on skin contact
- **Pelvic girdle pain** may cause discomfort around the bladder area without true urinary symptoms

### Critical immediate checks
- **Gestational age** — establish immediately; UTI in any trimester is dangerous, but pyelonephritis in second/third trimester carries high risk of preterm labour
- **Fever** — any urinary complaint with fever must be assessed for pyelonephritis urgently
- **Reduced or absent urine output** — may indicate acute kidney injury; escalate immediately
- **Suprapubic or flank/loin pain** — loin pain raises suspicion for upper urinary tract involvement

## SECTION 2: TRIAGE — RED FLAGS

The following findings require immediate escalation. Listed from most to least dangerous:

1. **No urine output or very little urine for several hours** — possible acute kidney injury or severe systemic illness
2. **High fever (feels very hot, shivering, rigors) with loin or back pain** — pyelonephritis with risk of sepsis and preterm labour
3. **Fever with urinary symptoms at any gestational age** — UTI with systemic involvement
4. **Loin pain (pain in the side or back below the ribs)** — upper urinary tract involvement; pyelonephritis must be excluded
5. **Blood in urine (haematuria) that is visible** — requires urgent assessment
6. **Urinary symptoms with signs of preterm labour** — contractions, pelvic pressure, back cramping before 37 weeks
7. **Confusion, drowsiness, or altered behaviour alongside urinary symptoms** — urosepsis
8. **Urinary symptoms with vomiting and inability to keep fluids down** — dehydration risk, systemic illness
9. **Known diabetes or previous kidney problems with new urinary symptoms** — higher risk of rapid progression
10. **Caregiver or woman herself feels something is seriously wrong** — uncertainty always escalates

## SECTION 3: CLINICAL REASONING GUIDE

### 3.1 Urinary Tract Infection — Uncomplicated (Lower UTI / Cystitis)
**Nigerian Priority Level: HIGHEST**
UTI is extremely common in pregnancy in Nigeria, with high rates of asymptomatic bacteriuria that can progress. *E. coli* is the commonest organism; *Klebsiella* and *Staphylococcus saprophyticus* also prevalent. Untreated UTI in pregnancy directly causes preterm labour, low birthweight, and maternal sepsis.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Dysuria, frequency, urgency — the classic triad
- Suprapubic discomfort or pressure
- Cloudy or smelly urine
- NO fever, or only low-grade fever
- NO loin pain
- Woman otherwise feels reasonably well

**Differentiating questions:**
- Is there burning or stinging specifically when urine is passing?
- How often is she urinating compared to her usual pattern in this pregnancy?
- Does the urine look different — cloudy, dark, or with a strong smell?
- Any lower tummy pain or pressure?
- Any fever, chills, or back pain?
- Has she had a UTI before in this pregnancy or previously?

**Confirm towards if:**
Dysuria + frequency/urgency + suprapubic discomfort + no fever + no loin pain + cloudy or smelly urine

**Uncertainty note:**
If dysuria is present but the full UTI picture is unclear, or if any fever is present, do not treat as simple lower UTI — escalate towards pyelonephritis pathway.

---

### 3.2 Pyelonephritis (Upper UTI / Kidney Infection)
**Nigerian Priority Level: HIGHEST — EMERGENCY**
Pyelonephritis is one of the most dangerous infections in pregnancy. In Nigeria it is a significant cause of maternal sepsis and preterm birth. It can develop rapidly from untreated lower UTI. Right-sided predominance due to uterine dextrorotation.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Loin pain — pain in the flank or back below the ribs, often one-sided (commonly right)
- High fever, rigors, chills
- Nausea and vomiting often present
- Dysuria and frequency may or may not be present
- Woman looks and feels seriously unwell
- Costovertebral angle tenderness (if assessed)

**Differentiating questions:**
- Is there pain in her back or side, below the ribs?
- Does she have fever — feeling very hot or shivering?
- Is she vomiting?
- How unwell does she feel overall?
- Did she have urinary symptoms (burning, frequency) before the back pain and fever started?

**Confirm towards if:**
Fever + loin/flank pain + nausea or vomiting + any urinary symptoms = pyelonephritis until proven otherwise

**Uncertainty note:**
Any pregnant woman with fever AND loin pain must be escalated as pyelonephritis regardless of whether urinary symptoms are present. Do not wait for confirmation.

### 3.3 Asymptomatic Bacteriuria
**Nigerian Priority Level: HIGH**
Asymptomatic bacteriuria (ASB) affects 2–10% of pregnant women in Nigeria and carries a 20–40% risk of progressing to pyelonephritis if untreated. By definition the woman reports no symptoms — this is flagged here because women presenting for routine care, or women reporting vague pelvic discomfort, may have ASB identified on urinalysis.

**Key differentiating features:**
- No dysuria, no frequency beyond normal pregnancy changes, no fever, no loin pain
- May report only vague lower abdominal discomfort or nothing at all
- Urine may appear normal to the woman
- Identified only on urinalysis/culture

**Differentiating questions:**
- Does she have any burning when urinating?
- Any change in how often she urinates beyond normal pregnancy?
- Has anyone tested her urine before in this pregnancy?
- Any back pain or fever at all?

**Confirm towards if:**
No or minimal symptoms + urine test showing infection = ASB

**Uncertainty note:**
If any symptoms are present at all alongside a positive urine test, treat as symptomatic UTI, not ASB.

---

### 3.4 Sexually Transmitted Infection — Gonorrhoea / Chlamydia with Urethral Involvement
**Nigerian Priority Level: MEDIUM-HIGH**
STIs including gonorrhoea and chlamydia are prevalent in northern Nigeria and may present with dysuria in pregnancy. Often co-exist with vaginal discharge. Frequently underdiagnosed due to stigma and limited testing.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Dysuria with prominent vaginal discharge (yellow, green, or purulent)
- May have vulval irritation or soreness
- Partner may have urethral symptoms
- No fever unless there is ascending infection
- Symptoms may overlap significantly with lower UTI

**Differentiating questions:**
- Is there vaginal discharge alongside the urinary symptoms?
- What does the discharge look like — colour, smell, amount?
- Has her partner had any symptoms such as pain or discharge?
- Has she had similar symptoms before?

**Confirm towards if:**
Dysuria + significant purulent or abnormal vaginal discharge + possible partner symptoms = consider STI

**Uncertainty note:**
STI and UTI can co-exist. If discharge is prominent, both pathways should be considered. Do not exclude STI because UTI symptoms are present.

---

### 3.5 Urinary Schistosomiasis
**Nigerian Priority Level: MEDIUM-HIGH in endemic areas**
Schistosoma haematobium is endemic in parts of northern Nigeria. Urinary schistosomiasis may present with haematuria (blood in urine), urinary frequency, and dysuria. Often under-recognised in pregnancy.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Haematuria — blood in urine, often at end of urinary stream (terminal haematuria)
- History of swimming, bathing, or wading in rivers, lakes, streams, or irrigation canals
- May have no other symptoms
- Chronic presentation — symptoms may have been present for some time before pregnancy

**Differentiating questions:**
- Is there blood in the urine? When does it appear — throughout or at the end of the stream?
- Does she or did she swim or bathe in rivers, ponds, or irrigation water?
- Did she grow up in a rural area near water sources?
- Has she had blood in her urine before, even before pregnancy?

**Confirm towards if:**
Haematuria + history of freshwater exposure + no fever or loin pain = consider schistosomiasis

**Uncertainty note:**
Haematuria in pregnancy always requires clinical assessment regardless of schistosomiasis history. Do not reassure about blood in urine.

---

### 3.6 Pre-eclampsia with Renal Involvement
**Nigerian Priority Level: HIGHEST after 20 weeks**
Reduced urine output, dark urine, or cessation of urination in a woman after 20 weeks of pregnancy must immediately raise concern for severe pre-eclampsia or eclampsia with renal involvement. This is a life-threatening emergency.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Reduced or absent urine output (oliguria or anuria)
- Headache, visual disturbance, or facial/hand swelling may accompany
- After 20 weeks gestation
- No urinary burning or discharge
- May have elevated blood pressure if measurable

**Differentiating questions:**
- How much urine has she passed in the last several hours?
- Any headache, blurred vision, or swelling of the face or hands?
- How many weeks pregnant is she?

**Confirm towards if:**
Reduced urine output + headache or swelling + after 20 weeks = pre-eclampsia with renal involvement — EMERGENCY

**Uncertainty note:**
Reduced urine output in the third trimester must never be attributed to dehydration alone without excluding pre-eclampsia.

---

### GENERAL UNCERTAINTY RULE
If the urinary complaint in a pregnant woman cannot be clearly categorised as simple lower UTI, or if any red flag is present, or if the clinical picture is mixed or unclear — **escalate**. Urinary complaints in pregnancy are never low-risk by default. When uncertain, treat as higher-acuity condition until proven otherwise.
