# UMMA Symptom File: Body Swelling in Pregnancy

---

## SECTION 1: SYMPTOM VERIFICATION

**Confirm the symptom:**
- Ask where the swelling is: feet and ankles only, legs, hands, face, or whole body
- Ask when it started, whether it has been getting worse, and whether it is present in the morning (after lying down) or only in the evening
- Ask gestational age — swelling after 20 weeks with any other symptom requires immediate pre-eclampsia screening
- Confirm it is actual swelling (pitting oedema) and not just a woman feeling heavy or describing a rash

**Commonly confused with:**
- Normal dependent oedema of pregnancy — bilateral ankle swelling at the end of the day, resolves overnight, no other symptoms
- Weight gain described as swelling — ask specifically about visible puffiness of face and hands
- Skin rash or redness described as swelling — ask whether the skin is raised and puffy or just discoloured

**Critical immediate checks:**
- Gestational age: any swelling after 20 weeks must trigger pre-eclampsia screening
- Face and hand swelling: far more significant than ankle swelling — escalate if present
- Swelling with headache, visual changes, or epigastric pain: emergency — do not delay

---

## SECTION 2: TRIAGE — RED FLAGS

Escalate immediately if any of the following are present:

1. Swelling of face or hands after 20 weeks — especially if new or rapidly worsening
2. Swelling with severe headache — pre-eclampsia emergency
3. Swelling with visual disturbance (blurring, flashing lights, loss of vision)
4. Swelling with epigastric or right upper quadrant pain
5. Swelling with difficulty breathing or inability to lie flat
6. Whole-body swelling (anasarca) at any stage of pregnancy
7. Swelling of one leg only — especially with calf pain, warmth, or redness (deep vein thrombosis)
8. Swelling with fever
9. Swelling that appears suddenly or worsens rapidly over hours
10. Swelling with reduced or absent urine output
11. Swelling in a woman with known heart disease, kidney disease, or previous pre-eclampsia
12. Swelling with reduced fetal movement
13. Woman or family is very worried or reports swelling is different from normal pregnancy puffiness

---

## SECTION 3: CLINICAL REASONING GUIDE

---

### DISEASE 1: PRE-ECLAMPSIA
**Nigerian priority level: HIGHEST**

Pre-eclampsia is the most dangerous cause of swelling in pregnancy and must always be the first differential after 20 weeks. It is among the leading causes of maternal death in Nigeria. Swelling of the face and hands, as opposed to feet and ankles alone, is a key distinguishing sign.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Swelling appearing or worsening after 20 weeks
- Face and hand swelling — present in the morning, not just at end of day
- Accompanied by headache, visual changes, or epigastric pain
- Hypertension if blood pressure has been checked
- Proteinuria if urine tested
- Previous pre-eclampsia increases risk significantly
- Rapid onset or rapid worsening

**Differentiating questions:**
- How many weeks pregnant is she?
- Is her face or hands swollen, or just her feet and ankles?
- Is the swelling there in the morning when she wakes up, or only later in the day?
- Has her blood pressure been checked recently — was it high?
- Does she have a headache or visual changes alongside the swelling?
- Does she have pain under her ribs on the right side?
- Has she had pre-eclampsia before?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Face or hand swelling after 20 weeks
- Any associated headache, visual disturbance, or epigastric pain
- Known or suspected hypertension
- Rapid onset or worsening of swelling

**Uncertainty note:**
Pre-eclampsia cannot be excluded without blood pressure measurement. Any woman with swelling after 20 weeks who has not had a recent blood pressure check must be escalated for assessment. Never reassure based on symptom description alone.

---

### DISEASE 2: NORMAL PHYSIOLOGICAL OEDEMA OF PREGNANCY
**Nigerian priority level: MEDIUM**

Mild dependent oedema of the ankles and feet is common and normal in the second half of pregnancy. It results from increased blood volume, uterine pressure on pelvic veins, and fluid redistribution. It is a diagnosis of exclusion.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Bilateral ankle and foot swelling only — not face, not hands
- Worse at end of day, improves after overnight rest or elevating legs
- No headache, no visual changes, no epigastric pain
- Develops gradually over weeks, not rapidly over hours or days
- Blood pressure normal on recent check
- No other concerning symptoms

**Differentiating questions:**
- Is the swelling only in the ankles and feet, or also in the hands and face?
- Is it better in the morning after sleeping and worse by evening?
- Does it get better when she rests with her feet raised?
- Has she had her blood pressure checked recently — was it normal?
- Does she have any headache, visual problems, or pain under her ribs?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Bilateral ankle oedema only
- Resolves with rest and elevation
- No associated symptoms
- Normal blood pressure confirmed
- Gradual onset over weeks

**Uncertainty note:**
Normal physiological oedema is a diagnosis that requires confirmed normal blood pressure and confirmed absence of face and hand swelling. Do not apply this diagnosis remotely. If there is any doubt, escalate.

---

### DISEASE 3: DEEP VEIN THROMBOSIS (DVT)
**Nigerian priority level: HIGH**

Pregnancy increases the risk of blood clots in the legs (DVT) significantly. DVT can progress to pulmonary embolism, which is rapidly fatal. It presents with unilateral leg swelling and must be distinguished from bilateral oedema.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Swelling of one leg only — this is the key distinguishing feature
- Calf pain or tenderness, often described as pain on walking
- Warmth and redness of the affected leg
- Leg may feel hard or cord-like in the calf area
- May develop in any trimester but risk increases with gestation and immobility
- Risk factors: long travel, bed rest, obesity, prior DVT, caesarean section

**Differentiating questions:**
- Is only one leg swollen, or are both swollen equally?
- Does the swollen leg hurt, especially in the calf?
- Is the affected leg warm or red compared to the other leg?
- Has she been sitting or lying down for a long time recently — long travel, bed rest?
- Has she had a blood clot before?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Unilateral leg swelling
- Calf pain, warmth, or redness on the same side
- History of immobility or prior DVT
- Risk factors present

**Uncertainty note:**
Any unilateral leg swelling with calf pain must be escalated urgently. DVT can progress to pulmonary embolism within hours. Do not observe at home.

---

### DISEASE 4: CARDIAC DISEASE IN PREGNANCY
**Nigerian priority level: HIGH**

Rheumatic heart disease is prevalent in northern Nigeria and may be undiagnosed before pregnancy. Pregnancy dramatically increases cardiac workload. Heart failure in pregnancy presents with oedema, breathlessness, and inability to lie flat.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Leg swelling with difficulty breathing, especially when lying flat (orthopnoea)
- Breathlessness on minimal exertion
- History of rheumatic fever, heart murmur, or previous cardiac diagnosis
- Palpitations or feeling of rapid or irregular heartbeat
- Fatigue out of proportion to pregnancy
- Swelling tends to be whole-body or involves abdomen

**Differentiating questions:**
- Does she find it hard to breathe when she lies flat to sleep?
- How many pillows does she need to sleep comfortably?
- Does she get very breathless doing small tasks like walking across a room?
- Has she ever been told she has a heart problem or heart murmur?
- Did she have rheumatic fever as a child?
- Does she feel her heart beating fast or irregularly?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Swelling with orthopnoea (cannot lie flat)
- Breathlessness on minimal exertion
- Known or suspected heart disease
- Palpitations and fatigue alongside swelling

**Uncertainty note:**
Cardiac disease in pregnancy escalates rapidly. Any breathlessness with swelling requires emergency evaluation. Do not attribute breathlessness with swelling to normal pregnancy without cardiac assessment.

---

### DISEASE 5: RENAL DISEASE / NEPHROTIC SYNDROME
**Nigerian priority level: MEDIUM-HIGH**

Kidney disease including nephrotic syndrome causes significant oedema in pregnancy. It may be pre-existing or first detected in pregnancy. It can be difficult to distinguish from pre-eclampsia without laboratory testing.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Generalised oedema — often more severe than typical pregnancy oedema
- Frothy urine (proteinuria sign)
- History of kidney disease, hypertension, or diabetes before pregnancy
- Swelling present from early in pregnancy, not just after 20 weeks
- Normal blood pressure (distinguishes from pre-eclampsia, though both can coexist)

**Differentiating questions:**
- Has she noticed her urine looks foamy or bubbly?
- Did she have swelling or kidney problems before pregnancy?
- Does she have diabetes or high blood pressure that was diagnosed before pregnancy?
- How far along is the swelling — did it start early in pregnancy?
- Has she had a urine test done during this pregnancy?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Frothy urine alongside swelling
- Pre-existing kidney or diabetic history
- Swelling present since early pregnancy
- Generalised (not just ankle) oedema

**Uncertainty note:**
Renal disease and pre-eclampsia can coexist and are difficult to differentiate without blood pressure measurement and urine testing. Escalate for facility evaluation in all cases of generalised oedema.

---

### DISEASE 6: SEVERE ANAEMIA WITH CARDIAC FAILURE
**Nigerian priority level: MEDIUM-HIGH**

Severe anaemia (common in northern Nigeria from malaria, nutritional deficiency, or sickle cell disease) can lead to high-output cardiac failure in pregnancy, presenting with oedema and breathlessness.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Swelling with visible pallor — pale conjunctiva, lips, palms
- Extreme fatigue and weakness
- Breathlessness from low haemoglobin driving high-output failure
- History of sickle cell disease, repeated malaria, or poor nutrition
- Rapid heart rate

**Differentiating questions:**
- Does she look pale — in her eyes, lips, or palms?
- Is she extremely tired or weak beyond normal pregnancy tiredness?
- Has she been having repeated malaria episodes?
- Does she have sickle cell disease?
- Has anyone checked her blood level (haemoglobin) during this pregnancy?

**Confirm towards if:**
- Swelling with marked pallor
- Extreme fatigue and weakness
- History consistent with severe anaemia
- Breathlessness out of proportion to exertion level

**Uncertainty note:**
Severe anaemia causing cardiac decompensation is an emergency. Escalate any woman with swelling and pallor without delay.

---

### GENERAL UNCERTAINTY RULE — BODY SWELLING IN PREGNANCY

Any swelling in pregnancy after 20 weeks that involves the face or hands, that is accompanied by headache, visual changes, or epigastric pain, or that is worsening rapidly must be escalated immediately as a pre-eclampsia emergency. Any unilateral leg swelling must be escalated for DVT evaluation. Ankle oedema alone may be physiological, but this can only be accepted if blood pressure has been confirmed normal at a facility and all associated symptoms are absent. When in doubt, escalate.
