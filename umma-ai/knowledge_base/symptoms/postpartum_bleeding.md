# SYMPTOM FILE: Postpartum Bleeding

**File ID:** symptom_postpartum_bleeding
**Version:** 1.0

## SECTION 1: SYMPTOM VERIFICATION

### What counts as postpartum bleeding?
- Any vaginal bleeding after delivery of the baby
- **Primary PPH**: excessive bleeding within 24 hours of delivery — traditionally defined as >500ml after vaginal delivery or >1000ml after caesarean section, but in practice any bleeding that soaks more than one pad per hour, causes dizziness or weakness, or causes the woman to feel seriously unwell
- **Secondary PPH**: abnormal vaginal bleeding from 24 hours to 12 weeks after delivery
- Normal lochia must be distinguished from pathological bleeding (see below)

### Normal lochia — what to expect
- **Days 1–4 (lochia rubra)**: red, blood-like, moderate flow, may contain small clots; similar to a moderate period
- **Days 4–10 (lochia serosa)**: pink or brown, lighter flow
- **Days 10 onwards (lochia alba)**: yellowish-white, light flow, dries up over weeks
- **Clots**: small clots (smaller than a 50 kobo coin) may be normal in first days; large clots or increasing clots are abnormal
- **Smell**: lochia should not smell offensive; offensive smell suggests infection

### Commonly confused symptoms — verify carefully
- **Normal lochia vs PPH**: normal lochia is expected. The key questions are: Is the flow increasing rather than decreasing? Is she soaking pads excessively? Are there large clots? Does she feel dizzy, faint, or very weak? Is the smell offensive?
- **Haematuria vs vaginal bleeding**: confirm whether blood is from vagina or urine stream
- **Rectal bleeding vs vaginal bleeding**: haemorrhoids are common postpartum; confirm anatomical source
- **Offensive lochia without excessive bleeding**: may indicate endometritis even without heavy bleeding — both can co-exist

### Critical immediate checks
- **Time since delivery**: Primary PPH (within 24 hours) vs secondary PPH (after 24 hours) have different most-likely causes
- **Mode of delivery**: caesarean section, instrumental delivery, or complicated vaginal delivery significantly increase PPH risk
- **Signs of haemodynamic compromise**: dizziness, fainting, rapid heart rate, pale skin, cold sweat, confusion — these indicate significant blood loss and shock
- **Placenta**: was the placenta delivered completely? Any history of retained placenta at delivery?
- **Fever with bleeding**: combination suggests endometritis or infected retained products

## SECTION 2: TRIAGE — RED FLAGS

Listed from most to least dangerous:

1. **Collapse, loss of consciousness, or inability to respond** — haemorrhagic shock; life-threatening emergency
2. **Dizziness, fainting, or near-fainting with heavy bleeding** — haemodynamic compromise; immediate emergency
3. **Very rapid heart rate (heart racing, palpitations) with heavy bleeding** — shock compensation; immediate emergency
4. **Skin is pale, cold, clammy, or sweaty with bleeding** — signs of shock; immediate emergency
5. **Soaking more than one pad per hour for two consecutive hours or more** — major PPH
6. **Large clots — bigger than a 50 kobo coin — being passed** — significant haemorrhage
7. **Bleeding that is increasing rather than decreasing after delivery** — abnormal trend; escalate
8. **Heavy bleeding with fever and offensive-smelling lochia** — endometritis with haemorrhage
9. **Heavy bleeding after previous placenta problems (placenta praevia, accreta)** — very high risk
10. **Bleeding with a uterus that feels soft, large, or does not contract** — uterine atony
11. **Bleeding in a woman known to have a clotting disorder or on anticoagulants** — increased haemorrhage risk
12. **Secondary PPH with offensive discharge and fever** — infected retained products or endometritis
13. **Any return of heavy bleeding after it had settled (weeks postpartum)** — retained products or late PPH
14. **Woman or family feels she is losing too much blood or is deteriorating** — uncertainty always escalates

## SECTION 3: CLINICAL REASONING GUIDE

### 3.1 Uterine Atony
**Nigerian Priority Level: HIGHEST — most common cause of primary PPH**
Uterine atony — failure of the uterus to contract after delivery — accounts for approximately 80% of primary PPH globally and is the leading cause of postpartum maternal death in Nigeria. The uterus remains soft and does not clamp down, allowing continued bleeding from the placental bed.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Occurs within minutes to hours of delivery
- Uterus feels soft, large, or "boggy" when palpated — not firm and contracted
- Heavy, continuous bleeding — often bright red
- May occur with or without placenta delivery
- Risk factors: prolonged labour, grand multiparity (4+ previous births), large baby, twins, polyhydramnios, use of oxytocin in labour, general anaesthesia
- Grand multiparity is particularly common in northern Nigeria and is a major risk factor

**Differentiating questions:**
- How long after delivery did the bleeding start?
- Does her abdomen feel soft or hard when pressed above the pubic bone?
- Was the labour long or difficult?
- How many previous deliveries has she had?
- Was there more than one baby?
- Has she been given any injection after delivery to help the uterus contract?

**Confirm towards if:**
Heavy bleeding within hours of delivery + soft uterus on palpation + no evidence of retained products or lacerations = uterine atony

**Uncertainty note:**
Uterine atony is the default diagnosis in primary PPH unless a specific alternative cause is identified. If the uterus feels soft, treat as atony while simultaneously excluding other causes.

### 3.2 Retained Placenta or Retained Products of Conception (RPOC)
**Nigerian Priority Level: HIGHEST**
Retained placenta (whole or partial) and retained products of conception are major causes of both primary and secondary PPH in Nigeria. In settings where deliveries occur at home or with traditional birth attendants, incomplete placental delivery is frequently missed. Retained products become infected over time, leading to secondary PPH with endometritis.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Primary: placenta not fully delivered, or doubt about completeness at delivery
- Secondary: bleeding weeks after delivery, often with offensive smell or fever
- Uterus may remain large and tender
- History of home delivery or delivery without skilled attendant raises risk significantly
- Passage of tissue (grey or fleshy material) from vagina
- Bleeding may be intermittent or persistent

**Differentiating questions:**
- Was the placenta delivered in full, or was there any doubt?
- Where did she deliver — hospital, health centre, home, or with traditional birth attendant?
- Has she passed any tissue or clumps of tissue from the vagina?
- Is the bleeding constant or coming and going?
- Is there a bad smell?
- Does the lower abdomen remain tender or has the uterus not gone down as expected?

**Confirm towards if:**
Persistent or secondary PPH + history of incomplete placental delivery or home birth + offensive lochia OR tissue passage = retained products

**Uncertainty note:**
Secondary PPH must always raise suspicion of retained products, particularly after home delivery. Offensive discharge with secondary PPH is retained infected products until proven otherwise.

### 3.3 Genital Tract Lacerations / Trauma
**Nigerian Priority Level: HIGHEST — in primary PPH**
Lacerations of the cervix, vagina, or perineum during delivery can cause severe primary haemorrhage. Unrepaired or inadequately repaired lacerations are particularly dangerous. In northern Nigeria, high rates of obstructed labour and delivery with untrained attendants increase laceration risk.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Bleeding despite firm, well-contracted uterus
- Bright red, arterial-type bleeding
- Onset immediately or very shortly after delivery
- History of instrumental delivery, precipitate (very fast) labour, or large baby
- History of obstructed labour
- Perineal pain and swelling — may suggest haematoma rather than open laceration

**Differentiating questions:**
- Did she have a tear or stitches during or after delivery?
- Is the uterus firm or soft?
- Was the delivery complicated — very fast, with instruments (forceps/vacuum), or was labour obstructed?
- Is there swelling or a lump on the perineum?
- Is the blood bright red and coming in a stream?

**Confirm towards if:**
Immediate post-delivery bright-red bleeding + firm contracted uterus = laceration until inspected and excluded

**Uncertainty note:**
A firm uterus does not exclude significant PPH — lacerations must be actively excluded by examination. Perineal haematoma can expand rapidly and is dangerous even without visible external bleeding.

### 3.4 Endometritis / Infected Retained Products
**Nigerian Priority Level: HIGHEST**
Infection of the uterine lining or infected retained placental tissue is a major cause of secondary PPH in Nigeria. These conditions are frequently associated and can co-exist. They carry risk of sepsis and maternal death. High rates in settings with home delivery, prolonged PROM, and limited postpartum care.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Secondary PPH — bleeding after 24 hours, typically days 5–14 but can be up to 12 weeks
- Offensive or foul-smelling lochia
- Fever — low or high grade
- Lower abdominal pain and uterine tenderness
- Uterus may remain large or tender
- History of home delivery, prolonged labour, or manual placental removal

**Differentiating questions:**
- When did the heavy bleeding start — immediately after delivery or days later?
- Is there a bad smell from the vaginal discharge?
- Any fever or chills?
- Any lower tummy pain?
- How and where did she deliver?

**Confirm towards if:**
Secondary PPH + fever + offensive lochia + uterine tenderness = endometritis/infected RPOC

**Uncertainty note:**
Any secondary PPH with offensive smell must be treated as infected retained products or endometritis. Do not wait for fever to develop before escalating.

### 3.5 Coagulopathy (Clotting Failure)
**Nigerian Priority Level: HIGHEST — when suspected**
Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) and other coagulopathies complicate severe obstetric haemorrhage, pre-eclampsia/eclampsia, placental abruption, sepsis, and amniotic fluid embolism. In Nigeria, DIC often develops because PPH is not treated quickly enough due to delayed access to care.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Blood that does not clot — bleeding from IV sites, gums, nose
- Bleeding from multiple sites simultaneously
- Previous severe haemorrhage, abruption, or eclampsia in this delivery
- Oozing from wound or injection sites
- Bruising spreading across the body
- Dark, non-clotting blood

**Differentiating questions:**
- Is the blood clotting normally or does it seem thin and watery?
- Is there bleeding from anywhere else — gums, nose, injection sites, wound?
- Did she have major problems during delivery such as heavy bleeding, abruption, or seizures?
- Is there spreading bruising on the body?

**Confirm towards if:**
Ongoing heavy bleeding + non-clotting blood + bleeding from multiple sites + history of severe obstetric complication = DIC — EMERGENCY

**Uncertainty note:**
Any non-clotting or very thin watery blood from the vagina postpartum must be treated as possible coagulopathy. This is a systemic emergency requiring immediate hospital care.

### 3.6 Subinvolution of the Uterus
**Nigerian Priority Level: MEDIUM-HIGH**
Subinvolution is failure of the uterus to return to its normal size after delivery, leading to prolonged or returning vaginal bleeding weeks postpartum. Often caused by retained products or endometritis but can occur independently.

**Key differentiating features:**
- Prolonged lochia beyond 6 weeks, or return of bleeding after it had stopped
- Uterus remains large, soft, or tender when it should have reduced
- May be associated with mild fever or offensive smell
- Not dramatically heavy but persistent

**Differentiating questions:**
- How many weeks ago was the delivery?
- Has the bleeding ever fully stopped, or has it been continuous?
- Did the bleeding return after it had settled?
- Is there any smell or discomfort?
- Has the lower abdomen reduced back towards normal size?

**Confirm towards if:**
Prolonged or returning postpartum bleeding + large or poorly-involuted uterus = subinvolution, investigate for underlying cause

**Uncertainty note:**
Subinvolution is a clinical finding that requires a cause to be identified — retained products and endometritis must be excluded.

---

### GENERAL UNCERTAINTY RULE
Postpartum bleeding is one of the leading causes of maternal death in Nigeria. Any bleeding that is heavier than expected, returning after it had settled, associated with dizziness or weakness, offensive-smelling, or associated with fever must be escalated without delay. When uncertain about whether the amount of bleeding is normal or abnormal — escalate. The threshold for escalation in postpartum bleeding must be low. Signs of shock require immediate emergency referral.
