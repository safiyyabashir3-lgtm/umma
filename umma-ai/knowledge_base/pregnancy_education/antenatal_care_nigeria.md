# Antenatal Care in Nigeria

**Topic:** Antenatal Care (ANC) — Clinic Visits During Pregnancy
**Guideline Source:** Nigerian National Guidelines for Antenatal Care; WHO ANC Model 2016
**Context:** Kano State, Northern Nigeria

---

## OVERVIEW

Antenatal care means visiting a health clinic while you are pregnant — before your baby is born. These visits help keep you and your baby safe and healthy. At each visit, a nurse or midwife will check your health, give you medicines to protect you and your baby, and watch for any problems early — when they are easier to treat.

In Nigeria, the government recommends at least **8 antenatal visits** during pregnancy. Missing these visits can put you and your baby at risk. UMMA wants to help you understand when to go, what will happen, and how to overcome challenges that make it hard to attend.

---

## KEY INFORMATION

**When to start antenatal care:**
- Go to the clinic **as soon as you know you are pregnant** — ideally before 12 weeks (3 months).
- Starting early gives you and your baby the best protection.

**How many times to go:**
Nigeria follows the WHO 2016 model of **8 visits minimum:**

| Visit | When to Go |
|-------|------------|
| 1st visit | As early as possible (before 12 weeks) |
| 2nd visit | 20 weeks |
| 3rd visit | 26 weeks |
| 4th visit | 30 weeks |
| 5th visit | 34 weeks |
| 6th visit | 36 weeks |
| 7th visit | 38 weeks |
| 8th visit | 40 weeks |

**What happens at antenatal visits:**
- Your blood pressure is checked
- Your weight is checked
- Your baby's position and heartbeat are checked
- Blood and urine tests are done
- You receive **iron and folic acid tablets** — take them every day as instructed
- You receive **malaria prevention medicine** (IPTp) — malaria is very dangerous in pregnancy
- You receive **tetanus vaccine** — to protect your baby from tetanus after birth
- You are taught about danger signs, nutrition, and safe delivery

**Remember:** Write down or remember your next appointment date each time you visit.

## NIGERIAN CONTEXT

**Where to go for antenatal care in Kano:**
- **Primary Health Care (PHC) centres** — in most wards and communities. These are free or very low cost.
- **General hospitals** — in LGAs across Kano State
- **Specialist Hospital Kano (Murtala Muhammad Specialist Hospital)** — for high-risk pregnancies
- **Private clinics and mission hospitals** — available in many areas but may charge fees

**Is antenatal care free in Kano?**
- At government PHC centres, antenatal care is largely free or subsidised under the **Basic Health Care Provision Fund (BHCPF)**.
- Some states offer **free antenatal care cards** — ask at your nearest PHC centre.
- Bring your **National Health Insurance card** if you have one.

**If you live far from a clinic:**
- Many LGAs in Kano have mobile outreach teams that visit communities on scheduled days. Ask your village head or community health worker (CHEW) about outreach days near you.
- Some women travel together to share transport costs — talk to your neighbours.

**Common barriers and how to address them:**
- *Husband's permission:* Many women in northern Nigeria need their husband's support. Share this with him: "The Prophet (SAW) said there is no harm in seeking treatment." Antenatal care protects the mother Allah has entrusted to his care.
- *Transport cost:* PHC centres are designed to be close to communities. Kano State also has community health workers who can guide you to the nearest one.
- *Long waiting times:* Go early in the morning. Bring water and something to do while you wait.
- *Female provider preference:* Many PHC centres have female nurses and midwives. You can request a female provider — this is your right.

## COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

**"If I feel healthy, I don't need to go to the clinic."**
This is not correct. Many problems in pregnancy — like high blood pressure, anaemia, and infections — have no symptoms at first. They can only be found by a health worker during a check. By the time you feel unwell, the problem may already be serious.

**"Antenatal care is only for women having their first baby."**
This is not correct. Every pregnancy is different. Problems can happen in any pregnancy — even the 5th or 6th. Experienced mothers still need all their antenatal visits.

**"Going to the clinic early will bring bad luck or cause miscarriage."**
This is not true. Visiting the clinic early is safe and helps protect the pregnancy. Delaying visits means missing important vaccines, medicines, and early detection of problems.

**"Traditional birth attendants (TBAs) can do everything a clinic can."**
TBAs can offer support and comfort, but they cannot check blood pressure, give vaccines, do blood tests, or manage emergencies. Antenatal care at a clinic and support from a trusted TBA or family member can work together — but the clinic visits are essential.

**"I don't need iron tablets — I eat well."**
Even women who eat well often don't get enough iron during pregnancy. Your baby takes iron from your body. Iron tablets help prevent anaemia, which causes tiredness, weakness, and can be dangerous during labour. Take your iron tablets every day as instructed.

## PRACTICAL ADVICE

1. **Book your first visit as soon as your period is late** — do not wait until you are showing.

2. **Keep your ANC card safe** — this card records your health and your baby's health. Bring it to every visit. Keep it in a dry, safe place at home.

3. **Take your medicines every day:**
   - Iron and folic acid tablets — take with food to reduce nausea
   - Malaria prevention medicine — take as directed, on a full stomach

4. **Involve your husband or a trusted family member** — a support person can help you attend appointments, especially later in pregnancy.

5. **Ask questions at the clinic** — you have the right to understand what is happening to your body. If you do not understand something, ask again.

6. **Plan your birth early** — during your visits, discuss where you will deliver. Having a plan means you are ready if labour starts suddenly.

7. **Know your next appointment** — before you leave the clinic, confirm your next visit date. Write it on your phone, your wall, or your ANC card.

## WHEN TO SEEK CARE

Antenatal care is for regular check-ups. But some situations need **immediate** clinic or hospital attention — do not wait for your next scheduled visit.

Go to a clinic or hospital **right away** if you experience:
- Heavy bleeding from your vagina at any time in pregnancy
- Severe headache that does not go away
- Blurry vision or seeing spots
- Swelling of your face, hands, or feet (especially sudden swelling)
- Severe pain in your belly
- Your baby stops moving or moves much less than usual (after 28 weeks)
- Fever with chills or shaking
- Leaking water from your vagina before labour begins
- Fits or convulsions (sudden body shaking you cannot control)

**These are danger signs. They need urgent care. Do not wait.**

If you are unsure whether something is serious, it is always better to go and be checked. A health worker will not judge you for coming. Your life and your baby's life matter.
