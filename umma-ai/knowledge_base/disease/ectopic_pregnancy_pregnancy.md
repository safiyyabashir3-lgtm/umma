# ECTOPIC PREGNANCY
*UMMA Disease File | Pregnancy Category*

---

## OVERVIEW

Ectopic pregnancy occurs when a fertilised egg implants outside the uterine cavity — most commonly in the fallopian tube (>95%), but also cervix, ovary, or abdominal cavity. Tubal ectopic pregnancy can lead to tubal rupture, massive intraperitoneal haemorrhage, and death within hours if not recognised and treated surgically. It is one of the leading causes of maternal death in the first trimester in Nigeria.

**Epidemiology (Kano/Northwest Nigeria):**
- Rates of ectopic pregnancy are higher in Nigeria than high-income countries due to higher background prevalence of pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), sexually transmitted infections (particularly chlamydia and gonorrhoea), and unsafe uterine instrumentation
- Previous PID with tubal scarring is the most common predisposing factor in this population
- Previous unsafe abortion with secondary PID is a significant risk factor — relevant given high rates of unsafe abortion in Nigeria
- Prevalence of diagnosed ectopic pregnancy at major Nigerian hospitals: approximately 1 in 50–80 deliveries (compared to 1 in 100 in high-income settings)
- Contributes disproportionately to first-trimester maternal mortality in Nigeria

**Why it matters for UMMA:**
- Ectopic pregnancy is a time-critical emergency — hours matter once rupture occurs
- Women in Kano often do not know they are pregnant (irregular cycles, poor ANC early uptake) making early recognition harder
- Symptoms are frequently dismissed as UTI, appendicitis, or normal early pregnancy symptoms by both patients and PMVs
- UMMA must flag any combination of missed period + pain + bleeding as potentially ectopic until proven otherwise
- Even haemodynamically stable presentations can rupture suddenly

**Common delays and barriers:**
- Late presentation to healthcare — women and families often unaware of early pregnancy
- Misdiagnosis as gastroenteritis, UTI, appendicitis, or ovarian cyst pain
- PMVs treating abdominal pain and vaginal bleeding with medications (misoprostol, antibiotics) without diagnosis — can mask symptoms or delay referral
- Financial barriers to emergency surgery and blood transfusion
- Transport barriers — journey to surgical facility may take hours
- Absence of ultrasound at primary care level to confirm intrauterine pregnancy
- Stigma around discussing pregnancy and sexual health delays disclosure

## HOW IT PRESENTS IN NIGERIAN PATIENTS

### Mild Presentation (Unruptured or early ectopic — haemodynamically stable)
- Amenorrhoea (missed period) — may be 4–8 weeks; woman may not have confirmed pregnancy
- Lower abdominal pain: typically unilateral (one side), may be intermittent, dull or crampy — not yet severe
- Light vaginal bleeding or spotting — often described as "unusual period" or "late period"
- Urine pregnancy test positive (or unknown — woman may not have tested)
- Minimal or no adnexal tenderness on examination
- Cervical motion tenderness may be present
- No peritoneal signs
- Vital signs normal
- No shoulder tip pain

*This stage is the ideal point for diagnosis — but is frequently missed because symptoms are vague and patient may not report missed period.*

### Moderate Presentation (Threatened/leaking ectopic — haemodynamically stable but worsening)
- More persistent unilateral lower abdominal pain, becoming constant
- Vaginal bleeding (may be heavy or scanty — does not correlate with internal bleeding severity)
- Shoulder tip pain (referred pain from diaphragmatic irritation by blood in peritoneum) — an important diagnostic clue, especially right-sided
- Nausea and vomiting
- Feeling of pelvic pressure or urge to defecate
- On examination: unilateral adnexal tenderness, cervical motion tenderness (cervical excitation)
- Possible adnexal mass palpable
- Vital signs still in normal range but patient in pain
- Mild tachycardia may be present

*This stage requires immediate hospital referral — patient can deteriorate very rapidly.*

### Severe Presentation (Ruptured ectopic — haemodynamic compromise)
- Sudden severe abdominal pain — may have had preceding pain or onset can seem sudden
- Generalised abdominal pain and distension — peritoneal irritation from haemoperitoneum
- Shoulder tip pain — often bilateral now
- Syncope or near-syncope (fainting or feeling about to faint)
- Signs of haemorrhagic shock: pallor (check conjunctivae and palms), cold clammy skin, rapid weak pulse, low blood pressure, rapid breathing
- Altered consciousness in severe cases
- Abdominal rigidity and guarding
- Vaginal bleeding may be minimal despite massive internal haemorrhage — do not be reassured by lack of heavy vaginal bleeding
- Patient may describe sudden deterioration after initial improvement (following tubal abortion — temporary decompression before re-bleeding)

*THIS IS A SURGICAL EMERGENCY — death can occur within 1–2 hours of rupture if not surgically managed.*

## DIFFERENTIATING FEATURES

**Ectopic vs threatened miscarriage (intrauterine pregnancy):**
- Both: positive pregnancy test, pain, bleeding, early pregnancy
- Ectopic: pain typically unilateral and colicky, cervical motion tenderness, shoulder tip pain, adnexal mass/tenderness; miscarriage has midline cramping, bleeding often heavier, central uterine tenderness
- Definitive distinction requires ultrasound — UMMA cannot differentiate clinically; both require hospital assessment
- **Key rule: no visualised intrauterine pregnancy on ultrasound in a pregnant woman = ectopic until proven otherwise**

**Ectopic vs appendicitis:**
- Appendicitis: periumbilical pain migrating to right iliac fossa, fever, nausea, no pregnancy history (or unknown), Rovsing's sign positive
- Ectopic: positive pregnancy test is the key differentiator — always check pregnancy test in women of reproductive age with lower abdominal pain
- Ectopic can mimic appendicitis exactly — pregnancy test is mandatory

**Ectopic vs PID:**
- PID: bilateral pain, fever, vaginal discharge, no (or negative) pregnancy test, cervical motion tenderness
- Ectopic: positive pregnancy test; PID + positive pregnancy test = ectopic until proven otherwise
- PID and ectopic share risk factors; PID increases ectopic risk

**Ectopic vs ovarian cyst torsion:**
- Cyst torsion: sudden severe unilateral pain, nausea, adnexal mass, but pregnancy test usually negative
- Both require emergency hospital assessment — differentiation by ultrasound

**Ectopic vs urinary tract infection:**
- UTI: dysuria, frequency, suprapubic pain, no missed period, urine dipstick abnormal, no adnexal tenderness
- Ectopic does not cause urinary symptoms; check pregnancy test before attributing lower abdominal pain to UTI in women of reproductive age

**Ectopic vs corpus luteum cyst:**
- Corpus luteum cyst can mimic ectopic very closely including positive pregnancy test and unilateral pain
- Cannot be differentiated clinically — ultrasound required

## SEVERITY ASSESSMENT

### Mild (Stable, unruptured — high suspicion)
- Positive pregnancy test + unilateral pain ± minimal vaginal bleeding
- Haemodynamically stable (normal pulse, normal BP)
- No signs of peritoneal irritation
- No shoulder tip pain
- Walking, talking, alert

### Moderate (Leaking/threatened rupture — urgent)
- All of above PLUS any of:
  - Shoulder tip pain
  - Worsening unilateral pain becoming constant
  - Cervical motion tenderness
  - Adnexal mass
  - Mild tachycardia (pulse 90–110)
  - Patient unable to walk comfortably due to pain

### Severe — any ONE = surgical emergency:
- Signs of haemodynamic compromise: tachycardia > 110, low BP, pallor, cold clammy extremities
- Syncope or loss of consciousness
- Generalised abdominal rigidity, guarding, or rebound tenderness
- Shoulder tip pain + haemodynamic compromise
- Sudden worsening of pain after initial partial improvement
- Patient too unwell to walk

## MANAGEMENT BY SEVERITY

### Mild (Suspected ectopic — stable)

**Clinical actions:**
- Same-day referral to hospital with ultrasound and gynaecological assessment capacity — do not wait for symptoms to worsen
- Do not give misoprostol, uterotonic medications, or herbal abortifacients
- Advise no food or water (nil by mouth) in case emergency surgery required
- Explain to woman that she must not be left alone during transport
- Ensure accompanying person understands signs of deterioration
- Carry out urine pregnancy test if not already done — if positive + unilateral pain = refer regardless of bleeding status

**Plain language for UMMA:**
"I am worried that your pain and missed period together could be a sign of a pregnancy in the wrong place — this is called ectopic pregnancy and it is an emergency if not treated quickly. You need to go to the hospital today for a scan to check where the pregnancy is. Please do not eat or drink anything until you have been seen, in case you need surgery. Do not take any medicines, herbs, or tablets for this pain. Go with someone who can stay with you. If your pain suddenly gets much worse, you feel like you are going to faint, or you feel very cold and sweaty, call for emergency help immediately — do not wait."

---

### Moderate (Probable ectopic — urgent)

**Clinical actions:**
- Immediate hospital referral — no delay
- Nil by mouth
- If IV access available: IV line and fluids en route if possible (community health setting)
- Transport with support person
- Call ahead to hospital if possible
- Do not administer any medications including pain relief that might mask deterioration

**Plain language for UMMA:**
"This looks like it could be a pregnancy in the wrong place and it is becoming urgent. You need to go to the hospital right now — not tomorrow, not later today. Take someone with you. Do not eat or drink. If you feel faint or the pain gets suddenly much worse on the way, stop and get emergency help at the nearest clinic. At the hospital, tell them you think you might have an ectopic pregnancy."

---

### Severe (Ruptured ectopic — emergency)

**Clinical actions:**
- This is a life-threatening emergency — immediate action
- Lie patient flat, elevate legs if possible (shock position)
- Do not give food, water, or any medications
- Call emergency services or community health worker immediately
- Transport to nearest surgical facility with blood bank capacity — Teaching Hospital or Federal Medical Centre in Kano
- If patient loses consciousness during transport: recovery position, monitor airway
- Inform receiving hospital en route that patient has suspected ruptured ectopic with haemorrhagic shock — they need theatre and blood ready
- Stay with patient — do not leave alone

**Plain language for UMMA:**
"This is a medical emergency. The pregnancy in the wrong place has almost certainly burst and she is bleeding inside. She needs emergency surgery right now to save her life. Call for help immediately. Lay her flat on her back with her legs raised. Do not give her anything to eat or drink. Get her to [Murtala Muhammad Specialist Hospital / AKTH] as fast as possible — every minute matters. Tell someone at the hospital she is bleeding inside from a ruptured ectopic pregnancy. Do not leave her alone."

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC WARNINGS

**Dangerous practices:**
- PMVs dispensing misoprostol for "irregular bleeding" or "missed period" without confirming intrauterine pregnancy — in ectopic pregnancy, misoprostol delays surgical diagnosis and provides false reassurance; no evidence it effectively treats ectopic pregnancy
- Herbal abortifacients used for unwanted early pregnancies — can mask symptoms, delay diagnosis, and cause additional harm
- "Managing at home" — women and families frequently persuaded by relatives to wait and see; ectopic pregnancy is fatal if rupture is not surgically treated
- Fluid given by mouth during resuscitation attempts before surgery — aspiration risk in surgical emergency

**Infrastructure limitations:**
- Ultrasound is not available at primary care level — all suspected ectopics must be referred to a facility with ultrasound and gynaecological surgery
- Blood bank capacity is limited even at secondary level — direct referral to tertiary centre (AKTH, MMSH Kano) is recommended for suspected rupture
- Anaesthesia availability can be limited — early referral gives the best chance of timely surgery
- Night-time transport in Kano can be extremely difficult — emphasise that this cannot wait until morning

**Cultural factors:**
- Women may delay disclosing pregnancy due to shame around non-marital pregnancy, early pregnancy before formal announcement, or previous pregnancy loss
- Husbands/family may need to be involved in decision to travel — UMMA should encourage disclosure of the emergency to husband or trusted family member to facilitate transport
- Fear of surgery — belief that surgery will affect future fertility; reassure that untreated ruptured ectopic destroys the tube regardless, and surgery gives the best chance of future fertility preservation

**High-risk subgroups:**
- Women with previous PID or STI history
- Women with previous ectopic pregnancy (recurrence risk approximately 10–15%)
- Women with previous uterine surgery, including caesarean section (caesarean scar ectopic — rare but described)
- Women with history of unsafe induced abortion
- Women with known or suspected tubal ligation (failure rate exists and ectopic risk increased)
- Women using intrauterine contraceptive device (IUCD): lower absolute risk of pregnancy, but if pregnancy occurs, higher proportion are ectopic
- Women who have never confirmed an intrauterine pregnancy on ultrasound in this pregnancy
