# DISEASE FILE: URINARY TRACT INFECTION AND KIDNEY INFECTION IN PREGNANCY
## Nigerian Context (Kano / Northwest Priority) — Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## OVERVIEW
Urinary tract infection (UTI) in pregnancy ranges from asymptomatic bacteriuria
to cystitis (bladder infection) to pyelonephritis (kidney infection). All forms
carry risk in pregnancy and require treatment. Asymptomatic bacteriuria — bacteria
in the urine with no symptoms — affects 2 to 10% of pregnant women and if
untreated progresses to pyelonephritis in up to 30% of cases. Pyelonephritis
in pregnancy is associated with preterm labour, low birth weight, maternal
sepsis, and rarely maternal death. In Kano and northern Nigeria, limited access
to antenatal urine testing means many UTIs are diagnosed late or missed entirely.
The most common causative organisms in Nigerian pregnant women are Escherichia
coli, Klebsiella, and Staphylococcus saprophyticus. Resistance to commonly
available antibiotics — ampicillin, cotrimoxazole — is high in Nigerian isolates.
UTI in pregnancy must never be dismissed as minor.

---

## HOW IT PRESENTS IN NIGERIAN PREGNANT WOMEN

### Asymptomatic Bacteriuria
- No symptoms at all
- Diagnosed only on urine culture at antenatal clinic
- Still requires treatment in pregnancy — to prevent progression
- Woman feels completely well

### Cystitis — Lower UTI
- Burning or pain when passing urine
- Frequent urination — needing to go more often than usual
- Urgency — strong sudden urge to urinate
- Urine may appear cloudy or smell different
- Mild lower abdominal discomfort — suprapubic
- No fever or low-grade fever only
- No loin or back pain
- Woman otherwise well

### Pyelonephritis — Upper UTI, Kidney Infection
- Fever — often high, with chills and rigors
- Loin pain — pain in the back, below the ribs, on one or both sides
- Costovertebral angle tenderness — pain when the area below the ribs
  at the back is pressed or tapped
- Vomiting
- General malaise — feeling very unwell
- Urinary symptoms may or may not be prominent
- In pregnancy — associated with uterine contractions and risk of
  preterm labour
- Can progress rapidly to sepsis

### Sepsis from UTI — Emergency
- High fever with rigors
- Confusion or altered consciousness
- Very fast breathing
- Signs of shock — fast weak pulse, cold extremities
- Reduced or no urine output
- Extreme weakness — unable to stand

---

## DIFFERENTIATING FEATURES
- Burning urination + frequency + no fever = likely lower UTI (cystitis)
- Loin pain + fever + vomiting = pyelonephritis until proven otherwise
- Fever with loin pain in pregnancy — do not assume malaria alone;
  pyelonephritis must be actively considered
- UTI can present without urinary symptoms in pregnancy — silent UTI
  causing fever must always be on the differential list
- Preterm contractions in a febrile pregnant woman — consider
  pyelonephritis as a treatable trigger
- Cloudy or foul-smelling urine — supports UTI diagnosis but absence
  does not exclude it

## SEVERITY ASSESSMENT

### Mild — Cystitis
- Burning urination and frequency only
- No fever
- No loin pain
- Baby moving normally
- No vomiting

### Moderate — Pyelonephritis without systemic features
- Fever present
- Loin pain
- Vomiting
- But: alert, able to drink, no shock, baby moving

### Severe — Pyelonephritis with systemic features or sepsis
- High fever with rigors
- Unable to keep fluids down
- Signs of sepsis — confusion, fast breathing, shock
- Reduced fetal movement alongside
- Unable to stand or walk

---

## MANAGEMENT BY SEVERITY

### Mild — Cystitis
- Urgent clinic visit — same day
- Urine test essential — dipstick and ideally culture
- Oral antibiotics — nitrofurantoin or cefalexin preferred in pregnancy
  (avoid nitrofurantoin at term); avoid cotrimoxazole in first trimester
  and at term
- Increase fluid intake — clean water
- Complete full antibiotic course
- Follow-up urine test after treatment to confirm clearance
- Return immediately if fever develops or symptoms worsen

Plain language for caregiver:
"The burning when you urinate and needing to go often may be an infection
in your bladder. In pregnancy this needs antibiotic treatment even if you
feel mostly well, because it can spread to the kidneys and cause serious
problems. Please go to the clinic today for a urine test and medicine."

### Moderate — Pyelonephritis
- Hospital today — same day
- IV or IM antibiotics likely needed — vomiting prevents oral treatment
- IV fluids for hydration
- Fetal monitoring — contractions may be triggered
- Admission likely needed

Plain language for caregiver:
"This infection has spread to your kidneys and is more serious. The fever
and back pain mean you need hospital treatment today — the infection needs
medicine given through a drip because vomiting makes tablets not work.
Your baby also needs to be checked. Please go now."

### Severe — Sepsis from UTI
- Emergency — hospital immediately
- IV antibiotics, IV fluids, close monitoring
- Fetal monitoring — preterm labour risk
- ICU-level care may be needed

Plain language for caregiver:
"This kidney infection has become very serious and is affecting your whole
body. This is an emergency. Please go to hospital immediately — do not wait."

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC WARNINGS
- Urine culture facilities are limited at primary and most secondary
  health centres in Kano — empirical antibiotic treatment is often
  necessary based on clinical diagnosis
- Antibiotic resistance is high to ampicillin and cotrimoxazole in
  Nigerian UTI isolates — these should not be first-line choices
- Many pregnant women in Kano dismiss urinary symptoms as normal in
  pregnancy — culturally, frequent urination is expected and burning
  may be tolerated; active questioning is needed
- Preterm labour triggered by pyelonephritis — must always assess for
  contractions in a febrile pregnant woman with loin pain
- Dehydration worsens rapidly in pyelonephritis in hot northern
  Nigerian climate — oral fluid advice is important alongside antibiotics
- Female hygiene practices — wiping technique and water hygiene after
  toileting — should be addressed sensitively as prevention
- Schistosomiasis — endemic in some areas around Kano — can cause
  blood in urine that may be confused with UTI; ask about blood in urine
  and geographic history
- Repeat UTI in pregnancy is common — each episode needs full treatment
  and follow-up urine test to confirm clearance
