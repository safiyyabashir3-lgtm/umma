# DISEASE FILE: MALARIA IN PREGNANCY
## Nigerian Context — Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## OVERVIEW
Malaria is the most common cause of fever in pregnant Nigerian women. Pregnancy increases susceptibility to malaria and severity of disease. Malaria in pregnancy causes maternal anaemia, low birth weight, preterm birth, and maternal death. It is frequently underdiagnosed. Always treat as high priority.

---

## HOW IT PRESENTS IN NIGERIAN PREGNANT WOMEN

### Early/Mild Presentation
- Episodic fever with chills and sweating
- Headache
- Mild nausea or vomiting
- Fatigue and weakness
- Mild abdominal discomfort
- Woman still able to walk and talk normally
- Baby still moving normally

### Moderate Presentation
- High fever with pronounced chills and sweating episodes
- Significant vomiting
- Marked weakness and fatigue
- Pallor of eyelids or palms suggesting anaemia
- Reduced appetite
- Headache worsening
- Woman unwell but conscious and oriented

### Severe Presentation
- Very high fever unresponsive to simple measures
- Altered consciousness or confusion
- Inability to stand or walk
- Severe vomiting unable to keep anything down
- No fetal movement
- Severe anaemia features — extreme pallor, breathlessness at rest
- Jaundice
- Seizure or convulsion
- Dark brown or black urine (blackwater fever)

---

## DIFFERENTIATING FEATURES
- Episodic pattern — cold stage, hot stage, sweating stage — distinguishes from typhoid
- Chills and rigors more prominent than in UTI or typhoid
- Headache almost always present
- Vomiting common
- Can present without classic chills in pregnancy — do not rule out based on absence of chills alone
- Malaria and anaemia very commonly coexist in Nigerian pregnant women

---

## SEVERITY ASSESSMENT

### Mild malaria criteria
- Fever with chills and headache
- Able to drink and keep fluids down
- Conscious and oriented
- Baby moving normally
- No anaemia features

### Moderate malaria criteria
- High fever with significant vomiting
- Marked weakness
- Pallor suggesting anaemia
- Headache severe
- Still conscious and oriented

### Severe malaria criteria (any one = severe)
- Confusion or altered consciousness
- Seizure
- Unable to stand
- No fetal movement
- Severe anaemia with breathlessness at rest
- Dark urine
- Jaundice
- Unable to keep any fluid down

---

## MANAGEMENT BY SEVERITY

### Mild
- Urgent clinic visit same day for blood film confirmation and treatment
- Do not start treatment at home without confirmation
- Keep drinking fluids
- Rest
- Do not take aspirin or ibuprofen in pregnancy

### Moderate
- Urgent clinic visit immediately
- Do not delay for any reason
- If clinic far — go to nearest health facility first

### Severe
- Emergency — go to hospital immediately
- Do not wait
- This is life threatening for mother and baby

---

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC WARNINGS
- Artemisinin-based combination therapy is standard treatment in Nigeria — clinic will prescribe
- Quinine is used in first trimester severe malaria — clinic decision only
- Do not take chloroquine — resistance is widespread in Nigeria
- Do not take herbal remedies for malaria in pregnancy
- Mosquito net use every night is essential throughout pregnancy
- Malaria can worsen very rapidly in pregnancy — do not wait to see if it improves
