# DISEASE FILE: PRE-ECLAMPSIA AND ECLAMPSIA
## Nigerian Context — Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## OVERVIEW
Pre-eclampsia is a major cause of maternal death in northern Nigeria. It occurs after 20 weeks of pregnancy. It is characterised by high blood pressure and organ damage. If untreated it progresses to eclampsia which causes seizures and can kill mother and baby. Early recognition is critical. Many Nigerian women present late due to delayed care-seeking.

---

## HOW IT PRESENTS IN NIGERIAN PREGNANT WOMEN

### Early/Mild Presentation
- Headache that does not go away
- Mild swelling of feet and ankles — may seem normal to patient
- Slight blurring of vision
- Gestational age over 20 weeks
- Woman may feel generally unwell but not dramatically so
- Blood pressure elevated — woman may not know this without measurement

### Moderate Presentation
- Persistent headache worsening over hours
- Swelling spreading to face and hands
- Noticeable visual disturbance — blurry vision, seeing spots or flashing lights
- Upper abdominal pain especially right side under ribs
- Reduced urine output
- Significant ankle and leg swelling
- Woman feels unwell and anxious

### Severe Presentation
- Severe headache described as worst ever
- Significant visual disturbance or temporary blindness
- Severe upper abdominal pain
- Marked swelling of face hands and feet
- Very reduced or no urine output
- Nausea and vomiting
- Confusion or agitation
- Seizure — this is eclampsia — immediately life threatening

---

## DIFFERENTIATING FEATURES
- Always after 20 weeks of pregnancy — before 20 weeks consider other causes
- Combination of headache plus swelling plus visual symptoms is highly suspicious
- Swelling of face and hands is more significant than feet swelling alone
- Upper right abdominal pain suggests liver involvement — severe feature
- Reduced urine output is serious sign
- Can occur without headache — swelling and visual symptoms alone warrant urgent assessment

---

## SEVERITY ASSESSMENT

### Mild criteria
- Headache present but tolerable
- Feet and ankle swelling only
- No visual symptoms
- Baby moving normally
- Over 20 weeks gestation

### Moderate criteria
- Persistent worsening headache
- Swelling reaching hands or face
- Mild visual symptoms
- Upper abdominal discomfort
- Reduced urine output

### Severe criteria (any one = severe)
- Worst ever headache
- Significant visual disturbance or blindness
- Seizure — eclampsia
- Severe upper abdominal pain
- Confusion
- No urine output
- No fetal movement

---

## MANAGEMENT BY SEVERITY

### Mild
- Urgent clinic visit today — do not wait until next scheduled appointment
- Blood pressure measurement essential
- Urine test essential
- Do not take any medicine for headache without medical advice

### Moderate
- Go to hospital today — not just a clinic
- Needs proper blood pressure monitoring and urine testing
- May need admission

### Severe
- Emergency — go to hospital immediately
- Do not wait for any reason
- Seizure is immediately life threatening — call for help and go
- This is one of the leading killers of pregnant women in northern Nigeria
- Do not drive herself — get help immediately

---

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC WARNINGS
- Many Nigerian women do not attend enough antenatal visits — blood pressure may have been rising undetected
- Cultural tendency to manage headache at home with analgesics — dangerous in this context
- Pre-eclampsia can worsen very rapidly — hours matter
- Magnesium sulphate is the treatment for eclampsia — only available at hospital
- After delivery pre-eclampsia can still occur — postpartum headache and swelling must be taken seriously
- Women with no formal education and no antenatal care are at highest risk in Nigeria
