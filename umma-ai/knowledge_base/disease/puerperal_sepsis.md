# DISEASE FILE: PUERPERAL SEPSIS — POSTPARTUM INFECTION
## Nigerian Context (Kano / Northwest Priority) — Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## OVERVIEW
Puerperal sepsis — serious infection occurring after childbirth or miscarriage —
is one of the five leading direct causes of maternal death in Nigeria and a
major contributor to maternal mortality in northern Nigeria specifically.
It is defined as infection of the genital tract occurring from delivery up
to 42 days postpartum. The most common sources are the uterus (endometritis),
wound infections (perineal tear or caesarean wound), urinary tract, and breast.
In Kano and northern Nigeria, risk is increased by home deliveries with
unhygienic practices, retained products of conception after delivery or
miscarriage, prolonged labour, multiple vaginal examinations during labour,
and unsafe abortion. Causative organisms include E. coli, group A and B
Streptococcus, Staphylococcus aureus, and anaerobes. Many women in Kano
present late because postpartum fever is normalised culturally or attributed
to other causes. Puerperal sepsis can progress to septic shock and death
within hours — it demands urgent hospital treatment at every severity level.

---

## HOW IT PRESENTS

### Mild — Early Endometritis
- Fever — usually from day 1 to 10 after delivery
- Lower abdominal pain or uterine tenderness — uterus tender when pressed
- Foul-smelling vaginal discharge — lochia that smells offensive
- Lochia heavier than expected or not reducing as expected
- General malaise and fatigue
- Woman feels unwell but still mobile and alert

### Moderate — Spreading Infection
- Higher fever — often with chills and rigors
- Significant uterine or abdominal tenderness
- Offensive heavy vaginal discharge
- Vomiting
- Reduced urine output
- Wound infection if caesarean — redness, swelling, discharge from wound
- Woman looks unwell, weak

### Severe — Sepsis
- High fever or paradoxically hypothermia — very low temperature
  in severe sepsis
- Confusion or altered consciousness
- Very fast breathing
- Fast weak pulse — signs of shock
- Cold clammy extremities
- No urine output
- Severe lower abdominal pain and rigidity
- Woman unable to stand or care for baby

### Septic Shock — Immediate Emergency
- Collapse
- Very low blood pressure
- Loss of consciousness
- Unresponsive

---

## DIFFERENTIATING FEATURES
- Postpartum fever from breast engorgement — day 2 to 3, breasts hard
  and full, no uterine tenderness, no offensive discharge, fever settles
  with feeding; not sepsis
- Urinary tract infection postpartum — burning urination, frequency,
  loin pain; may coexist with endometritis
- Mastitis — breast red, hot, tender, fever; may progress to abscess;
  separate from uterine infection but still needs treatment
- Wound infection — localised to caesarean or perineal repair site;
  redness, swelling, discharge, gaping
- Retained products of conception — incomplete expulsion of placenta
  or membranes; persistent heavy bleeding with fever; requires surgical
  evacuation
- Unsafe abortion complications — history of instrumentation or
  substances used to terminate pregnancy; approach non-judgementally;
  same management principles apply

---

## SEVERITY ASSESSMENT

### Mild
- Low grade fever, mild uterine tenderness, offensive lochia,
  woman alert and mobile, able to eat and drink

### Moderate
- Higher fever with chills, significant tenderness, offensive discharge,
  vomiting, wound infection, woman looks unwell

### Severe — Sepsis
- Any of: confusion, fast weak pulse, very fast breathing, cold extremities,
  no urine output, high or very low temperature, unable to stand

### Septic Shock — Immediate Emergency
- Collapse, loss of consciousness, unresponsive

---

## MANAGEMENT BY SEVERITY

### Mild
- Hospital today — same day — not just a clinic visit
- IV or oral antibiotics — combined broad-spectrum regimen covering
  aerobic and anaerobic organisms; metronidazole plus amoxicillin-
  clavulanate or ampicillin-gentamicin-metronidazole per local protocol
- Uterine evacuation if retained products suspected — hospital only
- Continue breastfeeding — safe with most antibiotic regimens used
- Do not manage at home with herbal medicines or analgesics alone

Plain language for caregiver:
"The fever and pain after delivery may mean an infection inside the
womb. Even if you feel mostly well now, this type of infection can
become very serious very quickly. You need to go to hospital today
for antibiotic medicine and a proper check. Please go now."

### Moderate
- Hospital today — urgent
- IV antibiotics — triple regimen ampicillin, gentamicin, metronidazole
  is standard in Nigerian hospitals
- IV fluids
- Assess for retained products — may need surgical evacuation
- Wound care if caesarean wound infected
- Close monitoring

Plain language for caregiver:
"This infection is becoming more serious. You need hospital treatment
today with medicine through a drip. Please go now — do not wait."

### Severe — Sepsis
- Emergency — hospital immediately
- IV antibiotics immediately upon arrival
- IV fluid resuscitation
- Surgical evacuation if retained products
- ICU-level monitoring if available
- This can be fatal within hours without treatment

Plain language for caregiver:
"This is a very serious infection that is spreading through your body.
This is an emergency. Please go to hospital immediately — right now.
Do not stop anywhere."

### Septic Shock
- Immediate life-threatening emergency
- Fastest possible transport to hospital with ICU capacity
- AKTH Kano — tertiary level care

Plain language for caregiver:
"This woman is in a life-threatening emergency. Go to the largest
hospital immediately. Every minute matters."

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC WARNINGS
- Home deliveries in Kano — conducted by traditional birth attendants
  or family members — carry high infection risk from non-sterile
  equipment and practices; always ask about delivery circumstances
- Retained placenta — common after home delivery without skilled
  attendant — always ask if placenta was fully delivered and checked
- Unsafe abortion — a significant cause of sepsis in northern Nigeria
  due to restrictive abortion laws; approach non-judgementally;
  management is the same regardless of cause; legal obligation to
  treat without discrimination
- Cultural normalisation of postpartum fever in some Kano communities —
  fever after delivery is sometimes expected and attributed to the
  delivery process itself, causing dangerous delays in care-seeking
- Postpartum women in purdah — some women in northern Nigeria observe
  purdah (staying indoors after delivery) — may make clinic attendance
  difficult; male partner or female family member involvement in
  decision-making is important
- Breastfeeding should continue during treatment — most antibiotics
  used for puerperal sepsis are compatible with breastfeeding
- Group A Streptococcus sepsis — rare but rapidly fatal form of
  puerperal sepsis; can kill within 12 to 24 hours; any woman who
  deteriorates extremely rapidly postpartum needs immediate escalation
- Postpartum period up to 42 days — puerperal sepsis can occur up to
  6 weeks after delivery; women and families must know that fever
  in the weeks after delivery still needs urgent assessment
