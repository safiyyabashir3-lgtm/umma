# DISEASE FILE: ANAEMIA IN PREGNANCY
## Nigerian Context — Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## OVERVIEW
Anaemia is extremely common in Nigerian pregnant women. Studies show over 60% of pregnant Nigerian women are anaemic. It is caused by iron deficiency, folate deficiency, malaria, and sickle cell disease. It increases risk of maternal death, preterm birth, low birth weight, and postpartum haemorrhage. It is frequently underdiagnosed and undertreated.

---

## HOW IT PRESENTS IN NIGERIAN PREGNANT WOMEN

### Early/Mild Presentation
- Mild fatigue and tiredness
- Slight pallor of inner eyelids
- Mild dizziness on standing quickly
- Mild shortness of breath on significant exertion
- Headache
- Woman still functional and able to do daily activities

### Moderate Presentation
- Significant fatigue — difficulty with normal daily activities
- Noticeable pallor of inner eyelids and palms
- Dizziness frequent
- Shortness of breath on mild activity such as walking short distances
- Palpitations — feeling heart beating fast
- Headache frequent
- Poor concentration

### Severe Presentation
- Extreme pallor — eyelids, palms, lips very pale
- Breathlessness at rest
- Inability to perform any activity without breathlessness
- Rapid heart rate felt by patient
- Chest pain
- Fainting or near fainting
- Swelling of feet — due to cardiac strain
- Very weak — unable to stand for long
- Baby may have reduced movement due to placental insufficiency

---

## DIFFERENTIATING FEATURES
- Pallor of inner eyelids is key sign — distinguishes from simple fatigue
- No fever unless infection coexists
- Gradual onset over weeks — unlike malaria which is more acute
- Dietary history often reveals poor iron and folate intake
- Malaria history common — malaria destroys red blood cells causing anaemia
- Sickle cell disease — may have history of previous crises or known diagnosis

---

## SEVERITY ASSESSMENT

### Mild anaemia criteria
- Mild fatigue and pallor
- Able to do daily activities
- No breathlessness at rest
- No chest pain or fainting

### Moderate anaemia criteria
- Significant fatigue limiting daily activities
- Noticeable pallor
- Breathlessness on mild activity
- Palpitations
- Frequent dizziness

### Severe anaemia criteria (any one = severe)
- Breathlessness at rest
- Chest pain
- Fainting
- Extreme pallor of lips and eyelids
- Unable to walk without breathlessness
- Reduced fetal movement

---

## MANAGEMENT BY SEVERITY

### Mild
- Urgent clinic visit for blood test to confirm and determine cause
- Dietary advice — increase iron rich foods — beans, dark green vegetables, meat, fish
- Avoid tea and coffee with meals as they reduce iron absorption
- Iron and folate supplements — prescribed at clinic
- Treat underlying malaria if present

### Moderate
- Urgent clinic visit same day for blood test and treatment
- Iron supplementation prescription needed
- Malaria treatment if coexisting
- Follow up closely

### Severe
- Emergency — go to hospital immediately
- May need blood transfusion
- Breathing difficulty or chest pain with anaemia is life threatening
- Do not wait

---

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC WARNINGS
- Malaria is the most common cause of severe anaemia in Nigerian pregnant women — always treat malaria
- Dietary anaemia very common — many Nigerian women do not eat enough iron-rich foods during pregnancy
- Sickle cell disease — common in Nigeria — worsens dramatically in pregnancy
- Iron supplements commonly cause constipation — reassure patient this is normal and manageable
- Many women stop iron tablets due to side effects — counsel to continue
- Postpartum haemorrhage risk is very high in anaemic women — delivery must be in a facility
