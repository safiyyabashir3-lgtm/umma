# DISEASE FILE: ANAEMIA IN A CHILD UNDER 5
## Nigerian Context (Kano / Northwest Priority) — Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## OVERVIEW
Anaemia — low haemoglobin — is extremely common in Nigerian children under 5.
In Kano and northwest Nigeria, anaemia prevalence in under-5 children exceeds
70% in some communities. The most common causes are malaria, malnutrition
(iron deficiency), and sickle cell disease. These causes frequently coexist.
Severe anaemia carries high mortality in children and frequently requires
blood transfusion, which is only available at hospital level. Many children
in Kano present with severe anaemia late, after prolonged pallor has been
normalised by caregivers or treated at home with tonics and herbal medicines.
Recognising pallor — particularly of the inner lower eyelid — is the most
accessible clinical sign for caregivers.

---

## HOW IT PRESENTS IN NIGERIAN CHILDREN UNDER 5

### Mild Anaemia
- Pallor of inner lower eyelids — lighter pink than normal
- Mild fatigue — child tires more quickly than usual
- Slightly reduced appetite
- Child otherwise active and feeding
- No fast breathing at rest
- No severe weakness

### Moderate Anaemia
- Pallor of inner eyelids clearly visible — pale or whitish
- Pallor of palms and lips
- Fatigue — child less active, tiring easily
- Reduced appetite
- Fast breathing on activity — not at rest
- Child looks pale and unwell
- Reduced playfulness

### Severe Anaemia — Emergency
- Inner eyelids very pale or white
- Palms, lips, and tongue pale or white
- Fast breathing at rest — even without chest infection
- Child extremely weak — cannot stand or walk, lies still
- Prostration — cannot sit up unsupported
- Pounding or racing heartbeat — child may report or caregiver may feel
- Altered consciousness — confused or difficult to rouse
- Cardiac failure signs — fast breathing, swollen face or legs, not
  from malnutrition alone
- Child deteriorating rapidly

## DIFFERENTIATING FEATURES BY CAUSE

### Malaria-related anaemia — most common in Kano
- Fever present or history of recent fever
- Episodic illness pattern
- Chills and sweating with fever episodes
- Acute onset or worsening after fever
- Rapid and severe anaemia possible with acute haemolysis
- Any age — most severe under 2 years

### Iron deficiency anaemia — nutritional
- Gradual onset over weeks to months
- No fever
- Poor diet history — predominantly cereal-based, little meat or legumes
- Early weaning from breast milk
- Child aged 6 months to 2 years most affected
- May coexist with malaria

### Sickle cell disease
- Known family history of sickle cell disease or sickle cell trait
- Recurrent episodes of pallor
- Episodes of bone pain — child crying and holding limb
- Swelling of hands and feet in infants — dactylitis
- Jaundice during crisis episodes
- Enlarged spleen in some cases
- Previous blood transfusions
- Worsened dramatically by malaria or infection

### Hookworm and intestinal parasites
- Older children — especially over 2 years
- History of walking barefoot on soil
- Pallor gradual onset
- May have abdominal pain or visible worms in stool
- Poor nutrition alongside

## SEVERITY ASSESSMENT

### Mild
- Pale inner eyelids, otherwise well, no fast breathing, active

### Moderate
- Clear pallor of eyelids and palms, some fatigue, fast breathing on
  exertion only, child still ambulatory

### Severe — urgent to emergency
- Pale to white inner eyelids AND any of:
  - Fast breathing at rest
  - Prostration
  - Altered consciousness
  - Cardiac failure signs
  - Haemoglobin below 5g/dL if measurable

### Hyperacute — immediate emergency
- Acute severe anaemia from sickle cell crisis or massive malaria haemolysis
- Rapid deterioration with extreme pallor and collapse

---

## MANAGEMENT BY SEVERITY

### Mild
- Urgent clinic visit — same day
- Blood test to confirm anaemia and identify cause
- Iron supplementation if nutritional cause
- Malaria treatment if malaria confirmed
- Dietary advice — iron-rich foods, continued breastfeeding

Plain language for caregiver:
"Your child looks pale which may mean low blood. Your child needs to be
seen at the clinic today for a blood check and the right treatment.
Keep giving nutritious food and breastfeeding."

### Moderate
- Urgent clinic or hospital today
- Blood test essential — haemoglobin measurement
- Treat underlying cause — malaria, nutritional, parasites
- Close monitoring — may deteriorate

Plain language for caregiver:
"The paleness in your child's eyes and hands suggests the blood level
may be quite low. Your child needs to go to hospital today to check the
blood level and get the right treatment."

### Severe
- Emergency — hospital immediately
- Blood transfusion may be needed — only available at hospital
- Treat underlying cause — malaria, sickle cell crisis
- Do not delay for any reason

Plain language for caregiver:
"Your child's blood level may be dangerously low — this is a medical
emergency. Your child needs to go to hospital immediately. A blood
transfusion may be needed. Please go now."

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC WARNINGS
- Blood transfusion is only reliably available at secondary and tertiary
  hospitals in Kano — primary health centres cannot manage severe anaemia
- Blood screening for HIV and hepatitis is done at Kano hospitals —
  transfusion safety is maintained at AKTH and MMSH
- Tonics and herbal iron preparations widely used in Kano — they do not
  adequately treat anaemia and delay proper care
- Sickle cell disease prevalence is high in Nigeria — always ask about
  family history of sickle cell when child has recurrent anaemia episodes
- Malaria prevention — consistent mosquito net use — is the single most
  important intervention to prevent anaemia in Kano children
- Iron-rich foods — meat, fish, beans, green leafy vegetables — are often
  under-represented in the diet of poor households in Kano
- Routine deworming every 6 months for children over 1 year is
  recommended and reduces anaemia burden — many families in Kano
  are unaware of this programme
- G6PD deficiency causes acute haemolytic anaemia — sudden collapse
  after exposure to camphor or certain medicines — always ask
