# URINARY TRACT INFECTIONS IN PREGNANCY
*UMMA Disease File | Pregnancy Category*

---

## OVERVIEW

Urinary tract infections (UTIs) in pregnancy encompass a spectrum from asymptomatic bacteriuria (ABU) through uncomplicated lower UTI (cystitis) to upper UTI (pyelonephritis). In pregnancy, all forms carry greater risk than outside pregnancy due to physiological changes — ureteral dilatation (hydroureter), vesicoureteral reflux, urinary stasis, and immune modulation — which allow bacteria to ascend more easily from bladder to kidneys and increase risk of preterm labour.

**Critically important in pregnancy context:**
- Asymptomatic bacteriuria in pregnancy, if untreated, progresses to pyelonephritis in 20–40% of cases and is associated with preterm birth and low birthweight
- Pyelonephritis in pregnancy can trigger preterm labour, sepsis, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), and maternal death
- UTI is the most common bacterial infection requiring antibiotic treatment in pregnancy

**Epidemiology (Kano/Northwest Nigeria):**
- Prevalence of asymptomatic bacteriuria in pregnant Nigerian women: 8–14% (higher than in high-income settings) — driven by limited access to clean water, inadequate sanitation, high rates of anaemia reducing immune defences, and late ANC booking
- *E. coli* remains the most common organism but *Klebsiella spp.*, *Staphylococcus saprophyticus*, and *Proteus mirabilis* are more prevalent in Nigerian studies than Western data — with different resistance patterns
- Antibiotic resistance is a significant clinical issue in Kano — fluoroquinolone and trimethoprim resistance is high; culture and sensitivity is important for recurrent or treatment-refractory cases
- Routine urine culture is not performed at most ANC clinics in Kano — asymptomatic bacteriuria is largely undetected
- Female genital schistosomiasis (common in some parts of northwest Nigeria) can affect bladder and lower urinary tract, mimicking or co-occurring with UTI
- Sickle cell disease (common in Nigerian population) independently increases UTI risk and severity in pregnancy

**Why it matters for UMMA:**
- UTI is a very common complaint UMMA will receive — high volume condition
- The key clinical task is distinguishing lower UTI (manageable with appropriate antibiotics) from upper UTI/pyelonephritis (requires urgent hospital assessment and possible admission)
- Untreated UTI in pregnancy = risk of preterm labour — UMMA must always advise clinic attendance, never home management alone
- PMV antibiotic dispensing without diagnosis is extremely common and leads to inadequate treatment

**Common delays and barriers:**
- Symptoms normalised by women — "burning in pregnancy is normal"
- PMVs dispensing 3-day courses of inappropriate or subtherapeutic antibiotics without urine culture
- Costs of clinic attendance and urine tests
- Stigma around discussing urinary and genital symptoms
- Non-attendance at ANC (late booking, single ANC visit common in rural Kano) means screen for ABU missed

## HOW IT PRESENTS IN NIGERIAN PATIENTS

### Mild Presentation (Asymptomatic bacteriuria / early cystitis)
- **Asymptomatic bacteriuria:** No symptoms — only detected on urine culture; significant growth of bacteria on culture without symptoms; clinically important in pregnancy even without symptoms
- **Early cystitis:** Mild dysuria (burning/stinging when passing urine); frequency (needing to pass urine more than usual); urgency (sudden need to rush to toilet)
- Urine may appear cloudy or have offensive smell
- No fever
- No back pain or loin pain
- No nausea or vomiting beyond normal pregnancy nausea
- No systemic upset — eating, sleeping, daily activities normal
- Suprapubic discomfort (low central abdominal ache) may be present

### Moderate Presentation (Cystitis — established, or early upper tract involvement)
- More pronounced dysuria, frequency, urgency
- Suprapubic pain — constant or worsening
- Haematuria — visible blood in urine (pink, red, or brown urine) — alarming to patient; in pregnancy, always requires assessment
- Low-grade fever (37.5–38°C) — suggests early upper tract involvement or systemic response
- Mild loin discomfort — flank tenderness on the affected side beginning to develop
- Nausea may be worsening
- Urine strongly offensive, dark, or cloudy
- Symptoms not improving after 24–48 hours of antibiotic treatment started at PMV

### Severe Presentation (Pyelonephritis — upper UTI)
- High fever (≥38°C, often 39–40°C) with rigors (shaking chills)
- Loin pain — unilateral or bilateral flank pain, often severe; costovertebral angle (CVA) tenderness on percussion (pain when fist-percussed over the kidney area at the back)
- Nausea and vomiting — may prevent oral fluid and medication intake
- Systemically unwell: fatigue, malaise, headache
- Tachycardia (rapid pulse)
- Lower urinary symptoms may or may not be prominent — upper UTI can occur without marked dysuria/frequency
- In severe pyelonephritis: signs of sepsis — very high fever or hypothermia, fast breathing, confusion, hypotension, reduced urine output
- Increased uterine activity or contractions — preterm labour may be triggered
- Reduced foetal movements in severe cases — foetal distress from maternal sepsis

*In pregnancy, any fever ≥38°C must be taken seriously — fever in pregnancy can cause tachycardia in the foetus and trigger preterm labour.*

## DIFFERENTIATING FEATURES

**UTI vs normal pregnancy urinary frequency:**
- Normal pregnancy: frequency without dysuria, burning, or pain; urine is clear and normal smelling; no fever; no loin pain
- UTI: dysuria is the key differentiator; offensive or cloudy urine; systemic features in upper UTI

**UTI vs pre-eclampsia/HELLP:**
- Pre-eclampsia: proteinuria, hypertension, oedema, headache, visual disturbance, epigastric/RUQ pain — epigastric pain can mimic UTI if not examined carefully
- Pyelonephritis: loin/flank pain (posterolateral), fever, rigors, dysuria — not hypertension or visual symptoms
- Both can coexist — do not let UTI diagnosis delay assessment for hypertension in pregnancy

**UTI vs appendicitis in pregnancy:**
- Appendicitis: appendix is displaced upward in pregnancy; pain may be in right flank or right upper quadrant (not classic RIF); fever; no dysuria typically; nausea/vomiting
- UTI: dysuria, frequency, loin tenderness — if right-sided pyelonephritis, can closely mimic appendicitis; urine dipstick/culture helps differentiate
- Both require hospital assessment if fever is present

**UTI vs preterm labour:**
- Preterm labour triggered by UTI: uterine contractions + UTI symptoms coexisting; important to recognise both are occurring simultaneously
- Contractions or pelvic pressure in a woman with UTI symptoms = high urgency referral; UTI may be triggering preterm labour

**UTI vs vaginal infection (bacterial vaginosis, trichomoniasis):**
- Vaginal infection: vaginal discharge (colour, odour), vulval itching/soreness, no dysuria on initiation of urine stream (dysuria with vaginal infections is typically vulval burning as urine passes over inflamed tissue — "external dysuria" — rather than internal burning on bladder filling)
- UTI: internal dysuria (burning inside pelvis/urethra during voiding); frequency and urgency; no vaginal discharge necessarily
- Both can coexist; mid-stream urine sample technique important to avoid vaginal contamination of sample

**UTI vs schistosomiasis (urogenital):**
- Urogenital schistosomiasis: haematuria (especially terminal haematuria — blood at end of urinary stream), chronic pelvic pain, dyspareunia; in Kano context, consider in women from rural areas with freshwater exposure history
- Can mimic UTI and co-occur with UTI; persistent haematuria not responding to antibiotics should prompt consideration

## SEVERITY ASSESSMENT

### Mild (Asymptomatic bacteriuria / uncomplicated cystitis):
- No fever or temperature < 37.5°C
- Symptoms limited to dysuria, frequency, urgency, suprapubic discomfort
- No loin pain or back tenderness
- No nausea/vomiting
- Systemically well

### Moderate (Cystitis with systemic features / early upper tract):
- Low-grade fever 37.5–38°C
- Loin discomfort developing
- Visible haematuria
- Nausea and vomiting
- Not improving on antibiotic treatment already started
- Any symptoms in a woman with sickle cell disease — automatic escalation

### Severe (Pyelonephritis / sepsis) — any ONE = urgent/emergency:
- Fever ≥ 38°C with loin pain and systemic upset
- Rigors (shaking chills)
- Severe costovertebral angle tenderness
- Vomiting preventing oral medication
- Signs of sepsis: tachycardia, hypotension, confusion, fast breathing
- Uterine contractions or signs of preterm labour coexisting
- Reduced foetal movements
- Any UTI symptoms + fever in third trimester = urgent referral regardless of clinical certainty

## MANAGEMENT BY SEVERITY

### Mild

**Clinical actions:**
- All pregnant women with UTI symptoms require clinic attendance and urine sample (dipstick + culture if available)
- Increase fluid intake — 8–10 glasses of clean water daily
- Appropriate antibiotic: nitrofurantoin (avoid after 36 weeks — neonatal haemolytic anaemia risk), amoxicillin, or cefalexin — choice guided by local resistance patterns and trimester; 7-day course minimum in pregnancy (not 3 days)
- Advise on hygiene: wipe front to back, void after intercourse, avoid bladder irritants
- Retest urine after completing antibiotic course — test of cure is essential in pregnancy
- Any PMV-initiated antibiotic treatment requires clinic review — may not be appropriate agent or duration

**Plain language for UMMA:**
"The burning you feel when passing urine is a sign of a urine infection. In pregnancy, urine infections need to be treated properly because they can cause early labour if left. Please go to the clinic or antenatal clinic to give a urine sample and get the right antibiotic. Drink plenty of clean water — 8–10 glasses a day. Do not take antibiotics from the chemist without a proper check, and if you have already started them, still go to the clinic to make sure they are the right ones. After finishing your medicine, go back to the clinic to have your urine checked again."

---

### Moderate

**Clinical actions:**
- Same-day clinic or ANC attendance
- Urine dipstick and culture — guide treatment
- Oral antibiotics appropriate to trimester (avoid quinolones in pregnancy, avoid nitrofurantoin after 36 weeks, avoid trimethoprim in first trimester — clinic decision)
- Antiemetics if nausea limiting fluid/medication intake
- If not tolerating oral fluids: hospital assessment for IV hydration
- Monitor foetal movement; if decreased — escalate
- Review in 24–48 hours; if not improving, escalate

**Plain language for UMMA:**
"Your urine infection is getting more serious and needs clinic attention today. There is a risk it could spread to your kidneys and trigger early labour if not treated quickly. Please go to the clinic or hospital today. Make sure to tell them you are pregnant — the antibiotic they give you must be safe for pregnancy. Keep drinking plenty of water. If you start to feel very hot with shaking, your back hurts a lot, you start vomiting and cannot keep fluids down, or you feel your baby is not moving as much — go directly to the hospital, not the clinic."

---

### Severe (Pyelonephritis / sepsis)

**Clinical actions:**
- Immediate hospital admission — oral antibiotics are insufficient for pyelonephritis in pregnancy
- IV antibiotics (ceftriaxone is appropriate first-line in Nigeria, pending culture)
- IV hydration — patients with pyelonephritis in pregnancy can become severely dehydrated
- Foetal monitoring — CTG if available; assess for preterm labour
- Antipyretics (paracetamol) to reduce fever-induced foetal tachycardia
- Serial assessment of renal function
- If preterm labour triggered: manage jointly with obstetric team — balance tocolysis against infection
- Blood cultures if signs of sepsis
- Repeat urine culture after treatment — test of cure mandatory

**Plain language for UMMA:**
"This is a serious kidney infection that can be very dangerous in pregnancy — it can cause early labour and can affect your own health severely. You need to go to the hospital right now to receive medicines through a drip, not just tablets. Please go directly to [ANC/maternity unit at referral hospital] — do not wait. If you are having pains in your belly or the baby seems to have stopped moving, tell the hospital immediately when you arrive. Make sure someone goes with you and stays."

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC WARNINGS

**Dangerous practices:**
- PMVs dispensing 3-day courses of cotrimoxazole (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole) — inadequate duration; cotrimoxazole is also inappropriate in first trimester (folate antagonism) and near term (neonatal hyperbilirubinaemia); very high trimethoprim resistance in Nigerian uropathogens
- Fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin) widely dispensed at PMVs for UTI — contraindicated in pregnancy due to cartilage effects; high resistance rates also limit efficacy
- Tetracyclines: absolutely contraindicated in pregnancy; sometimes dispensed at PMVs
- Self-treatment with leftover antibiotics from previous illness — wrong agent, incomplete course
- Drinking very limited fluids due to excessive urinary frequency — makes UTI worse; must counsel to increase not decrease fluid intake
- Herbal teas and "kidney flushes" — not evidence-based; some traditional herbal preparations contain nephrotoxic agents

**Infrastructure limitations:**
- Urine culture is not routinely available at PHC level in Kano — many women receive empiric treatment without culture; UMMA should advise clinic attendance but acknowledge that empiric treatment with an appropriate agent is necessary when culture unavailable
- Test of cure urine culture is rarely performed even where initially available — UMMA should specifically counsel on follow-up
- IV antibiotic administration for pyelonephritis requires hospital admission — PHCs cannot manage this; referral pathway to ANC clinic or maternity hospital is required
- Nitrofurantoin is in the essential medicines list but stock-outs occur — cefalexin is a reliable alternative

**Cultural factors:**
- Urinary and gynaecological symptoms are private topics — women may be reluctant to discuss with male health workers; UMMA's female conversational framing may reduce this barrier
- Pain and discomfort in pregnancy widely normalised — "it is part of being pregnant" — UMMA must explicitly communicate that burning urine and fever in pregnancy are NOT normal and must be treated
- Drinking large amounts of water may be difficult where water access is limited or water must be purchased — acknowledge this practically and help problem-solve

**High-risk subgroups:**
- Women with sickle cell disease (SS or SC): dramatically increased risk of severe UTI and pyelonephritis; should receive antibiotic prophylaxis throughout pregnancy (clinic decision); any UTI symptoms require immediate escalation
- HIV-positive pregnant women: immunocompromised; higher risk of ascending infection
- Women with diabetes (including gestational diabetes): glucose in urine (glycosuria) promotes bacterial growth; higher UTI risk
- Grand multiparas: bladder prolapse and incomplete voiding increase stasis and risk
- Women with previous renal disease or recurrent UTI: require closer monitoring and possibly prophylaxis
- Adolescent mothers: less likely to attend ANC consistently; higher prevalence of untreated ABU
