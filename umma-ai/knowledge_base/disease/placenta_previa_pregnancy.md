# UMMA Disease File — Placenta Praevia & Antepartum Haemorrhage
# Category: Pregnancy illness | Subcategory: Obstetric emergency
# Last reviewed: 2025

---

## OVERVIEW

This file covers antepartum haemorrhage (APH) — vaginal bleeding after 24 weeks gestation — with a primary focus on placenta praevia and placental abruption, the two most dangerous and common causes. These are obstetric emergencies that cause significant maternal and perinatal mortality in Kano and northwest Nigeria.

**Placenta Praevia:** The placenta is implanted partially or completely over the internal cervical os. As the lower uterine segment stretches in late pregnancy and labour, the placenta shears and bleeds. Painless, bright red, recurrent vaginal bleeding in the second or third trimester is the hallmark. Grand multiparity — extremely common in northern Nigeria — is a major risk factor. Without access to planned caesarean section, placenta praevia is potentially fatal.

**Placental Abruption:** Premature separation of a normally sited placenta from the uterine wall before delivery. Causes sudden, painful bleeding — which may be concealed (blood trapped behind placenta) or revealed (external). Associated with hypertension, pre-eclampsia, trauma, smoking (rare), and cocaine use (rare in this context). Fetal mortality is high in major abruption.

**Other APH causes** (less common but important):
- Vasa praevia — fetal vessels cross the membranes over the cervical os; rupture causes rapid fetal exsanguination; rare but catastrophic
- Cervical ectropion or cervical cancer — small amount of contact bleeding; lower risk but cervical cancer is not uncommon in Nigerian women
- Show — blood-stained mucus at onset of labour — small amount, not heavy

**Why it matters for UMMA:** Any vaginal bleeding in pregnancy after 24 weeks is an emergency until proven otherwise. Women in Kano commonly attribute bleeding to exertion, intercourse, or "the baby shifting position" and wait to see if it stops. Even if bleeding resolves, placenta praevia will bleed again — often more severely. UMMA must generate an immediate, non-negotiable emergency referral for any antepartum haemorrhage.

**Common delays and barriers:**
- Bleeding attributed to non-dangerous causes — waiting for it to stop
- Night-time bleeding with no transport available
- Financial barriers to hospital admission
- Fear of caesarean section preventing attendance
- Previous episode resolved — false reassurance that it is not serious
- Women managed at home by TBAs who do not recognise the danger
- Retained placenta praevia on ultrasound — some women told at private scan but not counselled on danger of bleeding

## HOW IT PRESENTS IN NIGERIAN PATIENTS

### Mild Presentation — minor APH, woman haemodynamically stable
**Placenta praevia — minor bleed:**
- Painless bright red vaginal bleeding — characteristically painless; this is the key distinguishing feature
- Bleeding may be small — spotting to moderate flow; soaking less than one pad in an hour
- No abdominal pain or uterine contractions
- Uterus soft and non-tender on palpation
- Fetal movements present and normal
- Maternal pulse and BP stable
- This is still an emergency — she needs hospital assessment same day; another bleed will come and will be larger

**Placental abruption — minor:**
- Abdominal pain — constant, not intermittent; uterus may feel tense
- Vaginal bleeding — may be small or absent (concealed abruption); do not use absence of bleeding to reassure
- Uterus tender on palpation
- Fetal movements may be reduced

### Moderate Presentation
**Placenta praevia — significant bleed:**
- Heavier vaginal bleeding — soaking pads; blood on clothing or bedding
- Painless (usually)
- Tachycardia beginning to develop
- Mild dizziness or lightheadedness
- Fetal movements may be reduced as maternal blood pressure falls
- No pain

**Placental abruption — moderate:**
- Severe constant abdominal pain
- Uterus board-hard — rigid on palpation (a cardinal sign)
- Bleeding may be heavy, dark, or partially concealed
- Tachycardia
- Fetal movements significantly reduced
- Signs of shock developing

### Severe Presentation — major haemorrhage, maternal/fetal compromise
**Either cause:**
- Heavy continuous bleeding — soaking multiple pads rapidly or blood pooling
- Maternal shock: rapid pulse >120/minute; systolic BP <90 mmHg; cold clammy skin; altered consciousness; collapse
- Severe dizziness, near-syncope or syncope
- Fetal movements absent — fetal compromise or death
- Woman cannot stand

**Abruption specifically:**
- Board-hard, extremely tender uterus
- Fetal heart absent — fetal death from acute abruption
- Maternal DIC: bruising, bleeding from gums or IV sites, uncontrolled haemorrhage

**Vasa praevia — catastrophic fetal haemorrhage:**
- Rupture of membranes followed immediately by bright red bleeding and sudden fetal bradycardia or absent heart rate
- Fetus exsanguinates within minutes — this is the most acute fetal emergency in obstetrics
- Cannot be distinguished from other APH causes at home — all require the same immediate emergency response

## DIFFERENTIATING FEATURES

**Placenta praevia vs Placental abruption — key distinctions:**

| Feature | Placenta Praevia | Placental Abruption |
|---|---|---|
| Pain | Painless | Painful — constant |
| Bleeding | Bright red, external | Dark, may be concealed |
| Uterus | Soft, non-tender | Hard, board-like, tender |
| Fetal compromise | Later — from maternal blood loss | Earlier — from placental separation |
| Risk factors | Grand multiparity, previous CS | Hypertension, trauma, pre-eclampsia |

**UMMA note: this distinction matters for anticipating severity but NOT for management — both require immediate emergency hospital referral. Do not delay referral by attempting to distinguish them.**

**APH vs Bloody show of labour:**
- Show: small amount of blood-stained mucus; woman may have contractions starting; amount minimal — not soaking a pad
- APH: frank blood; may be painless or painful; heavier than show; no or irregular contractions

**APH vs Post-coital bleeding:**
- Post-coital: small amount after intercourse; cervical ectropion common in pregnancy; painless; not increasing
- APH: heavier; may occur without intercourse; recurrent; any amount in third trimester requires assessment

**APH vs UTI causing haematuria:**
- UTI: blood in urine, not from vagina; dysuria; frequency; offensive urine
- APH: blood clearly vaginal in origin

**Cervical cancer — important in Nigerian context:**
- Nigeria has high cervical cancer prevalence; it can cause vaginal bleeding in pregnancy
- Cervical cancer bleeding: contact or post-coital; may be offensive; cervix friable on speculum (assessed at facility)
- Any recurrent small-volume vaginal bleeding in pregnancy requires speculum examination at facility to exclude cervical pathology

## SEVERITY ASSESSMENT

### Mild criteria — STILL AN EMERGENCY; same-day hospital attendance mandatory
- Any amount of painless vaginal bleeding after 24 weeks — suspected praevia
- Any amount of painful vaginal bleeding with uterine tenderness — suspected abruption
- Maternal haemodynamically stable
- Fetal movements present

**UMMA must communicate: even if bleeding stops, she must still go to hospital today.**

### Moderate criteria — urgent emergency, go now
- Continued or recurrent bleeding soaking pads
- Tachycardia, dizziness developing
- Fetal movements reduced
- Significant abdominal pain with uterine tenderness
- History of previous APH with new bleed

### Severe criteria — life-threatening emergency, every minute counts
ANY ONE:
- Heavy uncontrolled bleeding — soaking multiple pads rapidly
- Maternal shock: collapse, cold clammy skin, rapid weak pulse, altered consciousness
- Board-hard uterus with bleeding
- Absent fetal movements with bleeding
- Syncope / collapse
- Signs of DIC: spontaneous bruising, bleeding from other sites

## MANAGEMENT BY SEVERITY

### Mild
UMMA should advise:
- Go to the hospital or general hospital today — not the PHC; she needs speculum examination, fetal monitoring, and likely ultrasound
- Do not have intercourse until seen and cleared by a doctor
- Do not insert anything into the vagina
- Apply a clean pad to monitor blood loss — bring the used pad to show the hospital
- Lie down and rest while arranging transport
- If she has a previous ultrasound showing low-lying placenta: tell the hospital immediately — this changes her management entirely
- Do not assume the bleeding has stopped and it is safe — placenta praevia will bleed again

**Plain language for UMMA to adapt:**
"Any bleeding from the vagina in pregnancy is something that needs to be checked at the hospital today — even if it has stopped. It does not matter if there is no pain. Please go to the general hospital now. Lie down while you arrange your transport. Put a clean pad on and take it with you to show the nurse. If you have had a scan that showed your placenta was low down, tell the hospital straight away. Do not wait to see if it bleeds again — you must go today."

### Moderate
UMMA should advise:
- Go to the hospital immediately — do not wait
- Lie flat during transport — keeps blood pressure distributed
- Do not attempt to walk long distances — conserve blood pressure
- Bring all ANC documents
- Someone must accompany her — she should not travel alone
- If available: call ahead to hospital to prepare for arrival
- Identify blood donor from family if possible — may be needed

**Plain language for UMMA to adapt:**
"You need to go to the hospital right now — this cannot wait. Lie down in the car on the way. Take someone with you. Bring your ANC card. Tell the doctors you are bleeding in pregnancy and whether you have pain. Think of someone in your family who could donate blood if needed — the hospital may ask. Do not stop at the health centre."

### Severe
UMMA should advise:
- Emergency — call for help immediately; go to hospital by fastest means
- Lie flat — legs slightly elevated if possible (improvised with cloth or pillow under buttocks)
- Apply firm pressure with clean cloth to perineum — do not pack inside the vagina
- Do not leave her alone — collapse risk
- Call ahead to AKTH or general hospital emergency
- At hospital: state immediately that she is bleeding heavily in pregnancy — she must be seen without waiting in queue
- Potential need for emergency caesarean section and blood transfusion — family should be told and a donor sought immediately

**Plain language for UMMA to adapt:**
"This is a life-threatening emergency. She is losing a lot of blood and needs to get to the hospital this minute. Lay her flat. Get there as fast as you can — do not stop anywhere. Call the hospital if you can and tell them a pregnant woman is coming in with heavy bleeding. When you arrive, do not wait — go straight to the nurse or doctor and say she is bleeding heavily in pregnancy. They may need to operate and give blood. Think right now about who in the family can donate blood."

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC WARNINGS

**Dangerous practices:**
- Waiting for bleeding to stop before going to hospital — placenta praevia bleeds are recurrent and escalating; the next bleed may be fatal
- Vaginal examination by TBA in suspected praevia — catastrophic haemorrhage can be triggered by digital vaginal examination when placenta praevia is present; no one should put fingers inside the vagina until praevia is excluded at hospital
- Herbal preparations to "stop" the bleeding or "hold" the pregnancy — ineffective; some cause uterine stimulation and worsen abruption
- Intercourse after a warning bleed — can precipitate catastrophic haemorrhage in praevia
- Attempting home delivery with known placenta praevia — almost certainly fatal
- Abdominal massage for pain of abruption — worsens placental separation

**Infrastructure limitations:**
- Ultrasound: essential for praevia diagnosis but not available at PHC; available at general hospitals and AKTH; private scan centres in Kano city widely used — many women have had scans but not been told their implications
- Emergency caesarean section: available at general hospitals and AKTH; theatre and anaesthesia availability variable, especially overnight
- Blood transfusion: major haemorrhage requires large-volume transfusion; blood bank stock at AKTH is most reliable in Kano; family replacement donors commonly required
- IV access and fluid resuscitation: available at general hospital level; PHC cannot manage major APH
- Neonatal care for premature infant delivered emergently: AKTH neonatal unit; limited capacity

**Cultural factors:**
- Grand multiparity: women with 5 or more deliveries have significantly higher placenta praevia risk due to uterine scarring; this is a very common presentation in Kano; UMMA should flag grand multiparity as a risk factor whenever APH occurs
- Previous caesarean section: increasingly common in Kano with urban hospital deliveries; previous CS scar increases risk of placenta praevia and the catastrophic condition of placenta accreta (placenta growing into uterine scar) — UMMA should ask about previous CS in any APH case
- Fear of caesarean section: women with praevia who require CS may refuse or delay; UMMA must communicate that in placenta praevia, there is no safe vaginal delivery option
- Husband's consent for caesarean: in some Kano households, CS requires husband's signature; this causes dangerous delay when husband is absent; UMMA should advise the woman that her life-threatening emergency authorises treatment without delay and help her contact her husband immediately

**High-risk subgroups:**
- Grand multiparas (≥5 deliveries) — highest praevia risk
- Women with previous caesarean section — praevia and accreta risk
- Women with previous uterine surgery — myomectomy, manual removal of placenta
- Multiple pregnancy — larger placental surface area; higher praevia risk
- Women with known low-lying placenta on early scan — must be rescanned at 32–36 weeks; if still praevia, plan for CS
- Women with pre-eclampsia or hypertension — highest abruption risk
- Women who have experienced abdominal trauma — road traffic accidents, domestic violence — abruption must be excluded after any abdominal blow in pregnancy
