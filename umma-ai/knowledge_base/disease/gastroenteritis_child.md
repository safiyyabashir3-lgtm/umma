# DISEASE FILE: GASTROENTERITIS AND DIARRHOEAL DISEASE IN A CHILD UNDER 5
## Nigerian Context (Kano / Northwest Priority) — Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## OVERVIEW
Diarrhoeal disease is the second leading cause of death in Nigerian children
under 5, accounting for approximately 23% of under-5 mortality. In Nigeria,
malaria and diarrhoea together account for 50% of all children's hospital
admissions. The leading causes in Kano include rotavirus, Salmonella, Shigella,
and cholera during outbreak periods. Poor sanitation, unsafe water, and high
malnutrition rates in Kano drive high incidence and severity. Death from
diarrhoea is caused by dehydration — assessment of hydration status is
the most critical clinical task. Malnourished children dehydrate faster
and die sooner.

---

## HOW IT PRESENTS IN NIGERIAN CHILDREN UNDER 5

### Mild Presentation — No Dehydration
- Loose or watery stool — increased frequency
- Child alert and active
- Drinking normally
- Eyes normal — not sunken
- Mouth and tongue moist
- Skin pinch returns immediately
- Passing urine normally

### Moderate Presentation — Some Dehydration
- Loose watery stool — frequent
- Child restless and irritable or more lethargic than usual
- Eyes appear sunken
- Mouth and tongue dry
- Drinks eagerly when offered — very thirsty
- Skin pinch returns slowly — 1 to 2 seconds
- Reduced urine output
- Vomiting may be present

### Severe Presentation — Severe Dehydration, Emergency
- Child unconscious or very difficult to rouse
- Eyes deeply sunken
- Unable to drink — too weak or unconscious
- Mouth and tongue very dry
- Skin pinch returns very slowly — more than 2 seconds
- No urine output
- Sunken fontanelle in infant
- Rapid weak pulse
- Child limp or unresponsive

## DIFFERENTIATING FEATURES BY CAUSE

### Viral gastroenteritis (rotavirus most common)
- Profuse watery stool — no blood
- Vomiting prominent at onset
- Low-grade fever or no fever
- Age under 2 years most common
- Rapid dehydration risk

### Bacterial dysentery (Shigella)
- Blood and mucus in stool — key feature
- High fever
- Abdominal cramps
- Child straining to pass stool
- Can cause seizures — Shigella toxin

### Typhoid (Salmonella Typhi)
- Prolonged fever — worsening each day
- Diarrhoea or constipation
- Abdominal pain and distension
- Lethargy — child looks very unwell beyond just diarrhoea
- Fever beyond 3 days with no improvement

### Cholera
- Rice-water stool — profuse, almost colourless
- Rapid severe dehydration — within hours
- Little or no fever
- Vomiting present
- Community outbreak — others affected

## SEVERITY ASSESSMENT

### No dehydration — mild
- Alert, drinking normally, eyes and mouth normal, skin pinch immediate

### Some dehydration — moderate, urgent
- Two or more of: restless/lethargic, sunken eyes, drinks eagerly,
  slow skin pinch

### Severe dehydration — emergency
- Two or more of: unconscious/floppy, very sunken eyes, cannot drink,
  very slow skin pinch

### Additional escalation triggers regardless of dehydration assessment
- Blood in stool with fever
- Suspected cholera — any age
- Child under 2 months with diarrhoea
- Malnourished child with any diarrhoea
- Diarrhoea persisting more than 14 days

---

## MANAGEMENT BY SEVERITY

### No Dehydration — Home ORS
- Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) — give after every loose stool
- Continue breastfeeding — do not stop
- Continue feeding — do not withhold food
- Zinc supplementation — 10mg daily under 6 months, 20mg daily
  6 months and over, for 10 to 14 days
- Return immediately if child worsens, develops sunken eyes, or cannot drink

Plain language for caregiver:
"Your child is not severely dehydrated yet but needs extra fluids to stay
that way. Give ORS after every loose stool. Keep breastfeeding. Keep feeding
normally. Come back immediately if the child gets worse or cannot drink."

### Some Dehydration — Urgent Clinic
- ORS 75ml per kg over 4 hours at clinic — supervised rehydration
- Reassess after 4 hours
- If improving — continue ORS at home
- Zinc supplementation
- If not improving or vomiting all ORS — hospital

Plain language for caregiver:
"Your child is showing signs of dehydration and needs to go to the clinic
today to get the right amount of fluids under supervision. Please go now."

### Severe Dehydration — Emergency
- IV fluids immediately — hospital only
- This is life-threatening
- Do not attempt home rehydration for severe dehydration
- Go to hospital immediately

Plain language for caregiver:
"Your child is severely dehydrated and needs hospital treatment immediately.
This is an emergency. Please go now — do not wait."

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC WARNINGS
- ORS sachets are available at most pharmacies and patent medicine vendors
  in Kano — caregivers should always have some at home
- Home sugar-salt solution is a second option only if ORS unavailable —
  never recommend soft drinks, juices, or energy drinks for rehydration
- Antibiotics are NOT needed for most watery diarrhoea — only for
  confirmed bacterial dysentery or cholera
- Do not withhold food or breastfeeding during diarrhoea — this worsens
  malnutrition and slows recovery
- Herbal purgatives sometimes given by caregivers in northern Nigeria
  worsen diarrhoea significantly — always ask and advise to stop
- Cholera outbreaks occur in Kano — during any known outbreak, profuse
  watery diarrhoea must be treated as cholera until proven otherwise
- Malnourished child with diarrhoea — always escalate, they cannot
  tolerate dehydration
