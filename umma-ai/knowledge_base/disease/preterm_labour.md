# DISEASE FILE: PRETERM LABOUR
## Nigerian Context (Kano / Northwest Priority) — Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## OVERVIEW
Preterm labour — onset of labour before 37 completed weeks of pregnancy —
is a leading cause of neonatal death in Nigeria. Preterm birth accounts
for a significant proportion of the approximately 300,000 neonatal deaths
Nigeria records annually. In northern Nigeria, preterm birth rates are
high, driven by malaria in pregnancy, anaemia, infections including UTI
and malaria, poor antenatal care attendance, and young maternal age.
Many women in Kano do not know their exact gestational age, making
identification of preterm labour more challenging. Tocolysis — medications
to stop labour — has limited evidence and is rarely available at primary
level in Kano. The priority is prompt referral to a facility with neonatal
care capacity before delivery occurs. Antenatal corticosteroids — given
to mature the baby's lungs — are available at secondary and tertiary
facilities in Kano and dramatically improve preterm infant survival.

---

## HOW IT PRESENTS

### Threatened Preterm Labour — May Settle
- Irregular uterine contractions — tightenings, before 37 weeks
- Pelvic pressure or low back pain that comes and goes
- Vaginal discharge — increased or mucus-like
- No cervical change confirmed on examination
- Contractions irregular and may stop with rest and hydration

### Active Preterm Labour — Escalate Urgently
- Regular painful contractions — coming every 10 minutes or more
  frequently, before 37 weeks
- Contractions lasting 30 to 60 seconds each
- Low back pain rhythmically coinciding with contractions
- Pelvic pressure — feeling baby is pushing down
- Contractions not stopping with rest
- Cervical changes on examination — dilation or effacement
  (only assessable by trained health worker)

### Preterm Labour with Complications — Emergency
- Rupture of membranes — fluid leaking or gushing from vagina before
  37 weeks (PPROM — preterm premature rupture of membranes)
- Fever alongside contractions — suggests infection as trigger
  (chorioamnionitis)
- Vaginal bleeding with contractions — may indicate abruption
- Cord prolapse — cord coming through cervix — immediate emergency
- Fetal distress signs — reduced or absent fetal movement

---

## DIFFERENTIATING FEATURES
- Braxton Hicks contractions — normal, irregular, not painful, do not
  increase in frequency, settle with position change or hydration
- True preterm labour contractions — regular, painful, increasing in
  frequency, do not settle
- UTI and pyelonephritis — common triggers for preterm labour in Nigeria;
  always treat UTI promptly to reduce preterm risk
- Malaria — a leading cause of preterm labour in Nigeria; fever from
  malaria can trigger contractions
- Preterm premature rupture of membranes — fluid leaking, no pain
  initially, but infection risk is immediate

---

## SEVERITY ASSESSMENT

### Threatened — Urgent but not immediately emergency
- Irregular contractions before 37 weeks, settling with rest,
  no fluid leaking, no fever, fetal movement present

### Active preterm labour — Urgent, same-day hospital
- Regular contractions before 37 weeks, not settling,
  no complications yet

### Complicated preterm labour — Emergency
- Any of: fluid leaking, fever, bleeding, reduced fetal movement,
  cord visible, rapid cervical progress

---

## MANAGEMENT BY SEVERITY

### Threatened Preterm Labour
- Hospital today — same day
- Rest and hydration — may be sufficient if very early and irregular
- Exclude infection — urine test, malaria test
- Treat any identified infection promptly
- Antenatal corticosteroids — steroid injection to mature baby's lungs
  — given at hospital between 24 and 34 weeks gestation; dramatically
  improves baby's survival if birth occurs
- Cervical assessment by trained health worker

Plain language for caregiver:
"The tightenings you are feeling before your baby is due may mean your
body is trying to start labour too early. This needs to be checked at
hospital today. Try to rest and drink water while you prepare to go.
An injection is available at the hospital that can help your baby's
lungs develop faster in case the baby comes early — this is very
important to receive. Please go now."

### Active Preterm Labour
- Emergency — hospital immediately
- Antenatal corticosteroids urgently if between 24 and 34 weeks and
  not already given
- Tocolysis may be considered by clinician — nifedipine most available
  in Kano setting — to buy time for steroids to work
- Magnesium sulphate — for neuroprotection of baby below 32 weeks —
  available at AKTH Kano
- Neonatal team alert — baby may need neonatal unit support

Plain language for caregiver:
"Your body is starting to deliver your baby too early. You need to go
to hospital immediately so that the baby can be delivered safely and
receive the care a premature baby needs. Please go now — do not wait."

### Complicated — Fluid Leaking, Fever, Bleeding
- Immediate emergency — hospital now
- PPROM — risk of cord prolapse and infection — do not place anything
  in the vagina, lie down, go immediately
- Chorioamnionitis — IV antibiotics needed urgently
- Cord prolapse — head-down position if cord visible, immediate delivery

Plain language for caregiver:
"This is an emergency for you and your baby. Please go to hospital
immediately — right now. Do not stop anywhere."

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC WARNINGS
- Antenatal corticosteroids — betamethasone or dexamethasone — are
  available at AKTH and MMSH Kano and are life-saving; many women
  and even some health workers are unaware of their importance
- Magnesium sulphate for neuroprotection of the preterm infant is
  available at AKTH Kano — should be given before 32 weeks where possible
- Malaria is a leading preventable cause of preterm labour in Kano —
  malaria prevention with nets and intermittent preventive treatment
  in pregnancy (IPTp) must be reinforced at every antenatal contact
- Many women in Kano do not know their gestational age accurately —
  any woman with regular painful contractions who may be less than 37
  weeks should be treated as possible preterm labour
- Traditional practices — herbal medicines, uterine massage — may be
  sought before hospital in some Kano communities; these can worsen
  preterm labour and must be discouraged sensitively
- Neonatal intensive care capacity in Kano is concentrated at AKTH —
  very preterm babies (below 32 weeks) should ideally be born at AKTH
- Home delivery planned by some families in Kano — strongly discourage
  in the context of preterm labour, which carries high neonatal mortality
  without skilled neonatal support
