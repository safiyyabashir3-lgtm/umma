# DISEASE FILE: PLACENTAL ABRUPTION
## Nigerian Context (Kano / Northwest Priority) — Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## OVERVIEW
Placental abruption — premature separation of the placenta from the uterine
wall before delivery — is one of the leading causes of maternal and perinatal
death in Nigeria. It accounts for a significant proportion of antepartum
haemorrhage in Nigerian hospitals. Abruption ranges from mild partial separation
with minimal bleeding to complete separation causing catastrophic haemorrhage,
fetal death, and maternal shock. In northern Nigeria, pre-eclampsia and
hypertension are the leading predisposing causes. Trauma — including road
traffic accidents and in some cases domestic violence — is also a recognised
cause. Many women in Kano present late after significant blood loss has
already occurred, reducing survival chances. Placental abruption is
a time-critical emergency — outcome depends entirely on speed of reaching
hospital with surgical and transfusion capacity.

---

## HOW IT PRESENTS

### Mild Abruption — Partial Separation
- Mild to moderate vaginal bleeding — dark blood
- Mild uterine tenderness — uterus tender to touch
- Uterine contractions — may trigger preterm labour
- Fetal movements may be reduced
- No signs of maternal shock
- Woman anxious but alert

### Moderate Abruption
- Moderate vaginal bleeding — may be concealed internally with little
  visible external bleeding despite significant internal haemorrhage
- Significant uterine pain and tenderness — constant, not just with
  contractions
- Hard, board-like uterus — constant uterine contraction (hypertonic)
- Fetal movements significantly reduced
- Woman looks unwell — pale, anxious, in pain
- Early shock signs — fast heart rate, feeling faint

### Severe Abruption — Emergency, Life-Threatening
- Heavy vaginal bleeding OR minimal bleeding with severe concealed
  haemorrhage — the absence of heavy bleeding does NOT mean abruption
  is mild
- Severe constant abdominal pain — uterus hard as a board
- Maternal shock — very fast weak pulse, cold clammy skin, confusion,
  extremely low blood pressure, feeling of collapse
- Fetal distress or fetal death — absent fetal movement, absent fetal
  heart sounds
- Coagulopathy — bleeding from other sites, oozing from gums or IV sites
  — blood not clotting
- Oliguria — no urine output
- Woman losing consciousness

---

## DIFFERENTIATING FEATURES
- Placental abruption vs placenta praevia:
  Abruption — painful bleeding, tender hard uterus, fetal distress
  Praevia — painless bright red bleeding, soft non-tender uterus,
  fetal usually unaffected initially
- Concealed abruption — NO visible bleeding but severe constant uterine
  pain and maternal shock; this is the most dangerous pattern because
  caregivers may be falsely reassured by absence of visible blood
- Uterine rupture — also presents with sudden severe pain and shock;
  clinically very similar and equally dangerous; both are emergencies
- Pre-eclampsia commonly coexists — ask about headache, visual symptoms,
  swelling; this combination dramatically worsens prognosis

---

## SEVERITY ASSESSMENT

### Mild
- Small amount of bleeding, mild tenderness, fetal movements present,
  no maternal shock

### Moderate
- Significant bleeding or concealed haemorrhage, uterine tenderness,
  reduced fetal movement, early shock signs

### Severe — Immediate Emergency
- Any of: maternal shock, absent fetal movement or fetal heart,
  board-like uterus, coagulopathy, loss of consciousness, no urine
  output, very heavy bleeding

---

## MANAGEMENT BY SEVERITY

### Mild
- Emergency — hospital immediately even if bleeding seems small
- Do not wait to see if bleeding settles — abruption can worsen rapidly
- No internal examination at home — nothing in the vagina
- Lie down during transfer
- IV access needed at hospital — blood transfusion may be needed

Plain language for caregiver:
"Bleeding in pregnancy with tummy pain is an emergency that needs
hospital assessment immediately. Even if the bleeding seems small,
the placenta may be separating inside and this can become very
dangerous very quickly. Please go to hospital right now — do not wait."

### Moderate
- Immediate emergency — hospital now
- Blood transfusion likely needed — available at AKTH and MMSH Kano
- Emergency delivery likely — caesarean section or assisted vaginal
  depending on clinical assessment
- Fetal monitoring — fetal heart rate assessment urgently

Plain language for caregiver:
"This is a serious emergency for you and your baby. The bleeding and
pain suggest the placenta may be coming away. You need emergency
hospital care right now. Please go immediately."

### Severe
- Immediate life-threatening emergency
- Fastest possible transport to hospital with surgical and blood
  transfusion capacity
- Do not stop at any lower-level facility — go directly to AKTH
  or MMSH Kano if at all reachable
- While waiting for transport — lie flat, keep warm

Plain language for caregiver:
"This is a life-threatening emergency for both you and your baby.
Go to the largest hospital immediately — right now. Every minute
matters. Do not stop anywhere on the way."

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC WARNINGS
- Concealed abruption — internal bleeding with little external blood —
  is the most dangerous pattern and is frequently underestimated
  by caregivers and sometimes by health workers at primary level
- Blood transfusion is essential in moderate to severe abruption —
  available at AKTH and MMSH Kano; primary health centres cannot
  manage this condition
- Pre-eclampsia is the leading predisposing cause of abruption in
  northern Nigeria — all women with pre-eclampsia must be counselled
  on the risk of abruption and the importance of immediate return
  if abdominal pain or bleeding develops
- Domestic violence — a recognised but underreported cause of
  abruption in Nigeria; must be considered and approached
  sensitively without accusation
- Trauma from road traffic accidents on Kano roads — abdominal trauma
  in pregnancy must always raise suspicion of abruption even if
  no immediate bleeding occurs
- Traditional practices — abdominal massage by traditional birth
  attendants in some Kano communities — can trigger or worsen abruption
- Coagulopathy (DIC) complicates severe abruption — blood not clotting —
  requires fresh frozen plasma and platelets available only at
  tertiary level
- Delay in reaching hospital is the primary cause of abruption deaths
  in Kano — transport barriers and late presentation are critical
  issues; emergency transport planning should be discussed antenatally
