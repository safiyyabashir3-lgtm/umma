# DISEASE FILE: SEVERE ACUTE MALNUTRITION (SAM)
## Nigerian Context (Kano / Northwest Priority) — Awaiting Clinician Verification

---

## OVERVIEW
Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) is a life-threatening condition and one of
the leading causes of child death in northern Nigeria. Kano State has among
the highest malnutrition rates in Nigeria — over 62% of under-5 children
are affected by some form of malnutrition. SAM affects approximately
1 in 5 children in some Kano communities. Nigeria has the second highest
number of SAM-affected children in the world. SAM dramatically worsens
the outcome of every other childhood illness — malaria, pneumonia, diarrhoea,
and measles all carry higher mortality in malnourished children.
Kano State has therapeutic feeding centres (TFCs) and community-based
management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) programmes — referral to these
is appropriate and life-saving.

---

## HOW IT PRESENTS

### Marasmus — Severe Wasting Without Oedema
- Severe visible wasting — child looks like skin stretched over bones
- Ribs, shoulder blades, and hip bones very prominent
- Buttocks — loose baggy skin, flat, described as baggy trousers sign
- Face — old man appearance, sunken cheeks, prominent cheekbones
- Arms and legs — extremely thin, little muscle visible
- Hair — may be sparse but usually normal colour in marasmus
- Child may be alert but very weak
- Hungry — appetite often preserved in marasmus
- No swelling of feet

### Kwashiorkor — Oedematous Malnutrition
- Bilateral pitting oedema — swelling of both feet minimum
  (press for 3 seconds — remains indented = pitting oedema)
- Swelling may extend to legs, hands, and face in severe cases
- Child may not look as wasted — oedema can mask thinness
- Hair — thin, reddish, light-coloured, falls out easily
- Skin — peeling and cracking, dark patches, called flaky paint sign
- Apathetic — very quiet, withdrawn, miserable expression
- Poor appetite — unlike marasmus
- Enlarged liver — abdomen may look distended
- Child not interested in surroundings or play

### Marasmic Kwashiorkor — Mixed Type
- Features of both severe wasting AND bilateral oedema
- Most severe and highest mortality form
- Urgently needs inpatient care

---

## DIFFERENTIATING FEATURES
- Bilateral oedema of feet — always confirms oedematous SAM (kwashiorkor)
  regardless of how child looks otherwise
- Severe visible wasting without oedema — marasmus
- MUAC less than 115mm — confirms SAM if measured
- Weight for height Z-score less than -3 — confirms SAM
- In community setting without measurements — visible severe wasting or
  bilateral foot oedema is sufficient to diagnose SAM

## SEVERITY ASSESSMENT

### Uncomplicated SAM — eligible for outpatient treatment (CMAM)
All of the following must be true:
- Alert and responsive
- Appetite present — passes appetite test (can eat RUTF)
- No bilateral oedema or mild oedema of feet only (+)
- No medical complications
- Caregiver able to attend CMAM programme

### Complicated SAM — requires inpatient treatment (TFC / hospital)
Any ONE of the following:
- Severe bilateral oedema — swelling extending above ankles (++ or +++)
- No appetite — failing appetite test
- Medical complication present — pneumonia, malaria, severe anaemia,
  severe dehydration, tuberculosis, severe skin infection
- Altered consciousness or convulsion
- Severe anaemia with fast breathing
- Hypoglycaemia signs — sudden deterioration, sweating, seizure,
  loss of consciousness
- Child under 6 months with SAM — always inpatient
- Marasmic kwashiorkor — always inpatient

---

## MANAGEMENT BY SEVERITY

### Uncomplicated SAM — Outpatient CMAM
- Ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF) — Plumpy'Nut — weekly ration
- Treatment of any infections — routine antibiotics per protocol
- Vitamin A, folic acid, measles vaccination if not done
- Weekly or fortnightly clinic visits
- Danger signs to watch for — return immediately if develops
- Continue breastfeeding throughout

Plain language for caregiver:
"Your child has severe malnutrition that needs special treatment food
and regular clinic visits. There are centres in Kano that provide this
for free. Your child can be treated at home with this special food if
they are eating and have no other illness. I will explain where to go."

### Complicated SAM — Inpatient TFC / Hospital
- Hospital or therapeutic feeding centre admission
- Stabilisation phase — F75 therapeutic milk, treat complications
- Transition phase — F100 or RUTF when stable
- Management of concurrent infections — malaria, pneumonia, sepsis
- Management of hypoglycaemia — glucose
- Careful rehydration — standard WHO ORS for SAM not normal ORS
- Emotional stimulation and play therapy during recovery

Plain language for caregiver:
"Your child is very seriously ill and needs to stay in hospital or a
feeding centre for treatment. This is not something that can be managed
at home right now. The centre will provide food and medicine for your
child. Please go today — this is urgent."

## NIGERIAN SPECIFIC WARNINGS
- Kano State has CMAM sites and therapeutic feeding centres — always
  refer where accessible; caregivers are often unaware these exist
- RUTF — Plumpy'Nut — is provided free through government programmes
  in Kano — cost should not be a barrier
- Do not give normal family food alone as treatment for SAM — it does
  not contain the right nutrients in the right amounts
- Rehydration in SAM uses a special low-sodium ORS — standard ORS
  can cause dangerous fluid shifts — never give IV fluids for SAM
  dehydration outside hospital
- Hypoglycaemia is a major killer in SAM — a child with SAM who
  becomes suddenly worse or unconscious may be hypoglycaemic —
  small amount of sugar water by mouth if conscious while going to hospital
- Common cultural practices in northern Nigeria — giving herbal medicines,
  restricting certain foods — must be addressed sensitively
- Mothers of SAM children are often also food insecure — referral to
  social support is part of complete management
- Measles vaccination status must always be checked — measles is
  devastating in a malnourished child
- All SAM children must be screened for TB in Kano given high TB burden
  in northern Nigeria
