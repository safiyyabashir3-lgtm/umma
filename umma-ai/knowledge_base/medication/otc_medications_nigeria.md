# otc_medications_nigeria.md
# UMMA Medication File — Common OTC Medicines at Nigerian Patent Medicine Vendors
# Category: Medications | Population: Pregnant women and children under 5
# Context: Patent medicine vendors (PMVs), open markets, and roadside chemists in Kano and northwest Nigeria
# Last reviewed: 2025
# NOTE: UMMA never gives doses. Always refer to doctor or pharmacist for dosing decisions.

---

## OVERVIEW

Patent medicine vendors (PMVs) are the first point of contact for illness in the majority of households in Kano and northwest Nigeria. They sell prescription and non-prescription medications without prescription, often in unregulated quantities and without counselling. For pregnant women and children under 5, many commonly sold PMV medications are inappropriate, ineffective, or dangerous.

This file gives UMMA a working reference for the most commonly purchased medications at PMVs in the Kano context, with specific guidance on safety for pregnant women and young children.

---

## COMMONLY SOLD PMV MEDICATIONS — SAFETY REFERENCE

### Paracetamol (Emzor, Panadol, M&B)
**Pregnancy:** SAFE — first choice for pain and fever
**Children under 5:** SAFE — use weight-appropriate dose; get syrup not adult tablets
**UMMA note:** The safest and most appropriate OTC medicine for this population. Always recommend over ibuprofen or aspirin.

---

### Ibuprofen (Brufen, Felvin, Advil, Emzor Ibuprofen)
**Pregnancy:** UNSAFE — avoid especially in third trimester; can affect fetal heart and kidneys
**Children under 5:** CAUTION — avoid under 3 months; avoid in any child with suspected malaria; paracetamol is safer first choice
**UMMA note:** Very commonly recommended at PMVs for fever and pain. UMMA should redirect to paracetamol in all cases for this population.

---

### Aspirin (various)
**Pregnancy:** UNSAFE at standard doses — bleeding risk, fetal ductus arteriosus closure
**Children under 5:** UNSAFE — Reye syndrome risk; never give to children
**UMMA note:** Still widely sold and recommended. UMMA must clearly advise against for both populations.

---

### Chloroquine (Malaricin, Nivaquine)
**Pregnancy:** INEFFECTIVE for falciparum malaria in Nigeria — do not use for treatment
**Children under 5:** INEFFECTIVE — widespread resistance means this will not treat malaria; delays proper treatment
**UMMA note:** One of the most dangerous common PMV practices — selling chloroquine for malaria when it does not work. UMMA should always redirect to ACT from a clinic.

---

### Artemether alone (Artenam and various single-agent artemether)
**Pregnancy:** INSUFFICIENT — use ACT (combination) instead; monotherapy in first trimester requires clinician guidance
**Children under 5:** INSUFFICIENT — must use combination ACT; monotherapy causes treatment failure
**UMMA note:** Very widely sold. UMMA must always specify that the combination (artemether + lumefantrine = Lonart, Coartem) is required, not artemether alone.

---

### Lonart / Coartem (artemether-lumefantrine)
**Pregnancy:** SAFE in second and third trimester — first-line treatment for uncomplicated malaria
**Children under 5:** SAFE — first-line treatment; dose is weight-based; needs to be appropriate paediatric formulation
**UMMA note:** The correct malaria treatment. PMVs do sell it — but dosing without weighing the child is a significant problem. Always advise clinic or pharmacist to determine the right dose for the child's weight.

---

### Ciprofloxacin (Cipro, Ciprotab)
**Pregnancy:** AVOID — joint/cartilage concerns; safer antibiotics available for most indications
**Children under 5:** AVOID — not safe for routine use in young children; damages developing cartilage
**UMMA note:** Extremely widely sold in Kano for any infection. Often used for UTI, diarrhoea, respiratory infections — for which it is frequently inappropriate. UMMA should redirect to clinic for correct antibiotic prescription.

---

### Amoxicillin (Amoxil, various generics — capsules and syrups)
**Pregnancy:** SAFE — appropriate first-line antibiotic for many infections when prescribed
**Children under 5:** SAFE — appropriate for pneumonia and bacterial infections when prescribed; dose is weight-based
**UMMA note:** Correct antibiotic for many common infections. Problem is: sold at PMVs without assessment of whether the infection is bacterial, what dose is correct, and for how long. UMMA should advise clinic assessment before use.

---

### Co-trimoxazole / Septrin (trimethoprim + sulphamethoxazole)
**Pregnancy:** CAUTION — avoid in first trimester; avoid near term; use only when prescribed for specific indications (e.g. HIV prophylaxis)
**Children under 5:** CAUTION — still used in some protocols but high resistance rates for common organisms; not first-line for most infections; appropriate for PCP prophylaxis in HIV-positive children
**UMMA note:** Very widely used at PMVs for all respiratory and urinary infections. Resistance is high for common pneumonia-causing organisms. UMMA should advise against self-purchasing for non-specific infections.

---

### Metronidazole / Flagyl (various)
**Pregnancy:** SAFE in second and third trimester for appropriate indications; avoid in first trimester without medical guidance
**Children under 5:** SAFE for specific indications (intestinal amoeba, giardia, anaerobic infections) when prescribed; dose is weight-based
**UMMA note:** Widely and often correctly used. Problem is self-diagnosis and inappropriate use (e.g. for watery diarrhoea which is usually viral and does not require metronidazole). Advise clinic assessment for persistent or bloody diarrhoea.

---

### Tramadol (various brands — capsules very widely sold)
**Pregnancy:** AVOID — neonatal withdrawal syndrome; respiratory depression in newborn
**Children under 5:** NEVER — respiratory depression risk; not approved for children
**UMMA note:** Epidemic use in Kano — used for pain, recreation, and to manage crying in children (the last is particularly dangerous). UMMA must clearly advise against in both populations.

---

### Loperamide / Imodium
**Pregnancy:** AVOID — limited safety data; not needed when ORS is available
**Children under 5:** NEVER in children under 2; dangerous at any age in young children — causes ileus and respiratory depression
**UMMA note:** Sold widely for diarrhoea. UMMA must clearly redirect to ORS + zinc.

---

### Cough syrups (Benylin, Actifed, Cosylan, Piriton Syrup, various)
**Pregnancy:** CAUTION — ingredients vary widely; some contain codeine (avoid), antihistamines (use with caution), alcohol (avoid); read ingredients or ask pharmacist
**Children under 5:** AVOID — no proven benefit; ingredients including antihistamines, codeine, and dextromethorphan are potentially harmful in young children
**UMMA note:** Among the most commonly purchased items at PMVs for respiratory illness in children. No cough syrup is appropriate first-line for a child with cough and fever — the child needs clinical assessment for pneumonia.

---

### Vitamin C (various syrups and tablets)
**Pregnancy:** SAFE — at standard supplement doses; not a treatment for infections but supports immune function; also enhances iron absorption
**Children under 5:** SAFE at supplement doses
**UMMA note:** Harmless supplement. UMMA does not need to discourage, but should clarify it does not treat malaria or infection.

---

### B-complex vitamins
**Pregnancy:** SAFE — B vitamins are beneficial in pregnancy; some formulations used for nausea (B6/pyridoxine)
**Children under 5:** SAFE at supplement doses
**UMMA note:** Harmless and sometimes helpful. Pyridoxine (B6) specifically has evidence for reducing nausea in early pregnancy.

---

### "Blood tonics" — Haematogen, Astymin, various iron syrups
**Pregnancy:** VARIABLE — some are appropriate iron supplements; some contain alcohol; some contain inappropriate additives; read the label
**Children under 5:** VARIABLE — some are appropriate; some contain alcohol (unsafe in infants); some have excessive vitamin A doses
**UMMA note:** Extremely widely used. UMMA should advise that ANC-prescribed iron and folic acid is the correct and safe option for anaemia in pregnancy. For children, clinic-prescribed iron is preferable to OTC blood tonics.

---

### Oral contraceptives (various combined pills)
**Pregnancy:** AVOID — if already pregnant, taking combined pills does not terminate the pregnancy but is unnecessary; progestogen-only pills are generally considered low risk if taken inadvertently in early pregnancy
**UMMA note:** Women who take OCP before knowing they are pregnant do not need to panic — advise ANC disclosure.

---

### Misoprostol / Cytotec
**Pregnancy:** DANGEROUS outside medical supervision — see unsafe_medications_pregnancy.md
**UMMA note:** Sold on the informal market in Kano. UMMA should never endorse home use. If a woman has taken it, direct to hospital immediately.

## KEY MESSAGES FOR UMMA ABOUT PMV MEDICINES

1. **PMV is not a substitute for clinical diagnosis.** The most dangerous pattern is buying medicines at a PMV for malaria, respiratory infections, or diarrhoea without confirming the diagnosis — the wrong medicine for the wrong condition delays correct treatment.

2. **The three most dangerous PMV practices for this population:**
   - Chloroquine for malaria (ineffective — child/mother deteriorates while "treated")
   - Cough syrup and antibiotics for pneumonia in children (no antibiotic for pneumonia = child dies)
   - Loperamide for diarrhoea in children (stops gut movement; does not prevent dehydration)

3. **Dose without weight is dangerous for children.** PMVs routinely dispense without weighing children. UMMA should always advise clinic or pharmacist to confirm dose.

4. **If in doubt, go to the clinic.** UMMA's message when unsure about PMV medication: "Before buying any medicine, it is worth going to the health centre first — they can confirm what the illness is and give you the right medicine at the right amount."
