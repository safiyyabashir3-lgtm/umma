# Danger Signs in Pregnancy

**Topic:** Warning Signs During Pregnancy That Need Urgent Care
**Guideline Source:** Nigerian Safe Motherhood Guidelines; FMOH Nigeria; WHO
**Context:** Kano State, Northern Nigeria

---

## OVERVIEW

Most pregnancies go well. But sometimes serious problems develop — and they can develop quickly. Knowing the danger signs of pregnancy can save your life and your baby's life.

A **danger sign** is a symptom that means something may be seriously wrong. When you see a danger sign, you should go to a health facility **immediately** — do not wait, do not try to treat it at home first, do not wait for morning if it is night.

In Nigeria, too many mothers and babies die from conditions that could have been treated — if the woman reached care in time. This file will help you recognise the signs and act fast.

---

## KEY INFORMATION

### The 8 Main Danger Signs in Pregnancy

**1. Heavy vaginal bleeding**
Blood coming from your vagina at any point in pregnancy is a danger sign. A small amount of spotting in early pregnancy may not be serious, but heavy bleeding — soaking a pad or more — always needs urgent care.

**2. Severe headache**
A very bad headache that does not go away, especially with blurry vision or seeing spots, can be a sign of pre-eclampsia (high blood pressure in pregnancy). This is a life-threatening condition.

**3. Blurry vision or seeing flashing lights/spots**
This is a sign of dangerously high blood pressure. It is an emergency.

**4. Swelling of the face, hands, or feet — especially sudden swelling**
Some swelling in the feet and ankles is normal in late pregnancy. But sudden or severe swelling of the face or hands is a warning sign of pre-eclampsia.

**5. Severe abdominal (belly) pain**
Strong, constant pain in the belly — not normal labour contractions — can mean serious problems like placental abruption (the placenta separating from the womb) or a ruptured ectopic pregnancy.

**6. Fever with chills or shaking**
A high temperature during pregnancy can mean infection — including malaria, which is especially dangerous in pregnancy. Fever needs prompt treatment.

**7. Baby not moving (after 28 weeks)**
By 28 weeks (7 months), you should feel your baby move every day. If your baby stops moving or moves much less than usual for a whole day, go to the clinic immediately. This may mean the baby is in distress.

**8. Fits or convulsions**
Sudden uncontrolled shaking of the body during pregnancy is an emergency. It is called eclampsia and it can be fatal without immediate treatment. Call for help and get to hospital immediately.

### Additional warning signs:
- Water breaking (fluid leaking from vagina) before labour starts
- Burning or pain when urinating (may indicate a urinary tract infection that needs treatment)
- Severe vomiting that prevents you from keeping any food or water down
- Feeling faint or actually fainting

## NIGERIAN CONTEXT

**Why danger signs are missed in Kano and northern Nigeria:**

- Some women are not told what danger signs are at their antenatal visits
- Some women are discouraged from going to hospital at night or without their husband's permission
- Some families believe that pain and difficulty in pregnancy are normal and must be endured
- Distance to hospitals and cost of transport cause delays — and delays cost lives
- Fear of hospitals, male providers, or how they will be treated sometimes prevents women from going

**The Three Delays that cause maternal death in Nigeria:**

1. **Delay in deciding to seek care** — not recognising the danger sign or thinking it will pass
2. **Delay in reaching care** — transport problems, distance, cost, road conditions
3. **Delay in receiving care** — being turned away or waiting too long at the facility

Knowing danger signs helps overcome the first delay. Planning ahead — knowing where to go, how to get there, and who will take you — helps overcome the second.

**Emergency numbers and facilities in Kano:**
- **Murtala Muhammad Specialist Hospital, Kano** — has emergency obstetric care (EmOC) available
- **Aminu Kano Teaching Hospital (AKTH)** — full emergency services
- **General Hospitals** in each LGA — ask your PHC about the nearest general hospital
- **Nigeria Emergency Medical Services:** Ask your community health worker for local numbers in your LGA

**If you cannot afford transport:**
- Ask a neighbour, relative, or community member for help
- Some communities have emergency transport arrangements — ask your ward focal person or community health worker
- Kano State has **Community Volunteer Networks** in some LGAs — your CHEW can connect you

## COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

**"These symptoms will pass on their own — it is just pregnancy."**
Some discomfort is normal in pregnancy. But the danger signs listed here are different — they mean something may be seriously wrong inside your body. Waiting for them to pass can be fatal. When you see a danger sign, act immediately.

**"It is not good to go to hospital at night."**
Emergencies do not wait for morning. Hospitals and maternity units are open at night for this reason. Delaying until morning with a danger sign like seizures, heavy bleeding, or no fetal movement can result in death. Your life is more valuable than the inconvenience of going at night.

**"If my husband is not here, I must wait for him."**
Your life and your baby's life do not wait. If you have a danger sign and your husband is not available, tell another family member, neighbour, or community leader immediately. Any adult can help you reach care.

**"Convulsions in pregnancy are caused by evil spirits or jinn."**
Convulsions during pregnancy (eclampsia) are caused by dangerously high blood pressure. This is a medical emergency. Prayer is important and good — and the woman also needs medicine and hospital care urgently. Both can happen at the same time.

**"Heavy bleeding after delivery is normal."**
Some bleeding after delivery is normal, but heavy bleeding — soaking more than one pad per hour, or passing large clots — is a danger sign called postpartum haemorrhage. It is the leading cause of maternal death in Nigeria. Seek help immediately.

## PRACTICAL ADVICE

**Make an emergency plan NOW — before you need it:**

1. **Choose your delivery facility** during your antenatal care — know exactly where you will go.

2. **Identify your transport** — who will take you? How will you reach them quickly? What if it is night? Have a backup option.

3. **Save emergency money** — even a small amount set aside for transport and hospital fees can save your life. Discuss this with your husband and family now.

4. **Identify a birth companion** — someone who will stay with you and can recognise danger signs. Teach her this list too.

5. **Know the danger signs yourself and share them** — tell your husband, your mother-in-law, your sister. The more people around you who know these signs, the safer you are.

6. **Do not lock your door alone** — in late pregnancy, always make sure someone knows where you are and can check on you.

7. **If in doubt, go** — it is always better to be checked and told everything is fine than to wait and find out it was serious. No health worker will punish you for coming to be checked.

---

## WHEN TO SEEK CARE

**Go immediately — do not wait — if you experience ANY of these:**

🔴 Heavy bleeding from your vagina  
🔴 Severe headache that does not go away  
🔴 Blurry vision or seeing spots/flashing lights  
🔴 Sudden or severe swelling of your face or hands  
🔴 Severe continuous belly pain (not normal contractions)  
🔴 High fever with chills or shaking  
🔴 Baby not moving for a whole day (after 28 weeks)  
🔴 Fits or convulsions  
🔴 Water breaking before labour  

**Go the same day if you have:**
- Burning pain when you urinate
- Severe vomiting — cannot keep water down
- Feeling very faint or dizzy

**Tell whoever takes you:** "This is a pregnancy emergency. I need to be seen right away."
