# UMMA Medication File — Unsafe and Caution Medications in Pregnancy
# Category: Medications | Population: Pregnant women
# Last reviewed: 2025
# NOTE: UMMA never gives doses. Always refer to doctor or pharmacist for all treatment decisions.

---

## OVERVIEW

This file covers medications that are unsafe or require significant caution in pregnancy. For each medication, UMMA must:
- State clearly whether it is unsafe or requires caution
- Explain the risk in plain terms
- Give a safe alternative where one exists
- Provide a calm, non-panic reassurance framework for women who have already taken the medication

---

## UNSAFE MEDICATIONS IN PREGNANCY

### Ibuprofen and other NSAIDs (Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs)
**Brand names commonly found in Nigeria:** Brufen, Advil, Nurofen, Felvin, Emzor Ibuprofen, Diclofenac (Voltaren, Cataflam), Piroxicam (Feldene), Indomethacin, Naproxen, Aspirin (at anti-inflammatory doses)

**Why it is unsafe:**
- First trimester: some evidence of increased miscarriage risk and cardiac septal defects with NSAID use
- Third trimester (most critical): NSAIDs cause premature closure of the ductus arteriosus — a vital fetal blood vessel — leading to fetal cardiac compromise and pulmonary hypertension; can cause oligohydramnios (reduced amniotic fluid); associated with fetal renal impairment
- At any stage: increases risk of antepartum haemorrhage by impairing platelet function
- Aspirin at anti-inflammatory doses is unsafe — low-dose aspirin (75–150 mg) is used under medical supervision for pre-eclampsia prevention only

**Safe alternative:** Paracetamol for pain and fever

**What UMMA tells the user:**
"Ibuprofen, diclofenac, and similar pain medicines are not safe to take in pregnancy — especially in the last three months. They can affect your baby's heart and kidneys. Use paracetamol (Panadol) instead for pain or fever. If you need something stronger, see a doctor."

**If already taken:**
"Taking ibuprofen once or twice before you knew you were pregnant, or very early on, is unlikely to have caused harm — but please tell your doctor or midwife at your next ANC visit so they can check everything is well. If you are in the last three months of pregnancy and you have been taking ibuprofen regularly, go to the clinic soon so they can check the baby."

### Aspirin at standard (anti-inflammatory / pain) doses
**Brand names commonly found in Nigeria:** Aspirin, Disprin, some combination cold medicines containing aspirin

**Why it is unsafe:**
- At standard doses (300–600 mg): antiplatelet effect increases bleeding risk; associated with miscarriage in first trimester with regular use; third trimester — premature closure of ductus arteriosus risk as with other NSAIDs
- Note: Low-dose aspirin (75–100 mg) is sometimes prescribed by doctors specifically to prevent pre-eclampsia in high-risk women — this is a controlled clinical decision, not OTC use

**Safe alternative:** Paracetamol

**What UMMA tells the user:**
"Standard aspirin tablets are not safe for pain or fever in pregnancy. Use paracetamol instead. If a doctor has prescribed you a small dose of aspirin specifically for your blood pressure, that is different — continue that as prescribed."

---

### Tetracycline antibiotics
**Brand names commonly found in Nigeria:** Tetracycline capsules, Doxycycline (commonly sold at PMVs for malaria prevention and STI treatment)

**Why it is unsafe:**
- Causes permanent yellowing and damage to developing fetal teeth (dental staining) and impairs fetal bone growth
- Avoid from second trimester onward especially — the fetal teeth are forming
- Doxycycline: same class; additionally used for malaria prevention — NOT safe in pregnancy; use SP (IPTp) for malaria prevention instead

**Safe alternative for infections:** Amoxicillin, erythromycin, or azithromycin (as prescribed by a doctor)
**Safe alternative for malaria prevention:** SP (IPTp) at ANC

**What UMMA tells the user:**
"Tetracycline and doxycycline tablets are not safe in pregnancy — they can damage your baby's teeth while they are forming. If you need an antibiotic, go to the clinic and ask for one that is safe in pregnancy. Do not take doxycycline to prevent malaria in pregnancy — the clinic will give you Fansidar instead."

**If already taken:**
"Taking a short course of doxycycline before you knew you were pregnant is unlikely to have caused serious harm, but please tell your doctor at your next ANC visit. If you took it in the second or third trimester, mention it at clinic so they are aware."

---

### Fluoroquinolone antibiotics
**Brand names commonly found in Nigeria:** Ciprofloxacin (Cipro, Ciprotab — very widely sold at PMVs), Ofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Norfloxacin

**Why it is unsafe:**
- Animal studies show fluoroquinolones cause damage to developing cartilage and joint tissue
- Human data are limited but the risk is sufficient to advise avoidance throughout pregnancy
- Ciprofloxacin in particular is extremely widely sold in Kano at PMVs for any infection — women must be redirected to safer alternatives

**Safe alternative:** Amoxicillin, nitrofurantoin (for UTI — avoid near term), erythromycin, or azithromycin as prescribed

**What UMMA tells the user:**
"Ciprofloxacin (Cipro) is not recommended in pregnancy because of concerns about its effect on developing joints and bones. If you have an infection, go to the clinic for an antibiotic that is safe in pregnancy. Do not buy ciprofloxacin from the chemist while pregnant."

**If already taken:**
"A short course of ciprofloxacin is unlikely to have caused definite harm, but you should mention it to your doctor at your next ANC visit. They may want to note it in your file."

---

### Chloramphenicol (systemic — oral or injectable)
**Brand names commonly found in Nigeria:** Chloramphenicol capsules, various generics

**Why it is unsafe:**
- Third trimester and near term: causes "grey baby syndrome" in newborns — cardiovascular collapse, hypothermia, ashen grey colour; potentially fatal
- Avoid at all stages of pregnancy where an alternative is available

**What UMMA tells the user:**
"Chloramphenicol tablets or injections are not safe in pregnancy, especially near your due date. Go to the clinic for a safer antibiotic."

---

### Misoprostol — used outside medical supervision
**Brand names commonly found in Nigeria:** Cytotec (widely available on the black market in Kano)

**Why it is unsafe (outside medical supervision):**
- Misoprostol causes uterine contractions — when used without medical supervision, it causes uterine hyperstimulation, placental abruption, uterine rupture, and fetal distress
- Used medically for: induction of labour, management of PPH, management of miscarriage — always under clinical supervision with monitoring
- Misuse for induced abortion: causes incomplete abortion, haemorrhage, sepsis, and maternal death
- Fetal exposure without abortion (failed attempt): associated with fetal limb defects and Möbius syndrome

**What UMMA tells the user:**
"Cytotec (misoprostol) should only be used under direct medical supervision in a clinic or hospital. Using it at home to bring on labour or end a pregnancy is very dangerous — it can cause severe bleeding, the womb can tear, and both mother and baby can die. If you have taken it or are considering it, please go to the hospital immediately."

**If already taken:**
"If you have taken misoprostol at home, please go to the hospital now — do not wait. Tell the doctors what you took and when. They are there to help you, not to judge you. This is a medical situation that needs urgent attention."

---

### Ergotamine and ergot derivatives
**Brand names commonly found in Nigeria:** Cafergot (ergotamine + caffeine), some traditional preparations containing ergot alkaloids

**Why it is unsafe:**
- Causes sustained uterine contractions and powerful vasoconstriction
- Associated with miscarriage, fetal growth restriction, and uterine rupture
- Some traditional herbal preparations in Kano contain ergot-like alkaloids — warn against all traditional preparations marketed to "cleanse" or "stimulate" the womb

**What UMMA tells the user:**
"Medicines or herbs that cause the womb to tighten are extremely dangerous in pregnancy and must never be taken. If anyone gives you something to 'stimulate' or 'clean' your womb during pregnancy, do not take it."

---

### Warfarin
**Brand names commonly found in Nigeria:** Warfarin, Coumadin (uncommon but used in women with mechanical heart valves or clotting disorders)

**Why it is unsafe:**
- Crosses the placenta; causes warfarin embryopathy (nasal hypoplasia, stippled bone epiphyses) especially in first trimester weeks 6–12
- Causes fetal bleeding including intracranial haemorrhage
- Women requiring anticoagulation in pregnancy should be on low molecular weight heparin (enoxaparin/Clexane) — does not cross the placenta; prescribed by specialist

**What UMMA tells the user:**
"Warfarin tablets are not safe in pregnancy. If you need blood-thinning medicine during pregnancy, the doctor will prescribe a different type that is safe. Please go to your specialist clinic if you are on warfarin and are pregnant."

---

### Oral Retinoids — Isotretinoin (Roaccutane), Acitretin
**Why it is unsafe:**
Extremely teratogenic — causes severe fetal malformations including brain, heart, and ear defects; miscarriage. Contraception is mandatory during use. Rare in Kano context but increasingly used by urban women for acne.

**What UMMA tells the user:**
"Roaccutane (isotretinoin) tablets for acne are extremely harmful to a developing baby. If you are pregnant or trying to get pregnant, stop immediately and go to your doctor. Do not take these tablets at any time during pregnancy."

---

### Methotrexate
**Why it is unsafe:**
Highly teratogenic; causes severe fetal malformations and miscarriage. Used for ectopic pregnancy management (under specialist supervision — this is a deliberate termination of an ectopic pregnancy, not general use), rheumatoid arthritis, and cancer. Women on methotrexate for chronic conditions must use contraception.

---

### Thalidomide
**Why it is unsafe:**
Causes severe limb defects (phocomelia). Not commonly prescribed but occasionally appears in leprosy treatment in Nigeria (rare in Kano).

---

### ACE Inhibitors and ARBs (antihypertensives)
**Brand names commonly found in Nigeria:** Lisinopril, Enalapril (Renitec), Ramipril, Losartan, Valsartan

**Why it is unsafe:**
- Second and third trimester: causes fetal renal failure, oligohydramnios, skull hypoplasia, limb contractures, and neonatal death — "ACE inhibitor fetopathy"
- Women with hypertension on ACE inhibitors or ARBs must switch to a pregnancy-safe antihypertensive before or immediately upon discovering pregnancy

**Safe alternatives for hypertension in pregnancy (as prescribed by doctor):** Methyldopa, nifedipine, labetalol

**What UMMA tells the user:**
"Blood pressure medicines called ACE inhibitors (like lisinopril or enalapril) and ARBs (like losartan) are not safe in pregnancy — they can seriously harm your baby's kidneys and development. If you are taking one of these for blood pressure and you are pregnant, go to your doctor as soon as possible to change to a safer medicine. Do not stop without a replacement — your blood pressure still needs to be controlled."

---

### Oral Hypoglycaemics — Metformin (caution), Sulphonylureas (avoid), Glitazones (avoid)
**Metformin:**
- Used for gestational diabetes and PCOS in pregnancy at some centres — evidence of safety improving but not fully established; some guidelines now permit use under specialist supervision; do not start without specialist advice
- Women on metformin for PCOS who become pregnant: continue until specialist review

**Sulphonylureas (glibenclamide, glipizide):**
- Crosses placenta; causes neonatal hypoglycaemia; avoid in pregnancy where possible; insulin is preferred for gestational diabetes requiring medication

**What UMMA tells the user:**
"If you take tablets for diabetes and you are pregnant, you need to see a doctor or specialist very soon. Some diabetes tablets are not safe in pregnancy and may need to be changed to insulin injections. Do not stop your diabetes medicine without a replacement."

## MEDICATIONS REQUIRING CAUTION IN PREGNANCY

### Codeine and tramadol
**Brand names commonly found in Nigeria:** Codeine linctus (cough syrup), some combination pain medicines, Tramadol capsules (widely available at PMVs)

**Risks:**
- Opioid use near term: neonatal withdrawal syndrome — baby born with tremors, irritability, poor feeding, seizures
- First trimester opioid exposure: some studies suggest small increased risk of certain birth defects — avoid where possible
- Tramadol: widely misused in Kano for pain and recreation; in pregnancy causes similar neonatal withdrawal effects; avoid

**When it might still be used:** Severe pain not controlled by paracetamol, under clinical supervision, for the shortest duration

**What UMMA tells the user:**
"Codeine and tramadol are strong pain medicines that should be avoided in pregnancy if at all possible. If you need them, this must be prescribed by a doctor who knows you are pregnant. Tramadol bought at chemist shops should not be taken in pregnancy."

---

### Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) — Phenobarbitone, Phenytoin, Carbamazepine, Sodium Valproate
**Risks:**
- All AEDs carry some fetal risk — neural tube defects (valproate highest risk), cardiac defects, cleft palate, cognitive effects
- Sodium valproate: highest teratogenic risk; associated with fetal valproate syndrome and significant neurodevelopmental problems; ideally should not be used in women of childbearing age who could become pregnant
- However: uncontrolled epilepsy in pregnancy is also very dangerous — maternal seizures cause fetal hypoxia and maternal injury
- Women with epilepsy must not stop AEDs without specialist review — sudden stopping causes status epilepticus

**What UMMA tells the user:**
"If you take epilepsy medicine and you are pregnant or planning a pregnancy, you need to see a specialist as soon as possible. Do not stop your medicine — stopping it could cause a very dangerous fit. The doctor will discuss the safest medicine for you and your baby."

---

### Nitrofurantoin — UTI treatment
**Risks:**
- Safe in second trimester for UTI
- Avoid in first trimester (theoretical risk) and at term (37+ weeks) — can cause haemolytic anaemia in the newborn if given near delivery
- Safe alternative at term: amoxicillin or cefalexin for UTI if clinically appropriate

---

### Antithyroid drugs — Propylthiouracil (PTU), Carbimazole
**Risks:**
- PTU preferred in first trimester (carbimazole associated with aplasia cutis); switch to carbimazole in second trimester in some guidelines
- Both cross placenta and can cause fetal hypothyroidism and goitre — lowest effective dose and specialist supervision essential
- Untreated hyperthyroidism is more dangerous than treated — do not stop without specialist advice

## WHAT TO DO IF ALREADY TAKEN

### General UMMA framework for unsafe medications already taken:

**Step 1 — Assess timing:**
- When was it taken? Early exposure (before 4 weeks from LMP — before the embryo has implanted) carries very low organ-specific risk
- How much was taken and for how long?

**Step 2 — Calibrate response:**
- Single dose of most medications: risk is usually very low even for medications on the unsafe list
- Prolonged use of clearly teratogenic medications (isotretinoin, warfarin, valproate): requires urgent specialist review

**Step 3 — Always advise ANC disclosure:**
- Every medication exposure should be disclosed at ANC — not to cause alarm but so it is documented and monitored appropriately

**Step 4 — Never cause panic:**
- Most single-dose exposures to common medications do not cause fetal harm
- Panic causes women to consider termination unnecessarily or to avoid ANC out of shame

**Step 5 — Know when to escalate urgently:**
- Misoprostol taken at home: emergency — go to hospital now
- Methotrexate or isotretinoin: urgent specialist review
- ACE inhibitor/ARB in second or third trimester: urgent switch to safe antihypertensive

**Template responses:**

*Woman took ibuprofen for a headache before knowing she was pregnant:*
"Taking ibuprofen once or twice very early in pregnancy, before you knew you were pregnant, is very unlikely to have harmed your baby. Please mention it to your doctor or midwife at your next visit so they have a record. There is no need to panic — just let them know."

*Woman has been taking an unsafe medication regularly:*
"This medicine is one that should be avoided in pregnancy, and it is important that your doctor knows you have been taking it. Please go to your ANC clinic as soon as possible — ideally this week — and tell them exactly what you have been taking and for how long. They may want to check the baby's development and find a safer alternative. Please go — it is better to tell them and let them assess than to worry at home alone."
